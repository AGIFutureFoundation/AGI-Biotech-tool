biodao.blockchain, from the AGI Future Foundation. A molecular research workspace that a scientist can operate by hand in a headset, and that another agent can operate over a protocol, with every result signed, costed, and traceable.

Discovery software assumes a human is driving. A chemist moves between a structure viewer, a docking program, six database websites and a spreadsheet, and nothing records why a decision was made. The thing most likely to do that work next is an agent, and almost no scientific software can be called by one.

So we built one workspace with three ways in. By hand: pick up a protein, drop a compound into its pocket, and pull it about while the physics runs. By voice: load LRRK2, find pockets, dock it. By agent: every action is a typed tool that an MCP client can call.

Your compound library imports from PDF, is profiled for drug-likeness, and is ranked against the target.

Here is what the numbers actually say. Re-docking recovers the crystal pose in two of four cases, with a median of two and a half angstroms. That is good enough to triage a library. It is not good enough to predict affinity. We publish that number, because serious customers run their own controls.

Twenty-seven nanoseconds a day of all-atom dynamics on a consumer GPU. Fourteen public databases, with no account and no key.

Underneath: an x402 payment challenge for metered tools, a signed NANDA agent facts document for discovery, OML-style fingerprints so a result can be verified later, and a hash chain over every run.

The roadmap: scoring that passes its own benchmark, hardware testing on Quest and Vision Pro, settlement and a public registry, then deployment for teams.

We are looking for disease foundations with a target and no computational team, pharma groups who want this behind their firewall, and engineers who want to work on the scoring function. Write to x at agifuturefoundation dot org.
