# pump.science — integration assessment

**Status: COMPLETE.** All research current as of **2026-09-22**; the live-state observations in §3
are same-day and will age quickly.

**Headline: do not integrate with pump.science.** As of today it lists zero compounds and its
experiment endpoints return HTTP 402/500. The scientifically valuable data in this ecosystem belongs
to **Ora Biomedical**, is reachable without pump.science, and is published as a dashboard rather than
an interface. See §6.

Scope note: the token/market dimension of pump.science is described factually only where it is
needed to understand how the organisation works. Nothing here is investment advice, an assessment
of token value, or a recommendation to take any financial action.

Evidence tiering used throughout: **[peer-reviewed] / [preprint] / [platform-published] /
[marketing or blog] / [secondary press] / [unverified]**.

---

## 1. What pump.science is

The starting understanding was: *a DeSci platform running longevity experiments on C. elegans and
Drosophila, testing compounds for lifespan effects, with a token layer attached.*

**That is broadly right but wrong in three ways that matter:**

1. **pump.science runs no assays.** It contracts four CROs, one per stage. It is a funding and
   trading layer with a results dashboard bolted on — not a laboratory.
2. **The pipeline is four stages, not two.** Worm → fly → mouse → human, gated on trading fees.
3. **The token is not an "attached" layer — it is the mechanism.** Trading fees *are* the research
   budget, and they gate whether an experiment happens at all.

### Confirmed from pump.science's own documentation (GitBook, `pumpscience.gitbook.io/pump.science`)

**[platform-published]** — this is the primary source and it is self-published, not peer-reviewed.

pump.science describes itself as "a crypto-powered research protocol to maximize human lifespan,"
combining the Solana blockchain with contracted longevity labs. Anyone may propose a compound or
combination; **each intervention launches as a token, and the token sale is the fundraising
mechanism for the experiment.** Documented mechanic: when a token's market cap reaches 303 SOL,
82 SOL migrates to a DEX for trading and **3 SOL pays for the first worm experiment.**

A four-stage escalation pipeline is documented:

| Stage | Organism | Lab | n per arm | Endpoints | Duration |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | *C. elegans*, wild-type **N2** | **Ora Biomedical** (WormBot) | 60 vs 60 vehicle control | median + max lifespan | up to 8 weeks |
| 2 | *D. melanogaster* | **Tracked Biotechnologies** (FlyBox) | 45 vs 45 control | lifespan, movement speed, distance, climbing latency (negative geotaxis) | ~70 days |
| 3 | Mouse, **C57BL/6**, 6–8 wk | **VivoArchitect** | 20 vs 20 | overall survival; Open Field, Rotarod, Y-Maze, EPM, grip strength; weight, temp, CBC, plasma biomarkers | not stated |
| 4 | Human | not named | ~30/arm, randomised placebo-controlled double-blind | wearable-derived VO2max, RHR, sleep efficiency | 12 weeks |

Dosing regimens are "set in consultation with each CRO and compound submitter."

Worm data is "streamed retrospectively once complete"; fly data is "continuous streaming video +
rolling data uploads." The headline metric surfaced to users is **PLE (percentage life extension)**.
**[platform-published / secondary]**

### Funding mechanics (factual; not an evaluation)

From `the-protocol/crypto-powered-science`: **"Research is funded through LP fees generated by
trading activity."** Split: **70 % of LP fees to the research, 25 % to pump.science as a platform
fee, 5 % to the compound submitter (dev).** Advancement is gated on cumulative trading fees, giving
an escalating cost ladder:

| Stage | Trigger (cumulative trading fees) |
| --- | --- |
| Worms | **$500** |
| Flies | **$1,500** |
| Mice | **$7,000** |
| Humans | **$25,000** |

(An earlier page states the first worm experiment costs **3 SOL** out of a 303 SOL bonding-curve
migration.) Advancement additionally requires that **"the lab agrees to run the experiment."**

**This ladder is useful to us independently of any integration** — it is a current, real-world price
quote for each in-vivo validation stage. For calibration, the pump.science-funded mouse preprint
(below) states that conventional rodent lifespan validation **exceeds $200K USD, takes over 2 years
and needs ~100 animals**, and that their short-term performance assay brought costs "to around $10K
(20-fold less expensive)."

### The IP claim — read this one carefully

pump.science's FAQ states: **"Token holders of compounds on pump science have the right to
participate in the governance of the compound IP and its development."** Platform summaries describe
this as token holders receiving rights to the IP created from the experiments.
**[platform-published]**

Factually, that is the whole of what is published: a governance right over compound IP attaching to
holders of that compound's token. **I found no licence text, no assignment agreement, no legal
entity named as the IP holder, and no description of how such a right would be perfected or
enforced.** The mouse preprint does name a **"Pump.Science Association"** as an author affiliation,
which is the only corporate-form signal I found anywhere.

**Why this is flagged:** this repo has just adopted a proprietary all-rights-reserved licence. An
arrangement in which experimental IP is said to attach to a distributed set of token holders is
structurally in tension with that position, and would need real legal review before any compound,
structure or dataset from this platform were submitted into it. That is a statement of the conflict,
not advice — and the financial side of the token is explicitly out of scope here.

### Compounds and what results actually exist

The two consistently named early launches are **Rifampicin ($RIF)** and **Urolithin A ($URO)**
**[secondary press + a still-indexed pump.science experiment URL for URO]**. The GitBook itself
names only **rapamycin**, and only as a worked example of launching an intervention — it reports no
results. At least five further tokens existed, named in the mouse preprint's funding statement:
**$BOODS, $TROL, $RAPTOR, $BLUPILL, $PNL**. So the catalogue was larger than press coverage
suggests, which makes today's empty catalogue more striking, not less.

**Critically: both already had strong published worm-lifespan results before pump.science tested
them.** Rifampicin extending *C. elegans* lifespan "by up to 60 %" via reduced advanced glycation
end-products and DAF-16 activation is a **2015 peer-reviewed paper (PMC4406675)**. Ora Biomedical's
own bioRxiv preprint *Comprehensive evaluation of lifespan-extending molecules in C. elegans*
(2024.06.24.600458) reports **rifampicin 50 µM at +59 % median** and **urolithin A 50 µM at +35 %
median**. **[peer-reviewed / preprint — independent of pump.science]**

So the headline numbers associated with pump.science's two flagship tokens are, as far as I can
establish, **restatements of pre-existing literature on compounds already known to work in worms** —
not novel discoveries the platform generated. I found **no pump.science-originated preprint,
publication or dataset for any compound.** The GitBook refers to "their preprint" for methodology
detail but does not cite it; I could not locate it.

### The one real scientific output I could find: a pump.science-funded mouse preprint

**bioRxiv 10.64898/2026.02.25.707674v1** — *"Short-Term Performance Assay Identifies Functional
Benefits and Early Toxicity of Longevity Interventions in Mice."* **[preprint, not peer-reviewed]**

- **Authors/affiliations:** seven authors, primarily **VivoArchitect Sàrl** (Switzerland), plus the
  University of Zaragoza and **Pump.Science Association**. Mice were housed at CEBEGA, Universidad
  de Santiago de Compostela.
- **Funding, verbatim:** *"This study was funded through fees allocated to aging research by token
  communities within Pump.Science (**$BOODS, $TROL, $RAPTOR, $BLUPILL, and $PNL**), as well as by
  VivoArchitect Sàrl."* This proves **more than two compounds have been tokenized**, and that the
  **mouse stage has actually been executed** — not roadmap.
- **Competing interests are declared**, including that one author "is the director and a shareholder
  of Pump.Science Association" and another is affiliated with the for-profit CRO. Good practice.
- **Design:** 7-month-old **male C57BL/6J**, one month acclimation, 8-week baseline then 8 weeks of
  chronic treatment. **n = 5 per group** across 6 groups (sildenafil dropped to n = 4 after a
  fighting-related euthanasia). Randomly assigned. Compounds: 17α-estradiol, rapamycin + SMER28,
  berberine + resveratrol, sildenafil, pinealon. Doses given in ppm in food, or 30 mg/kg IP.
- **Statistics:** one-way and two-way ANOVA with **Dunnett's multiple-comparison correction**,
  mixed-effects (REML), Kruskal–Wallis with Dunn's. Multiple comparisons are corrected for.
- **Limitations are stated by the authors themselves:** male-only C57BL/6J restricts sex- and
  strain-specific conclusions; the 8-week window may miss slower effects; sample sizes were small.
- **Reported finding of note:** rapamycin + SMER28 "modestly improved strength and sociability but
  induced anemia in **2 of 5 mice**."

**Crucially: this study does not measure lifespan at all.** It is explicitly a short-term functional
pre-screen run "before committing to full mouse lifespan studies." So even the strongest scientific
artefact associated with pump.science is **not lifespan data.**

Two problems I found that the authors do *not* raise:

1. **No blinding anywhere.** The words "blind" / "blinded" do not appear in the full text. Grip
   strength, open field, Y-maze and social-recognition scoring are exactly the assays where
   unblinded measurement inflates effects.
2. **Cage is confounded with treatment.** Mice were "group-housed (5 per cage)" and there were
   n = 5 per group — so each treatment group appears to be a single cage. Cage effects are then
   inseparable from drug effects, and n = 5 individuals is arguably n = 1 experimental unit.
3. **No data availability statement.** No repository, no Zenodo, no accession, no licence. Searched
   for and absent.

### Not established

- Whether the **fly** stage has ever been executed by anyone.
- Any **lifespan** result that pump.science originated and published.
- Whether any compound pump.science tested produced a result not already in the literature.
- Legal entity details beyond the name "Pump.Science Association", and current operating status.

## 2. The partners — and a correction to the premise

### They are not molecular vendors. They are CROs.

**State this plainly: none of these organisations supplies molecules.** Every one of them is a
**contract research organisation that runs assays** and returns data. They do not sell compounds,
they do not hold catalogues, and there is no ordering interface. If the goal is chemical sourcing,
these are the wrong entities entirely — that is Sigma/Enamine/PubChem vendor-record territory and a
completely separate problem.

### How many, and which — the docs disagree with themselves

**There are four partners, one per stage, not three.** But the documentation is internally
inconsistent about the fly partner, so I am recording both readings rather than picking one:

| Stage | Partner | Trigger | Source | Verified real? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Worm (*C. elegans*) | **Ora Biomedical** (WormBot-AI) | $500 / 3 SOL | both readings agree | **Yes** |
| Fly (*D. melanogaster*) | **Tracked Biotechnologies** (FlyBox) *or* **Juvion Health Sciences** (FlyGym) | $1,500 | pages disagree | **Juvion: yes. Tracked: not independently confirmed** |
| Mouse | **VivoArchitect** | $7,000 | both readings agree | **Yes** |
| Human (wearables) | **Reputable Health** | $25,000 | coordinator's fetch | **Yes** |

On the fly discrepancy: I loaded `the-protocol/scientific-experiment-protocol` **directly in a
browser** and it names **"Tracked Biotechnologies"** for Stage 2 and does not mention Reputable
Health for Stage 4. That page is stamped **"Last updated: 2025-07-06"** and GitBook renders it as
"Last updated 1 year ago." So **the protocol page is roughly 14 months stale**, and a Juvion /
Reputable Health line-up is most likely the newer one. Either way the docs are not currently
self-consistent, which is itself a finding.

### Independent verification of each

1. **Ora Biomedical** — **real and the strongest of the four.** Worm lifespan screening is its
   stated speciality. Runs the **WormBot-AI** (robotics + AI survival imaging) and the
   **Million Molecule Challenge**, a crowdfunded open-science screen aiming at 1,000,000
   interventions in five years. Has a **peer-reviewed MMC paper** (PubMed 37432607 / PMC10643437)
   and a PMC-indexed company article (PMC11690787). **Publishes an open results database** — see §3,
   where I inspected it. Protocol partially published: triplicate, matched same-WormBot controls,
   10–50 µM. Track record here is genuine. **[peer-reviewed + directly inspected]**
2. **Juvion Health Sciences** — **real.** Operates the **FlyGym** *Drosophila* high-throughput
   platform; markets itself as NAM-aligned / 3Rs-aligned, screening neuromuscular efficacy without
   mammalian testing. Assay panel covers mobility, lifespan, grip strength and humanised disease
   models; clients receive a statistics-and-visualisation package within ~60 days. **No public data
   repository or published protocol found — it is a commercial service.** **[company site]**
3. **Tracked Biotechnologies** — named by pump.science's stale protocol page as running the
   "FlyBox". **I could not independently verify this company** beyond pump.science's own docs and
   press summaries derived from them. Treat as unconfirmed.
4. **VivoArchitect** (VivoArchitect Sàrl, Switzerland) — **real, and the only partner with a
   pump.science-funded publication.** Preclinical CRO specialising in aging/healthspan/lifespan
   in vivo models (rodents, killifish). Authored the 2026 bioRxiv preprint described in §1.
   **[preprint + company site]**
5. **Reputable Health** — **real.** A CRO in the wearable / decentralised-clinical-trial space,
   publishing explainer content on wearables in decentralised trials. **No evidence found that a
   pump.science human study has actually been run.** **[company site]**

**Programmatic accessibility:** only Ora publishes anything at all, and even that is a dashboard
rather than an interface (§3). The other three expose **nothing** machine-readable.

## 3. Data accessibility

This is the section that decides the recommendation.

### Live state of pump.science as of 2026-09-22 (directly observed)

I loaded `https://pump.science` in a browser and inspected its network traffic. **The platform
appears to be substantially non-functional or dormant right now.**

- The compound table renders **"NO COMPOUNDS FOUND."**
- `GET /api/latest-compounds?limit=5` → **200** with body `{"compounds":[]}` — a working endpoint
  reporting **zero compounds**.
- `GET /api/latest-trades?limit=10` → **200** with body `{"trades":[]}` — **zero trades**.
- `GET /api/home-page-view?...` → **HTTP 402 Payment Required**, body
  `{"error":"Failed to fetch live experiments data"}`. Also 402: `/api/home-page-view-counts`.
- **HTTP 500** from `/api/token-tickers`, `/api/getKingOfPill`, `/api/admin-config`,
  `/api/next-scheduled-experiments`, `POST /api/tradeHistoryApi`.
- The only live signal on the site is `/api/recent-activities`, showing two wallet-connect events
  timestamped the same day — i.e. visitors arriving, not experiments running.
- The site is served behind a Vercel security checkpoint; the footer reads "© 2025 Pump Science."

A 402 from a Vercel-hosted backend is characteristically a billing/quota stop rather than an
application bug. Combined with the empty-but-healthy `latest-compounds` endpoint, the reasonable
reading is that **the experiment catalogue is empty and parts of the backend are switched off.**
I could not determine whether this is a temporary outage or a wind-down. **[directly observed —
this is the single most decision-relevant fact in this document.]**

The front page is also padded with several hundred words of invisible SEO keyword stuffing
("longevity investment opportunities … tokenized science experiments … peer-reviewed research
protocols"), which is a marketing artefact, not a scientific one.

### pump.science's published interface

- **No scientific API found.** Searches for a pump.science data API, data endpoint or public
  dataset return nothing. The only pump.science source code that exists publicly is the **Solana
  bonding-curve program**, audited through Code4rena in January 2025
  (`github.com/code-423n4/2025-01-pump-science`) — i.e. the *financial* layer is open source and
  audited; the *scientific* layer is not. **[verified by search; absence of evidence, recorded as
  such]**
- The GitBook FAQ **does not address raw-data access, publication timelines, preprints, peer
  review, or result dissemination at all.** It addresses IP governance ("token holders … have the
  right to participate in the governance of the compound IP") and a safety rule (compounds showing
  a significant lifespan *decrease*, p < .01, do not advance). **[platform-published]**
- What users get is a **web dashboard**: streamed video, charts comparing test vs control, "data
  tables … for those who want to see exact results," and a headline **PLE** figure. That is a
  human-readable UI, not a machine-readable distribution. No DOI, no CSV, no licence.
  **[platform-published]**

### Ora Biomedical (the worm data, which is the part with real scientific value)

- Ora runs an **open-access Million Molecule Challenge database / "Longevity Leaderboard"** at
  `orabiomedical.com/mmcleaderboard/`. The page is a **thin wrapper around an embedded
  third-party dashboard tool** ("live results … displayed through an embedded tool", with an
  "open it in a new tab" link). The wrapper page exposes **no download, no CSV, no API, no DOI**
  and the underlying embed platform is not disclosed on the page. **[verified by fetch]**
- Ora's MMC FAQ states results are "tested in triplicate", with "matched controls run at the same
  time and on the same WormBot", dose 10–50 µM. Sponsors may share their own results freely; Ora
  "reserves the right to pursue individual drugs for further development." No publication policy,
  no data licence, no access terms. **[platform-published]**
- MMC protocol detail found in secondary summary of Ora materials: adult **N2 Bristol**, raised
  egg-to-enrollment at **20 °C** on NGM agar with lab-grown *E. coli*; at late L4, **20–25 animals**
  transferred to NGM agar with compound or matched solvent control; **FUdR** added to block progeny;
  daily imaging by WormBot, analysed by WormBot-AI. (20–25 × triplicate ≈ the "60 worms" pump.science
  quotes.) **[needs direct confirmation from an Ora primary document — marked unverified]**
- The MMC concept has a **peer-reviewed publication** (PubMed 37432607 / PMC10643437) and Ora has a
  PMC-indexed company article (PMC11690787). Neither supplies an API, repository URL or access
  mechanism, and PMC11690787 contains **no protocol detail at all** — no strain, temperature,
  replicate, blinding or statistics information. **[verified by fetch]**

### The MMC database, directly inspected (2026-09-22) — this is the good part

The "open it in a new tab" link on Ora's page resolves to **`https://jaklemur.shinyapps.io/MMC-Database/`**
— an **R Shiny app on a free/personal shinyapps.io account.** It loads and it contains real data.

**Compiled Data tab — 160 single-intervention entries**, one row per compound/dose/sponsor, with
columns: Treatment, Dose, Username, **Median Lifespan, Animals Tested, Replicate Populations,
Median Lifespan (Control), Animals Tested (Control), Replicate Populations (Control), % Change in
Median Lifespan, Logrank p-Value, Temp (C)**. A separate Combinatorial Interventions section exists.
Temperature is a uniform **20 °C** across every row observed. Typical n is 30–113 treated vs 34–159
control across 2–6 replicate populations.

**Intervention Browser tab** renders, per compound and per replicate, a **Kaplan–Meier survival
curve with the control overlaid**, plus median, n, replicates, **LCL-95% / UCL-95% on median
lifespan**, p-value vs control, and compound annotation (molecular formula, pathway, target).

This is a materially better artefact than pump.science's marketing implies, and it is **Ora's, not
pump.science's.** Observations that matter for credibility:

- **Negative and null results are published alongside positive ones.** Rapamycin (sirolimus) 15 µM
  appears twice at **0 % change, p = 0.795 and p = 0.996**; temsirolimus and ridaforalimus at 0 %;
  acarbose 0 %; **resveratrol 50 µM at +5.6 %, p = 0.761**. A platform showing a null for rapamycin —
  the field's totem compound — is not curating for hype.
- **p-values are reported and are frequently non-significant** even for double-digit % changes
  (e.g. carvedilol +24.6 %, p = 0.076; spermidine +13.9 %, p = 0.185). The leaderboard carries an
  explicit "Statistical Significance" column.
- The leaderboard's top entries are dominated by one sponsor ("Rapamycin Longevity Lab"), and
  sponsors are self-chosen usernames, several "Anonymous".

Limitations found by inspection:

- **No download, no CSV, no API, no DOI, no licence.** Zero elements matching "Download" on the page.
  Shiny delivers over a websocket; there is no REST surface to call.
- **Raw per-animal survival data is not exported.** The KM curve is rendered server-side as a plot;
  only the summary row (median, n, replicates, CI, p) is in the DOM.
- **No compound identifiers.** Treatments are free-text names ("Doxycycline HCl", "Rapamycin
  (Sirolimus)", "(-)-Epigallocatechin gallate", "Telmisattan" — note the typo). **No InChIKey, no
  SMILES, no PubChem CID, no ChEMBL ID anywhere.** Every row would need name-based resolution.
- **Duplicate rows are present** in the compiled table (doxycycline, glycine, ezetimibe, several
  rapamycin rows repeat verbatim), so the 160 count overstates distinct experiments.
- Hosting is a **personal shinyapps.io account**, which is not a durable, citable archive.

### Bottom line on accessibility

Nothing crosses the boundary programmatically from **either** party. There is no endpoint, no bulk
file, no identifier scheme, and no licence.

- **From pump.science: nothing at all.** Its catalogue is empty and its data endpoints are
  returning 402/500 today.
- **From Ora's MMC database: real summary data, but only by scraping a Shiny app.** 160 rows of
  median lifespan / n / replicates / % change / logrank p / temperature, plus rendered KM curves.
  No raw survival data, no chemical identifiers, no licence, no stable citation.

## 4. Fit against this platform

Context (from this repo, verified by reading the code):

- `server/db_clients.py` — live clients for PubChem, ChEMBL, PubMed/E-utilities, UniProt, RCSB,
  AlphaFold, ClinicalTrials.gov, Reactome, STRING, Open Targets. Every call is real HTTPS with
  a SQLite cache, per-host throttling, retries and a circuit breaker; failures return
  `{"error": ...}` rather than raising.
- `server/repurposing_engine.py` — every number traces to a record in ChEMBL, Open Targets,
  Reactome, STRING or ClinicalTrials.gov, and every hypothesis carries identifiers to re-check it.
  Scoring is additive, fully published in `SCORING`, emitted with every report, and explicitly
  ordinal — "not a probability, not an effect size." It enforces two boundaries in code: **no dose
  is ever computed** (dose text is retrieved verbatim with its NCT id), and **combinations are
  flagged as questions, never asserted as synergy.**
- `server/longevity_panel.py` — 18 targets in two arms, every identifier re-resolved live on
  2026-09-22 and re-checked by `scripts/verify_longevity_citations.py`, which fails on anything
  that does not resolve. `human_evidence` and `model_organism_evidence` are **separate fields that
  are never allowed to blur** — "a mouse lifespan result is written in the mouse field and nowhere
  else." `failed_claims` records assertions that did not survive checking, each with a PMID and a
  verbatim contradicting quote.

The interesting angle: **every existing source in this platform is molecular or clinical. There is
no in-vivo model-organism lifespan data anywhere in it.** Worm and fly lifespan results would be a
genuinely different axis of evidence, and the longevity track just shipped.

### The fit is real in principle, and the panel proves it

`longevity_panel.py` already carries exactly the kind of statements worm data would speak to, and
carries them as *prose*:

- SIRT1: *"Sirtuin overexpression extends lifespan in yeast, worms and flies, but several of the
  founding results failed to replicate when outcrossed and controlled."*
- AMPK/metformin: *"Metformin extends lifespan in C. elegans and in some mouse strains, but the NIA
  Interventions Testing Program found no lifespan extension with metformin alone in genetically
  heterogeneous mice."*

Those are hand-written summaries with no re-resolvable model-organism identifier behind them —
precisely the weakest link in an otherwise identifier-anchored track. A worm-lifespan source with
median, n, replicates, p and temperature per compound would let `model_organism_evidence` cite
something checkable instead of asserting something remembered.

And the MMC data would *immediately* support the panel's own sceptical posture. Two rows found by
direct inspection:

- **Resveratrol 50 µM → +5.6 % median, p = 0.761.**
- **Rapamycin (sirolimus) 15 µM → 0 % change, p = 0.795 and p = 0.996** (two independent sponsors).

The first corroborates the SIRT1 entry, which already calls resveratrol "the cautionary entry of
the track." The second is a *new* negative the panel does not carry.

## 5. Rigor assessment, against this repo's own bar

The bar here is: every claim traces to a re-resolvable identifier, and a verifier fails on bad
input — strict enough that it recently caught a single-word misquote in its own data. Measured
against that:

### Where pump.science falls clearly short

- **No identifiers of any kind.** Compounds are free-text strings. No InChIKey, SMILES, PubChem CID
  or ChEMBL ID anywhere in pump.science or the MMC database. Nothing is re-resolvable. This alone
  disqualifies direct ingestion into a panel under current rules — there is nothing for
  `verify_*_citations.py` to fail on.
- **No blinding.** Absent from the MMC protocol description and absent from the word list of the
  mouse preprint's full text. Model-organism lifespan and behaviour assays are exactly where
  unblinded scoring inflates effects.
- **Temperature, diet, strain reported thinly.** MMC reports a uniform 20 °C and N2 Bristol with
  FUdR (the latter only via secondary summary), which is decent for worms; the fly and mouse stages
  report far less, and the docs' own methodology section defers to a **preprint it does not cite or
  link.**
- **Stale, self-contradicting documentation** (14-month-old protocol page naming a different fly CRO
  than current materials).
- **Marketing artefacts on the science surface** — several hundred words of SEO keyword stuffing on
  the front page including the phrases "longevity investment opportunities" and "peer-reviewed
  research protocols," when nothing on the platform is peer-reviewed.
- **Compound selection appears results-shaped.** The two flagship tokens, RIF and URO, were
  compounds with strong pre-existing published worm results (rifampicin +60 %, PMC4406675, 2015;
  rifampicin +59 % and urolithin A +35 % in Ora's own 2024 preprint). Launching tokens on compounds
  already known to work in worms is favourable for a token and near-valueless as discovery.
- **A tokenized incentive sits directly on the result.** Advancement is gated on trading fees and
  5 % of fees route to the compound submitter. That is a reason for *more* scrutiny of
  effect-size reporting, not less — and the headline user-facing metric is a single number (PLE)
  with no interval attached.
- **n = 5 per group with cage confounded with treatment**, in the mouse study.

### Where it is better than expected, and this should be said

- **Ora's MMC database publishes nulls and negatives**, including a null for rapamycin — the field's
  totem compound — and reports logrank p-values, 95 % CIs on median lifespan, replicate counts and
  both arms' n. A platform that shows rapamycin at 0 % is not curating for hype.
- **Ora has genuine peer-reviewed standing** for the MMC concept.
- **The mouse preprint corrects for multiple comparisons** (Dunnett's, Dunn's), **declares competing
  interests explicitly**, including the Pump.Science directorship, and **states its own limitations**
  (male-only, 8-week window, small n) rather than hiding them.
- The FAQ's safety rule — compounds showing a significant lifespan *decrease* (p < .01) do not
  advance — is at least a stated, directional stopping rule.

**Net:** Ora Biomedical clears a respectable screening-tier bar. **pump.science does not clear this
repo's bar, and is not the source of the part that does.**

## 6. Recommendation

### On pump.science: **nothing. Do not build an integration.**

The decisive fact is not a judgement call, it is an observation: **as of 2026-09-22 pump.science has
zero compounds and zero trades, and its experiment endpoints return HTTP 402 and 500.** There is no
API to call, no dataset to import, and right now no catalogue to import from. Designing around a
hope that this comes back would be building against an empty backend.

Even if it were fully operational, the case would still be no:

- Everything of scientific value that flows through pump.science is **generated by Ora and
  VivoArchitect**, both of whom are reachable directly without the token layer in between.
- pump.science adds **a funding mechanism, not a data source.** Nothing crosses the boundary that
  does not originate with a CRO.
- The **IP-governance claim** (§1) is an unresolved legal tension with this repo's
  all-rights-reserved licence, and that is not something to take on for a data feed that doesn't
  exist.

If the only real way to participate turns out to be buying a compound token, **then this is a
financial decision, not a technical one, and it is out of scope for this assessment.** It belongs
with the user and their own advisors, and nothing in this document should be read as encouragement
either way.

### On Ora Biomedical's MMC database: **a one-off, clearly-labelled dataset import is defensible —
but not a `db_clients.py` client, and not into the panels.**

**Not a client.** `db_clients.py`'s contract is live HTTPS against a documented, stable, versioned
public endpoint with published rate limits. The MMC database is an **R Shiny app on a personal
shinyapps.io account** with no REST surface, no download, no licence and no DOI. Writing a client
for it would mean scraping a websocket-driven dashboard — fragile, unversioned, of uncertain
legality under an unstated licence, and stylistically dishonest next to ten real database clients.
**It does not belong in that file.**

**A one-off import, if anything.** ~160 rows (fewer once the verbatim duplicates are removed) is
small enough to capture once into `data/` as a static, dated, provenance-stamped snapshot. But three
conditions should gate even that:

1. **Ask Ora first.** There is no licence on the data. A short email asking for a CSV and permission
   is cheap, is the correct thing to do, and might well produce a better artefact than any scrape —
   including the raw survival data that the dashboard does not expose.
2. **Resolve every compound name to an identifier on ingest, and drop what will not resolve.** Free
   text like "Telmisattan" (sic) must not enter this repo. PubChem name→CID already exists in
   `db_clients.py`; anything unresolvable goes to a review list, exactly as the compound extractor
   already does for bad SMILES.
3. **It goes in as `model_organism_evidence` with a screening-tier label and never touches
   `human_evidence`.** The panel's separation rule is the whole point. Unblinded, single-lab,
   dashboard-sourced worm screens are hypothesis-generating; they are not evidence of a human
   effect, and a verifier cannot re-resolve them.

**My actual recommendation, ranked:**

1. **Do nothing with pump.science.** Revisit only if the platform is operational *and* publishes a
   licensed, identifier-bearing dataset.
2. **Cheapest high-value action: email Ora Biomedical** asking about licensed access to MMC data,
   ideally raw survival curves with chemical identifiers. If they say yes, the calculus changes
   completely and a proper import becomes worth doing.
3. **Regardless of any integration, take the cost ladder** ($500 / $1,500 / $7,000 / $25,000,
   against ~$200K and 2+ years for a conventional mouse lifespan study) into the platform's
   documentation. It puts real current numbers on in-vivo validation and costs nothing to record.
4. **Optionally, add two negatives to `longevity_panel.py` by hand** — MMC's rapamycin 0 % / p ≈ 0.8
   and resveratrol +5.6 % / p = 0.761 — *only if* they can be given a re-resolvable citation. On
   present sourcing they cannot, so on this repo's own rules: **leave them out.**

## 7. What I could not verify

Explicitly unconfirmed. None of these should be repeated as fact.

1. **Whether pump.science is temporarily broken or winding down.** I observed 402/500 responses and
   an empty catalogue on one day. I could not establish the cause or whether it is permanent.
2. **The current, authoritative partner list.** The docs are self-contradictory: the protocol page
   (stale, 2025-07-06) says **Tracked Biotechnologies**; other current material says **Juvion Health
   Sciences** and adds **Reputable Health**. I could not determine which is live today.
3. **Tracked Biotechnologies as a company.** Not independently verifiable outside pump.science's own
   docs and press derived from them.
4. **The "preprint" the protocol page defers to for methodology.** Referenced, never cited or
   linked. Not located. The 2026 VivoArchitect mouse preprint may or may not be it — it postdates
   the page by ~7 months and covers mice, not the worm/fly methodology the page was deferring on.
5. **Whether the fly stage has ever been run**, by Tracked, Juvion, or anyone.
6. **Whether any human study has been run** with Reputable Health.
7. **Any pump.science-originated lifespan result.** The one preprint I found measures short-term
   function, not lifespan.
8. **The MMC worm protocol details** — N2 Bristol, 20 °C rearing, FUdR, 20–25 animals per plate —
   came from a **secondary summary of Ora materials, not an Ora primary document.** The 20 °C figure
   *is* independently confirmed as a column in the database itself; the rest is not.
9. **Blinding.** I can say the word does not appear in the mouse preprint's full text and is absent
   from Ora's published protocol description. I cannot say blinding was definitively not performed.
10. **How the IP-governance right actually operates.** No licence, no assignment text, no named
    holding entity beyond "Pump.Science Association," no enforcement mechanism found.
11. **pump.science's legal entity, jurisdiction, ownership and financials.** Not established.
12. **Whether MMC data may lawfully be scraped or redistributed.** No licence is published. This is
    an open question, not a permission.
13. **Total MMC scale.** 160 single-intervention rows were visible, with verbatim duplicates among
    them; I did not enumerate the combinatorial section or establish a distinct-compound count.
