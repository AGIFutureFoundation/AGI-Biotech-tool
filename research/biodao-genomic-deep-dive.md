# Who Owns the Science? BioDAOs, Genomic Data-Sharing, and What a Real BioDAO Would Need

## Decentralised science, the IP-token model, and the consent and licensing rules that decide whether genomic sharing works

*An honest status report from the AGI Future Foundation bio research programme*

*Illustration note: no photograph is used in this article. Any illustration added later must be labelled as a generated illustration, not a photograph or real data.*

Science has a funding problem and an ownership problem, and they are the same problem seen from two sides. Most early-stage biomedical research is too uncertain for commercial funders and too slow for the grant cycle; and when it produces something valuable — a molecule, a dataset, a patent — who owns it, who can use it, and who benefits is usually settled behind closed doors, long after the work is done. Decentralised science, or DeSci, tries to settle those questions in the open, at the start, using tokens for governance, non-fungible tokens for intellectual property, and smart contracts for the rules. Genomic data-sharing is the parallel attempt from inside the established system: the world's largest biobanks trying to share the most personal data there is — a person's genome — without betraying the people who gave it.

This article covers both: [Molecule](https://www.molecule.xyz/) and its IP-NFT model, [Bio Protocol](https://www.bio.xyz/) and its BioDAOs, [VitaDAO](https://www.vitadao.com/) and [AthenaDAO](https://athenadao.co/) — now AthenaBIO — and the smaller DAOs in their network; the genomic standards of [GA4GH](https://www.ga4gh.org/) and the access terms of the [All of Us Research Program](https://allofus.nih.gov/) and [UK Biobank](https://www.ukbiobank.ac.uk/); the open cancer resources built on [TCGA](https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov/) and [cBioPortal](https://www.cbioportal.org/); and the compound tooling — [RDKit](https://www.rdkit.org/), [ChEMBL](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/chembl/), [PubChem](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/) — on which the AGI Compounds library would be built. Every figure carries a source and an as-of date. Where a figure could not be verified, that is stated, not filled in.

## Molecule and the IP-NFT: putting legal rights on-chain

[Molecule](https://www.molecule.xyz/) is the infrastructure layer of decentralised science. Its core innovation is simple to state and hard to execute: attach legal intellectual-property rights to a non-fungible token — an **IP-NFT** — so a research asset can be co-owned and governed by a community rather than held by a single institution. Molecule is the tokenisation engine behind the Bio Protocol and BioDAO ecosystem, and its founder and chief executive, Paul Kohlhaas, also launched [Pump.science](https://pump.science/), a Solana launchpad funding longevity compound trials through freely tradeable tokens. The distinction most often blurred in DeSci coverage is between the token and the rights: an IP-NFT represents legal IP rights, which are owned, licensed, and governed — not public-domain data — and holding a token is not the same as owning the underlying science outright.

The IP-NFT stack has already turned over once. Molecule's first-generation IP-NFT contracts are now legacy: the contract index marks the Ethereum and Sepolia IP-NFT sections **"UNMAINTAINED"** and lists the live deployment as **"Molecule Labs core (v0.1.0)" on Base**. The current primitive, **V3 — the Lab**, is a different shape of thing: a Lab is "an NFT (ERC-721) that is permanently bound to its own smart contract wallet (ERC-6551)" with account abstraction (ERC-4337), installable modules (ERC-7579), and an attestation registry (ERC-7484). In plain terms, the research project becomes an account: it holds its own treasury, data anchor, and licences, and transferring the Lab NFT hands over the whole thing — a more capable container than a bare IP-NFT, and a newer, less proven one.

V3 traction, as reported on 12 October 2026 from on-chain data, is early and thin. The LabNFT contract reported **151 Labs**, the first minted on 8 July 2026. The OclTokenizer had issued **five IP Tokens**, the first on 18 September 2026 and none since 6 October 2026. The IP-NFT contracts reached version 2 in 2024, and in 2025 Molecule extended toward bridging tokenised IP into the real-world-asset market. Two cautions attach to the V3 figures: they come from a third-party wiki, [Caper](https://caper.network/wiki/desci/ecosystem/molecule), reading on-chain data, not from Molecule's own site, so the 151-Lab and 5-IPT figures should be verified against the Base contracts before being quoted publicly; and whether any Lab has yet produced a licensed or commercialised asset could not be verified at all.

What is open and what is gated follows the same split. Open: the Molecule IP-NFT version-2 contracts; the BioDAO token, vesting, and airdrop contracts (open-source Foundry projects under [bio-xyz](https://github.com/bio-xyz)); and the Bio Protocol documentation. Gated: the version-2 launchpad contracts, closed source, with six published audit reports as the only technical description; and the IP itself, which an IP-NFT or a Lab represents but does not make public. An IP Token is not a licence to use the IP commercially; commercialisation terms sit with the IP owner or the Lab.

## Bio Protocol and the IP-Token

[Bio Protocol](https://www.bio.xyz/) — bio.xyz, with the BIO token — is the meta-protocol incubated by Molecule and stewarded by the Bio.xyz Association. Communities form **BioDAOs**, one therapeutic area each, tokenise IP as **IP-Tokens (IPTs)**, and more recently launch AI research agents (**BioAgents**) with their own tokens. BIO is deployed on Ethereum, Base, BNB Chain, and Solana. The documentation is explicit on the point that matters most: an IPT carries **governance and information rights but no guaranteed financial return**, and whether IPT holders are paid on commercialisation is left to the IP owner. Do not present IPTs as equity, revenue shares, or investment returns.

Bio Protocol's second version replaced the earlier curation-and-auction launchpad model with a fixed-price **Ignition Sale** plus post-launch liquidity mechanisms. The launchpad contracts are not public. The BIO token's initial supply was **3,320,000,000**, described as uncapped — issuing more would require a new token contract — and the economy runs on four tokens: BIO, vBIO, veBIO, and BioXP. Governance is still transitioning from BIO and vBIO voting to veBIO voting. The documentation listed **eight BioDAOs** in September 2026 — VitaDAO, HairDAO, CerebrumDAO, ValleyDAO, AthenaDAO, CryoDAO, PsyDAO, Curetopia, "and others." A secondary figure sometimes quoted, "roughly $15 million into active research pipelines" with a "$315 million DeSci market, April 2026" (DAO Times, July 2026), was not verified against Bio Protocol's own reporting and is not quoted as fact. There is no repository actually named "BioDAO" in the Molecule organisation; BioDAOs run on shared Molecule and BIO contracts and generally have no code repositories of their own.

The shared contracts are real and checkable: in [moleculeprotocol](https://github.com/moleculeprotocol) (27 public repositories), [IPNFT](https://github.com/moleculeprotocol/IPNFT) holds the IP-NFT contracts (MIT, 46 stars, 14 October 2026) and [docs](https://github.com/moleculeprotocol/docs) holds the documentation (5 November 2026); the [biodao-bible](https://github.com/moleculeprotocol/biodao-bible) docs site is stale (29 April 2023). In [bio-xyz](https://github.com/bio-xyz) (23 repositories), [BioAgents](https://github.com/bio-xyz/BioAgents) is **archived** (185 stars, 7 November 2026), and vesting contracts and token lists are at [bio-xyz/vesting-contracts](https://github.com/bio-xyz/vesting-contracts) and [bio-xyz/tokenLists](https://github.com/bio-xyz/tokenLists).

## VitaDAO: the first BioDAO, and a governance slowdown

[VitaDAO](https://www.vitadao.com/) is the longest-running BioDAO and the template the others copied: a community-owned collective funding early-stage longevity and ageing research and holding the resulting IP on-chain as IP-NFTs, governed by VITA token holders. It launched in June 2021 from a white paper by Tyler Golato and Paul Kohlhaas of Molecule, and is now a BioDAO inside the Bio Protocol ecosystem. Its model, IP-NFT approach, Snapshot proposal history, and funded-project list are all public. Its first IP token, VITA-FAST, from the Korolchuk Lab at Newcastle University, was created by a vote of VITA holders.

The scale, as reported in 2026, is modest: **31 projects funded**, reported as "30-plus" in February 2026, with **about $4.7 million deployed** (Caper and Onchain Atlas, verified 26 July 2026) — 3 target-discovery, 18 drug-discovery, 3 preclinical, and 3 clinical drug-development projects, with 8 IP tokens and 3 companies founded. An older source says 24 projects and $4.2 million (November 2024); this article uses the 2026 figures. VITA is an ERC-20 on Ethereum, with mirrors on Solana and Base, total supply **64,298,880 tokens**. Its price on 7 November 2026 was roughly $0.163 to $0.164, market capitalisation roughly $3.9 million to $4.4 million (CoinPaprika, TokenRadar) — volatile market data, reported not endorsed. A $4.1 million strategic raise in January 2023 included Pfizer Ventures, Shine Capital, L1 Digital, BeakerDAO, and Spaceship DAO.

The governance record is where VitaDAO's story becomes a caution. The vote.vitadao.eth Snapshot space carried **129 proposals** against a **1,150,000 VITA quorum**, and recorded **no new proposal since 9 September 2025** (Caper dossier, updated October 2026). The most recent binding vote cited was VDP-163, funding FOXO3 oligos, which closed on 16 September 2025 with 17 voters and 6,618,900 VITA cast, all in favour. The January 2025 BIO-airdrop vote drew 37 voters. The public site's "Launch app" destination, app.vitadao.com, is, as of 18 August 2026, a subscription longevity-AI product whose deployed JavaScript bundle contains no occurrence of "governance," "proposal," "vote," or any Snapshot host — governance did not move there. Whether the inactivity since September 2025 reflects a move to another venue or a genuine pause could not be established; do not present VitaDAO governance as currently active without checking Snapshot or Bio Protocol first. VitaDAO holds a **21 million BIO allocation** from Bio Protocol's bio/acc rewards programme; VDP-161 put 6.9 million BIO back to the community as an airdrop (80 percent to VITA holders, 20 percent to IP-token holders) and kept 14.1 million BIO in treasury. A "$55.8 million treasury" cited from a VitaDAO newsletter of March 2026, including "$38 million of IP assets and IP-NFTs valued," is a self-reported valuation of illiquid IP, not cash, next to a roughly $4 million VITA market capitalisation in November 2026 — do not quote it without a fresh primary source. VitaDAO also funded one supplement, VD001, a high-dose spermidine supplement said to be approved by Thailand's FDA (November 2024, updated August 2026); that claim was not independently verified, and no longevity treatment or cure is claimed here. The IP is DAO-owned and licensed, not open data; voting requires holding VITA, and proposal creation is restricted to an allowlist of thirteen addresses. Contracts are at [VitaDAO/VitaDAO-contracts](https://github.com/VitaDAO/VitaDAO-contracts) (MIT-style, 16 stars, 23 October 2026).

## AthenaDAO, now AthenaBIO — and the rest of the network

[AthenaDAO](https://athenadao.co/) was founded in **2022** by Laura Minquini as a DeSci organisation sourcing, funding, and incubating women's-health research — ovarian ageing, reproductive longevity, diagnostics. It is a Bio Protocol BioDAO, with the token **ATH**, a capped ERC-20 with a maximum supply of 100 million and roughly 30 million circulating (February 2026 table). On **8 March 2026** it published "AthenaBIO: The first Science-First Ecosystem for Women's Health R&D," introducing itself as **AthenaBIO (formerly AthenaDAO)**. The rename had not reached the public site as of 25 October 2026 — athenadao.co still said "AthenaDAO." The shift began in **September 2025**, when AthenaDAO launched **AthenaBIO as an equity vehicle** — a holding company with special-purpose vehicles, not a closed-end fund — to carry vetted assets through validation, regulation, and scale, aiming to raise **roughly $5 million against roughly 15 early-stage assets** in AI diagnostics, ovarian health, and reproductive technology.

Funding to date: the Web3 community deployed **roughly $1.5 million**, funding **6 labs, 2 start-ups, 10 fellowships, and more than 100 live events** (BeautyMatter, 8 January 2026). Named bets include ovarian-ageing targets with AI drug-discovery firm Gero, the integrated stress response pathway, and the cGAS–STING pathway. Governance shows the same pause as VitaDAO: **18 proposals between October 2023 and September 2025, none since September 2025** (October 2026 check). A thesis of 15 September 2026 for a new translational organisation on ovary biology names no token, no vote, and no funding round. The community, fellowships, and events are open; the funded IP is DAO-governed and licensed, not open data. AthenaBIO holds its assets through special-purpose vehicles — conventionally gated, not on-chain open.

**No official public repository was found for AthenaDAO.** A GitHub search returns one result only: `DeSciWorldDAO/dashboard-AthenaDAO`, a third-party dashboard with 0 stars, updated 27 January 2023. That pattern repeats: **HairDAO** (hair-loss, HAIR) and **ValleyDAO** (synthetic biology and climate, GROW) have no official organisation repository — only third-party dashboards. **CryoDAO** (cryopreservation, CRYO) returned no repositories at all. **PsyDAO** and **CerebrumDAO** (neurodegeneration, NEURON) are listed as BIO-incubated and active in Bio Protocol documentation from September 2026, but no primary 2026 activity was verified for them beyond that listing. Secondary treasury and raise figures for these DAOs were not verified and are not quoted — including an unverified "$48 million treasury" claimed for HairDAO (DAO Times, July 2026).

**Curetopia** (rare diseases, $CURES on Solana, maximum supply 100,000,000) is the most instructive case. The first BioDAO to run a community auction on Solana, led by Dr. Ethan Perlstein, it tokenises funded research as IP-NFTs through Molecule and identified a potential treatment for **AARS2**, a fatal rare disease with no approved therapy, moving to file a provisional patent and tokenise it. But as of September 2026, curetopia.xyz linked no voting venue; Snapshot has no Curetopia space, and the Solana Realms registry (314 realms) has no entry. Its executive body of record is a Swiss *Verein*. The science, site, and token are current; on-chain governance is not evidenced. A "Quantum Biology DAO" named in an undated social-media list was not verified as a live BioDAO and is treated as unconfirmed.

The pattern across the cohort is the finding. The science vehicles continue — AthenaBIO's equity vehicle, Curetopia's patent filing, VitaDAO's funded projects — while on-chain governance thins: VitaDAO with no Snapshot proposal since 9 September 2025, AthenaDAO with none since September 2025, Curetopia with no findable voting venue. A proposed BioDAO that copies only the token and the IP-NFT, without a governance design that stays live, would copy the form and miss the lesson.

## Genomic data-sharing: GA4GH and the federated turn

The Global Alliance for Genomics and Health — [GA4GH](https://www.ga4gh.org/) — is the standards body for the other half of this article. Formed in **2013**, it unites **more than 500** leading institutions and develops technical standards, policy frameworks, and open-source tools for voluntary, secure genomic data sharing. It holds no genomic data itself; it writes the rules by which data moves. Three standards matter for any Genomic DAO proposal. The **Data Use Ontology (DUO)** is a hierarchical vocabulary of human- and machine-readable data-use terms, implemented by the Broad and Sanger Institutes and used in annotating **more than 200,000 datasets worldwide** (Cell Genomics, November 2021, still cited in 2026). The **GA4GH Passport** is the standard for data authorisation and access. A data-sharing lexicon — including "data visiting" and "federated data analysis" — was developed by a study group formed in September 2023 and **finalised in July 2025** (Human Genomics, 2025, volume 19, article 80).

"Data visiting" is the direction of travel, and the opposite of open download: shared data is analysed inside the provider's computing environment rather than downloaded. It is the model UK Biobank and All of Us both now use, and the one any honest Genomic DAO would have to copy. GA4GH tooling is on GitHub: [ga4gh/vrs-python](https://github.com/ga4gh/vrs-python) (Apache-2.0, 66 stars, 5 November 2026) and [ga4gh/phenopacket-schema](https://github.com/ga4gh/phenopacket-schema) (103 stars, 6 November 2026). Older repositories — [ga4gh/ga4gh-schemas](https://github.com/ga4gh/ga4gh-schemas), [ga4gh/ga4gh-server](https://github.com/ga4gh/ga4gh-server), and the Beacon specification — are retired and archived and should not be used as current references.

## All of Us and UK Biobank: controlled access at national scale

The NIH **All of Us Research Program** aims to gather data from **1 million or more people** in the United States, prioritising historically underrepresented populations. As of 14 May 2026, it had **880,000 or more participants** from all 50 states, **22,580 or more registered researchers** from **1,360 or more organisations**, **1,350 or more publications** (more than 500 in 2025), **14.7 million biosample aliquots**, and **499,000 or more electronic health records**, with DNA results returned to **733,000 or more individuals**. As of December 2025, **872,000 or more had consented and 613,000 or more were providing biospecimens**. Whole-genome sequencing data had been released for **more than 245,000 participants** (2025 review, cited by Capgemini 2026). An October 2026 Exeter study analysed **nearly 1 million whole genomes** from UK Biobank and All of Us combined (Genome Medicine, 22 October 2026). Access is through the **Researcher Workbench**, a cloud environment; aggregate data is publicly searchable through the Data Browser. There is **no access fee, but researchers bear their own computational costs** (November 2025). Individual-level genomic data is controlled access, not open download.

**UK Biobank** is the biomedical database of **roughly 500,000 volunteers** in the United Kingdom, with genotyping, imaging, electronic-health-record linkage, and whole-genome sequencing of **490,640 participants** (Nature, 2025). Data is available to eligible researchers worldwide for health-related research in the public interest. Its access terms were verified by a Firecrawl scrape of the official access page on 7 November 2026. Eligibility requires a track record of legitimate health-related research, affiliation with a recognised research organisation, and operation from a country complying with international regulations and not subject to UK, US, or EU sanctions, with background checks on every applicant. Proposals raising concerns go to an expert **Access Committee**, consulting the Ethics Advisory Committee and the Participant Advisory Group. **Since January 2025, applications from insurance companies for direct access to de-identified data are no longer approved.** Researchers must sign a Material Transfer Agreement; they are **prohibited from trying to re-identify participants and from sharing data with non-collaborators**. Since **2024, access is by default through the UK Biobank Research Analysis Platform (UKB-RAP)**, a restricted cloud platform — downloads are no longer the default — and researchers agree to delete data when the project ends. Tiered fees apply; the exact schedule was not verified. Summary findings are public; individual-level data are **controlled access, not open** — the model to copy.

The cautionary tale is **openSNP, which is closed**. Founded in 2011 by Bastian Greshake, openSNP let users upload consumer genotype files — from 23andMe, Ancestry, FamilyTreeDNA, and deCODEme — under **CC0**, public domain, with code on GitHub under MIT. Roughly 13,000 people shared data, and the site warned users there was zero privacy or anonymity. It was **dissolved and shut down**: announced 31 March 2025, with **all user data deleted on 10 April 2025**, after the **23andMe Chapter 11 bankruptcy of 23 March 2025** and public fears of DNA misuse. The founder judged that "the risk/benefit calculus of providing free & open access to individual genetic data in 2025 is very different compared to 14 years ago." **Do not cite openSNP as a live open-genomics resource.** Its archived data still appears in research — for example, a May 2026 arXiv benchmarking paper across 80 openSNP phenotypes. The lesson for a proposed Genomic DAO (S27, proposed) is direct: fully open, CC0 individual-level genomic data is now regarded by its own stewards as unsafe to hold. Any proposed model should be controlled, federated, and data-visiting, with consent and re-identification protections — not open download. No live resource called "Open Genes" was verified as an open genomics dataset; a longevity-genetics database sometimes called Open Genes exists, but its status, licence, and contents were not verified, and it is not cited here.

## Cancer genomics: the deepest open data, with the counts moving

The Cancer Genome Atlas — **TCGA** — is the large-scale effort organised by NCI and the National Human Genome Research Institute, launched in **2006** and completed in **2018**, to characterise cancer at the molecular level. Its scale is **roughly 2.5 petabytes** of genomic, epigenomic, transcriptomic, and proteomic data plus clinical information from **more than 11,000 patients representing 33 cancer types** (NCI bioinformatics training page, November 2026). Access is tiered: **not all TCGA data is open access.** Open-access data can be downloaded freely; data unique to an individual — germline variant data, primary sequence .bam files, clinical free-text, exon array files — are **controlled access** requiring certification through NCBI **dbGaP Authorized Access**. The primary hub is the **NCI Genomic Data Commons (GDC)**, with data from **22 programmes (including TCGA), 83 projects, 69 primary sites, and 44,637 cases** (NCI training page, November 2026). GDC access and downloads are **free, with no login required** except for controlled data, which requires dbGaP authorisation and an eRA Commons login. The original TCGA Data Portal is listed as **closed in 2026** (re3data, 8 September 2026), succeeded by the GDC. In NCI's Childhood Cancer Data Initiative download table for January 2026, TCGA-BRCA was the most-downloaded dataset at 755.95 terabytes.

**cBioPortal** ([cbioportal.org](https://www.cbioportal.org/)) is the open platform for exploratory, interactive visualisation, analysis, and download of cancer genomics data, from Memorial Sloan Kettering, Dana-Farber, and others, with NCI funding an instance. Users can view genomic alterations across patients and cancer types, run survival analysis and group comparisons, and explore by gene, mutation, or cancer type. It is best for exploration; for raw mRNA expression or full segmented copy-number files, use the GDC. It does not hold controlled raw sequence data. NCI also runs a Childhood Cancer Data Initiative instance at [cbioportal.ccdi.cancer.gov](https://cbioportal.ccdi.cancer.gov/). The number of studies hosted was **not pinned to a single verified figure** and is not quoted; the CCDI reports **40 multimodal paediatric and adolescent/young-adult studies** currently available (March 2026). Code is at [cBioPortal/cbioportal](https://github.com/cBioPortal/cbioportal) (Apache-2.0, 1,064 stars, 6 November 2026).

Two further resources anchor the cancer landscape. NCI's **SEER programme** (Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results) has been funded continuously since **1973**, with **17 core registries and 11 research-support registries across 22 geographic areas**, covering **roughly 45.9 percent of the US population**. Standard research files are **free to any user with a valid email address**; the linked SEER-Medicare product is not a public-use file and carries direct cost-recovery fees. **ClinicalTrials.gov**, run by the US National Library of Medicine, is free to search, and its aggregate database **AACT** is publicly available. Its counts differ by date and definition: a 2026 EACL paper gives **543,172 "all studies"**; Wikipedia gives **more than 444,000 trials from 221 countries**; a 2025 paper gives more than 345,000. Use "over 500,000 registrations (2026)" with the EACL source, or "over 444,000 trials from 221 countries" with Wikipedia — do not mix them. Many trials remain unregistered, so the registry is not a census.

## The compound stack — and the one trap in it

A compound library is only as good as its chemistry data, and the open stack for that is clean, with one trap. **ChEMBL**, from EMBL-EBI, is the standard curated bioactivity database. Version **ChEMBL 37, prepared 1 May 2026**, holds **2,921,148 compounds, 24,527,044 activities, 1,970,438 assays, and 101,100 documents**, under **CC BY-SA 3.0**, with ChEMBL IDs and the release number to be preserved on reuse; free through a REST API and bulk FTP download. **PubChem**, from the US National Center for Biotechnology Information, is the world's largest free general chemistry database: **roughly 124 million compounds and 349 million substances** (homepage, October 2026), a US federal public-domain resource, though reuse rights follow each depositor. The tooling is open too: **RDKit** ([github.com/rdkit/rdkit](https://github.com/rdkit/rdkit)), the standard open-source cheminformatics library (BSD 3-Clause, 3,672 stars, 7 November 2026), with the official ChEMBL Python client (Apache-2.0, 453 stars) and PubChemPy (MIT, 518 stars). **DrugBank is the trap**: version 5.1.22, released 27 June 2026, is under **CC BY-NC 4.0**, academic downloads are **temporarily paused**, and commercial use requires a commercial licence. None is a treatment resource: the ALS agent's "not a medical device" line must stay attached to any compound output.

**AGI Compounds** is the ALS Researcher agent's compound library — the concrete compound product in this programme. Its six Virtuals ACP jobs, verified live on 6 November 2026, are compound_library_snapshot ($10/24h), compound_research_report ($25/48h), compound_data_access ($50/24h), compound_lookup ($2/30min), library_growth_report ($15/48h), and compound_screening_pack ($150/72h), plus a library_weekly subscription ($25/30 days). The token is $ALS on Base, market capitalisation $3,600, one holder, not graduated; the agent is offline, with 0 jobs completed and $0 earned. The library exists only as those job listings, the agent site, and three staged articles — no compound list, molecule file, or screening data was found on disk. A real library would be built on ChEMBL and PubChem with source, version, and date cited for every record, as ChEMBL requires. The Foundation's molecular workspace, [AGI-Biotech-tool](https://github.com/AGIFutureFoundation/AGI-Biotech-tool) ("biodao.blockchain"), is a VR and browser workspace for docking compounds and scoring fit, wired to live public databases and the AGI compound collection, focused on neurogenetic targets including ALS and Parkinson's — an app with real code and documentation, last active 12 October 2026.

The ALS and cancer research the library would serve is genuinely open and genuinely investigational. **Answer ALS** released its full dataset on 3 November 2025: **more than 1,100 participants**, integrated with ALS TDI's ALS Research Collaborative study — **more than 150 terabytes spanning more than 2,500 ALS patient profiles**, with whole-genome, RNA-seq, proteomic, and epigenomic data. Since Neuromine launched in 2021, **500 or more independent ALS research projects** have been initiated (520 or more by March 2026). **Project MinE**'s Data Browser is open access (version 1.7, 14 October 2026), with the largest ALS exome dataset to date: **13,138 ALS patients and 69,775 controls**. **ALS TDI**, a registered 501(c)(3), runs the ARC study with **more than 1,800 participants across 50 countries**; its most advanced candidate, TDI-1831, advanced into IND-enabling studies in 2025, and its chief executive wrote: "I cannot promise you which program will reach a clinical trial." On cancer, FDA approvals are regulatory facts about specific drugs for specific indications, not endorsements: gedatolisib (Revtorpyk) for HR-positive, HER2-negative breast cancer, 14 July 2026; dostarlimab (Jemperli) for dMMR/MSI-H rectal cancer, accelerated approval 19 October 2026; sevabertinib (Hyrnuo) for HER2-mutant non-squamous lung cancer, accelerated approval 9 September 2026; amivantamab plus hyaluronidase (Rybrevant Faspro) for head-and-neck squamous cell carcinoma, accelerated approval 5 November 2026. No verified total count of FDA oncology approvals for 2025 or 2026 was established; do not quote a yearly total. No cure, treatment, or diagnosis claim attaches to any of this.

## What S18, S27, and S28 would need to be real

A proposed BioDAO (S18) would need, first, a shared-contract model copied from Molecule and BIO Protocol: open governance, vesting, and airdrop contracts; an IP-NFT or Lab container for the IP; and IPTs documented as governance and information rights with no guaranteed return. Second, a governance design that stays live — the VitaDAO and AthenaDAO pauses show a Snapshot space with no proposals for a year is the default failure mode, not an exception. Third, a funding record it does not yet have: no projects, no dollars, no IP. A proposed Genomic DAO (S27) and Genome.Blockchain (S28) would need the GA4GH model end to end: a Data Use Ontology for consent terms, a Passport-style authorisation system, federated data-visiting instead of downloads, an access committee with ethics and participant representation, a ban on re-identification and sharing with non-collaborators, and a UK Biobank-style bar on insurer access. Above all, they must learn from openSNP: no CC0 individual genomes, no open download of personal data, and no claim that a blockchain makes private data safe. A ledger can record consent, provenance, and access; it cannot make a genome anonymous. The honest sequence is controlled and federated first, DAO-governed licensing second, open data only where the data is genuinely non-personal — compounds, not genomes.

## Glossary

- **BioDAO:** a decentralised organisation funding research in one therapeutic area, holding IP on-chain.
- **Data visiting:** analysing shared data inside the provider's environment.
- **dbGaP:** NCBI Authorized Access for controlled-access genomic data.
- **DUO:** Data Use Ontology — GA4GH's vocabulary of machine-readable data-use terms.
- **IP-NFT:** a non-fungible token carrying legal intellectual-property rights.
- **IP-Token (IPT):** a fractional ERC-20 against an IP-NFT or Lab, carrying governance and information rights.
- **Lab (Molecule V3):** an NFT bound to its own smart-contract wallet, holding a project's treasury and licences.
- **SPV:** special-purpose vehicle holding specific assets (AthenaBIO).

## Frequently asked questions

**Does an IP-Token pay its holders?**
Not as documented. IPTs carry governance and information rights with no guaranteed return.

**Is any BioDAO or genomic series in this programme formed?**
No. S4, S5, S18, S27, and S28 are proposed, not formed; no entity, bank account, token, or dataset exists.

**Why not publish genomes openly, as openSNP did?**
openSNP closed in 2025 and deleted all user data, after the 23andMe bankruptcy, because its founder judged open CC0 individual genetic data unsafe to hold.

**What is the AGI Compounds library today?**
Six listed ACP jobs for the ALS Researcher agent, offline, with no jobs completed and no compound data file.

**Does AthenaDAO have a code repository?**
No official public repository was found; only a third-party dashboard.
