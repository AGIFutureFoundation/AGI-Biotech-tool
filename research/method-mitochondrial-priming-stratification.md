## §0 — What this method is, and how to read its grades

### 0.1 The method in one paragraph

Mitochondrial Priming Stratification (MPS) is a two-measurement stratification method. **Measurement 1** is functional: BH3 profiling of a neuron's mitochondria, summarised as a BH3 EC50 — the peptide concentration at which half-maximal mitochondrial outer membrane permeabilisation (MOMP) occurs — which reports how close the neuron sits to its apoptotic threshold ("priming"). **Measurement 2** is molecular: baseline phosphorylation of Akt at Ser473 (pAkt S473) and of its substrate BAD at Ser136 (pBad S136; Ser112 secondary), measured **before** treatment, in the same cells. The method's claim is that these two measurements, taken together, predict which neurons gain the most from an apoptosis-axis intervention that **enhances** anti-apoptotic BCL-2-family function — BCL-XL stabilisation plus neuron-targeted MCL1 gain-of-function — and that the prediction runs in the opposite direction to oncology, where high pAKT marks sensitivity to BCL-2-family **inhibition**. The method is a protocol for testing that claim in three diseases in order (ALS, then Alzheimer's, then Parkinson's), with the statistical test that decides it — the treatment × stratum interaction — fixed before any confirmatory data are collected.

### 0.2 Grading discipline

The grades below are the loop-1 grades, applied per claim, not per document:

| **UNVERIFIED** | No verified evidence | The claim rests on an internal document, a model output, or a search that has not been re-run. Stated as such, never upgraded by repetition. |

**Rules applied throughout this document:**

1. A claim is only as strong as its grade, and the grade sits next to the claim.
2. Model predictions are labelled **"model prediction, not yet validated in wet lab"** wherever they appear. The flagship model's numbers (MOMP 0.764 → 0.040; Bliss peak +0.0305 at day 13.25; BH3 EC50 held ~112.42 vs control 65.29 → 15.84) are single-source model outputs; the model code was not available (GAP-FLAG-1). They are used in §4 only as the *shape* of a prediction to test (acute near-additivity → chronic synergy; priming attenuation), never as effect sizes for power calculations.
3. Catalog numbers are labelled **"predicted (document) values"**. The catalog holds 336 HYPOTHETICAL structures (of 413 numbered entries) and 0 verified entries (see §7.4 for the reconciliation of 413 / 336 / 24 distinct SMILES); its POC/POM claims have no citations and are unverified document claims.
4. A negative search result is reported with its strategy (§10), so it can be re-run and refuted. A negative result is never stated as a positive fact about the world.
5. Prognostic and predictive are different words with different tests. A marker is **prognostic** if it tracks outcome regardless of treatment. It is **predictive** only if the treatment effect differs by marker stratum — the treatment × stratum interaction. MPS is a predictive claim, and only the interaction test can confirm it. This distinction is the reason the patent finding in §10 matters and the reason it does not, by itself, kill the hypothesis: the patent proposes pAkt as prognostic and as a responsiveness marker for a different intervention (an Akt-activating peptide), not as a predictive biomarker for a BCL-2-family intervention tested by an interaction.

### 0.3 What MPS is not

- MPS is not a diagnostic. It does not diagnose ALS, Alzheimer's, or Parkinson's in any person. It is an in-vitro research stratification: a way of sorting cultured neurons before an experiment.
- MPS is not a treatment. Even if every test in §4 passes, the result is a validated in-vitro mechanism and a stratification design — not a therapy. Translation would need animal work, delivery, GMP manufacturing, and regulatory engagement the Foundation does not have (§11).
- MPS is not a claim that BH3 profiling has been used in neurodegeneration before. No paper in this pass reports a BH3-profiling EC50 in an Alzheimer's or Parkinson's model, and the claim that longitudinal BH3 profiling has not been applied in neurodegeneration is a working gap claim, re-tested in §10.4 — it survived this pass's search, but a search is not a proof, and the search strategy is recorded so it can be refuted.

## §1 — Formal hypothesis and mechanism

### 1.1 The formal hypothesis

**H-MPS-1 (primary) [Grade B]:** In human iPSC-derived neurons under chronic disease-relevant stress, baseline Akt/Bad phosphorylation status — pAkt (S473) and pBad (S136), measured before treatment — stratifies the response to combined BCL-XL stabilisation + neuron-targeted MCL1 gain-of-function: **Akt-low / pBad-low neurons gain the greatest marginal benefit from the combination, and specifically from the BCL-XL-stabiliser arm**, because in those neurons unphosphorylated BAD is free to antagonise BCL-XL, leaving BCL-XL functionally impaired and therefore most rescuable.

**H-MPS-1a (mechanistic, acute) [model prediction, not yet validated in wet lab]:** The combination's acute MOMP inhibition is near-additive, not synergistic (Bliss excess ≈ 0 to +0.01 over Days 1–2), because each single intervention alone is sufficient to suppress acute MOMP — a floor effect.

**H-MPS-1b (mechanistic, chronic) [model prediction, not yet validated in wet lab]:** Over 42 days the combination transitions to strengthening synergy (Bliss excess rising to ~+0.03, peaking ~Day 13), because delayed-onset MOMP becomes the dominant failure mode and the two arms cover different failure modes (BAX vs BAK; MODE 1 vs MODE 2).

**H-MPS-1c (endpoint) [model prediction, not yet validated in wet lab]:** The combination attenuates mitochondrial priming: BH3-profiling EC50 is maintained near its starting value in the combination arm while falling steeply in control under chronic stress.

**H-MPS-2 (cross-disease) [Grade C]:** H-MPS-1 generalises across neuronal types and stressors: the same low-pAkt/pBad stratum gains the most in Aβ-stressed cortical neurons (Alzheimer's model) and in MPP+-stressed dopaminergic neurons (Parkinson's model), because the Akt→Bad→BCL-XL chain is a general neuronal survival mechanism, not an ALS-specific one.

**H-MPS-3 (assay) [Grade B]:** BH3-profiling EC50 is a valid, disease-agnostic functional endpoint in post-mitotic human neurons: it moves before irreversible death, moves in opposite directions under a BCL-XL inhibitor vs a BCL-XL stabiliser, and its peptide-panel pattern (NOXA → MCL-1 dependence; BAD → BCL-2/BCL-XL/BCL-w dependence) holds in neurons as it does in cancer cells.

**Scope limit:** H-MPS-1 through H-MPS-3 are claims about neuronal survival and mitochondrial priming *in vitro*. They make no claim about patients, disease progression, or clinical benefit.

### 1.2 The mechanism, step by step

**Step 1 — The BCL-2 family controls MOMP, the commitment step of intrinsic apoptosis. [Grade A]**

The BCL-2 family controls mitochondrial outer membrane permeabilisation (MOMP): anti-apoptotic members (BCL-2, BCL-XL, MCL1, BCL-w, BFL1/A1) sequester pro-apoptotic effectors (BAX, BAK) and BH3-only activators (BIM, BID, PUMA) and sensitisers (BAD, NOXA, BMF), preventing BAX/BAK oligomerisation, cytochrome c release, and caspase activation (Del Gaizo Moore & Letai 2013; Bolomsky et al. 2020 — flagship §2, V3/V5). MOMP is the point of no return for the intrinsic pathway: once BAX/BAK oligomerise and the outer membrane permeabilises, the cell is committed to death. Everything MPS measures and manipulates sits upstream of, or at, this step.

**Step 2 — Sequestration happens in two modes. [Grade A in cancer/developmental models; Grade B for the neuronal transfer]**

Llambi et al. (2011, *Mol Cell* 44:517–531; PMID 22036586; PMCID PMC3221787) established a unified model in which pro-survival BCL-2 proteins block MOMP in two modes: **MODE 1**, sequestering direct-activator BH3-only proteins (BIM, BID, PUMA) so they cannot activate BAX/BAK; and **MODE 2**, binding activated BAX and BAK directly. MODE 1 sequestration is less efficient and more easily de-repressed than MODE 2, and MODE 2 additionally prevents mitochondrial fusion. The two modes are sequentially engaged in stressed cells. MPS relies on this framework in two ways. First, it explains why two interventions on the same axis can be complementary rather than redundant: a BCL-XL stabiliser and MCL1 gain-of-function cover different failure modes, including MODE 1 vs MODE 2. Second, it sets the assay expectation: a BH3-profiling peptide panel reads out which mode is failing, because different peptides probe different dependencies (NOXA → MCL-1; BAD → BCL-XL/BCL-2). **Qualification:** the MODE 1 / MODE 2 framework was established in cancer and developmental models, not in neurons. Its neuronal validity is assumed in MPS, not shown — and §6 includes a test that would expose a failure of that assumption (if the peptide-panel mapping does not hold in neurons, H-MPS-3 fails while the survival result, if any, would need a different mechanistic explanation).

**Step 3 — BCL-XL holds BAX off the mitochondria by constant retrotranslocation. [Grade A]**

Edlich et al. (2011, *Cell* 145:104–116; PMID 21458670; PMCID PMC3070914) showed that BAX in healthy cells continually binds mitochondria and is retrotranslocated back to the cytosol through interaction with BCL-XL, measured by fluorescence loss in photobleaching (FLIP); BCL-XL overexpression nearly doubles the retrotranslocation rate, and BH3-only proteins and the BCL-2/BCL-XL inhibitor ABT-737 disrupt the equilibrium and let BAX accumulate on mitochondria. This is the mechanistic basis for the word "stabilisation" in MPS: a BCL-XL stabiliser, if it exists, must increase the rate or efficiency of this retrotranslocation (or an equivalent BCL-XL function), not merely occupy BCL-XL's binding groove. The FLIP assay is therefore a Phase 0 gate (§7.2), not an optional characterisation: it is the assay that distinguishes a stabiliser from a binder and from an inhibitor wearing the wrong label.

**Step 4 — Akt phosphorylates BAD, and phosphorylated BAD cannot antagonise BCL-XL. [Grade A]**

Datta et al. (1997, *Cell* 91:231–241; PMID 9346240) showed that growth-factor activation of PI3K/Akt culminates in phosphorylation of BAD, suppressing apoptosis; Akt phosphorylates BAD in vitro and in vivo and blocks BAD-induced death of primary neurons in a site-specific manner. Unphosphorylated BAD binds and antagonises BCL-XL (and BCL-2/BCL-w); phosphorylated BAD is sequestered by 14-3-3 proteins and cannot antagonise BCL-XL. The mechanistic chain MPS relies on is therefore:

> **Low Akt activity → low pBad (S136) → free BAD antagonises BCL-XL → BCL-XL functionally impaired → neuron closer to MOMP → a BCL-XL stabiliser has more room to help.**
>
> Conversely, high Akt activity → high pBad → BAD already neutralised by 14-3-3 → BCL-XL already free → less marginal benefit from stabilising BCL-XL.

This chain is the whole rationale for the stratification prediction, and it generates the method's most important measurement rule: **pAkt and pBad are different variables and must be measured separately and tested separately.** pAkt (S473) is the upstream kinase activity; pBad (S136) is the mechanistic variable — the actual occupancy of the BAD→BCL-XL antagonism. They are correlated through the pathway, not identical: BAD is also phosphorylated at Ser112 (by PKA and p90RSK) and Ser155, and Akt has substrates other than BAD (FOXO, GSK3, mTOR pathway components). A neuron can in principle be pAkt-high and pBad-low (if BAD is dephosphorylated by a phosphatase, or if Akt is diverted to other substrates) or pAkt-low and pBad-high (if another kinase phosphorylates BAD). MPS therefore pre-specifies two separate interaction tests — one on the pAkt stratum, one on the pBad stratum (§4.5) — and treats the question "which stratifies better" as an empirical question the confirmatory phase answers, not an assumption the design makes.

**Step 5 — The BH3-only effectors that execute neuronal death are PUMA and BIM. [Grade B]**

Steckley et al. (2007, *J Neurosci* 27:12989–12999; PMID 18032672) showed in cortical neurons that oxidative stress induces the BH3-only members BIM, NOXA, and PUMA, but that *Puma*−/− neurons — not *Bim*−/− or *Noxa*−/− neurons — are remarkably resistant to apoptosis induced by multiple oxidative stressors, and that PUMA plays a dominant role in regulating BAX activation, including by complexing with BAX directly. In Parkinson's models, BIM is transcriptionally induced in nigral dopaminergic neurons in the MPTP mouse model, and dopaminergic-neuron-specific *Bim* deletion confers resistance to MPTP neurotoxicity (c-Jun/BIM paper, *Neuroscience* 2019, DOI 10.1016/j.neuroscience.2018.12.026 — abstract verified in this pass). PUMA and BIM are therefore the effectors MPS names in its knockdown test (§6.3): if PUMA/BIM knockdown does not phenocopy protection, the BH3-only route is not the mechanism, and the method's mechanistic story is wrong even if protection is observed. Note the asymmetry the literature imposes: PUMA dominates in oxidative-stressed cortical neurons; BIM dominates in MPTP-stressed dopaminergic neurons. MPS respects that asymmetry by pre-specifying PUMA knockdown as the primary mechanistic test in the ALS and AD arms and BIM knockdown as the primary mechanistic test in the PD arm, with the other knockdown as a secondary test in each arm — rather than assuming one effector rules all three diseases.

### 1.3 The two interventions, defined

**Intervention A — BCL-XL stabilisation (small molecule, conditional on D1).** A compound that binds BCL-XL and *enhances* its anti-apoptotic function, as demonstrated by (i) increased BAX retrotranslocation rate in the FLIP assay, (ii) increased BH3 EC50 in treated neurons, and (iii) a dose–response that saturates rather than inverts. The flagship's candidate is AGI-183, described only as "a BCL-XL stabiliser" with no structure given — **UNVERIFIED** (§7). **And the gap is deeper than one compound: no BCL-XL stabiliser pharmacology is known to exist at all (red-team finding, §7.1).** The entire known BCL-XL small-molecule field is inhibitory (navitoclax, A-1155463, A-1331852, WEHI-539), and the nearest real biology to "BCL-XL stabilisation" is the removal of a degradation signal: RNF183, an E3 ubiquitin ligase that degrades Bcl-xL during prolonged ER stress (PNAS 2018;115:E2762–E2771, PMID 29507230) — a protein, not a compound, and its pharmacology runs in the opposite direction to stabilisation. Until D1 resolves, Intervention A is a *specification* (what a stabiliser must do), not a compound, and the programme must invent a new pharmacology before it can test any hypothesis that depends on this arm. If no characterised stabiliser exists, the small-molecule arm of MPS collapses and the method proceeds on Intervention B alone, with the consequences stated in §6.6. The two arms' fates are separate: the failure of A does not kill B.

**Intervention B — Neuron-targeted MCL1 gain-of-function (gene therapy, AAV6-hSyn-MCL1).** An adeno-associated viral vector carrying MCL1 under the neuron-specific human synapsin (hSyn) promoter, intended to raise MCL1 expression in neurons only, avoiding the systemic exposure that caused cardiotoxicity with small-molecule MCL1 inhibitors. Two open design questions travel with this arm and are not settled in this document: the serotype (AAV6 is not the standard CNS serotype; AAV9 and engineered capsids such as PHP.eB are the usual CNS choices — GAP-FLAG-3) and the long-term safety of MCL1 overexpression in post-mitotic neurons (§2.4, V8 — the "low-risk" claim is unsupported by its citation). The hSyn promoter is a mitigation for off-target expression, not a proof of safety.

**Why two arms.** The orthogonality is a tendency, not a clean partition (Llambi et al. 2011: BAX prefers BCL-2 and BCL-XL over MCL-1, while BAK interacts more strongly with BCL-XL *and* MCL-1 and only poorly with BCL-2; BCL-XL therefore binds both effectors). The combination rationale survives the qualification: the two arms cover different failure modes — BCL-XL stabilisation primarily rescues the BAX/MODE 2 failure that free BAD creates, while MCL1 gain-of-function primarily covers the BAK failure mode — and the model predicts the combination's value grows over time as delayed-onset MOMP becomes the dominant failure mode (H-MPS-1b). The two arms are complementary on paper; whether they are complementary in neurons is what the factorial design tests.

### 1.4 The stratification logic, stated as a decision rule

Before treatment, each culture (or each donor line's neurons, depending on the assay's resolution — §3.3) is assigned to a stratum by its baseline pAkt (S473) and pBad (S136):

- **Low stratum:** pAkt below the pre-registered cut-point AND/OR pBad below its pre-registered cut-point (tested separately; §4.5).
- **High stratum:** at or above the cut-point.

The method predicts: **treatment benefit (combination minus vehicle, on survival and on BH3 EC50) is larger in the low stratum than in the high stratum, driven by the BCL-XL-stabiliser arm.** The prediction is directional and falsifiable: a null interaction (F1) kills the biomarker hypothesis even if the combination works overall; a reversed interaction (high stratum gains more) kills the mechanistic chain in §1.2 Step 4 as stated and requires a new mechanism, not a re-interpretation.

### 1.5 The inversion — stated every time, because omitting it is misrepresentation

| | Neurodegeneration (MPS) | Oncology |
|---|---|---|
| Goal | **Enhance** anti-apoptotic function in neurons (keep neurons alive) | **Inhibit** anti-apoptotic function in cancer cells (make cancer cells die) |
| MCL1 | Overexpress (AAV6-hSyn-MCL1) | Inhibit (AZD5991, AMG 176, S63845) |
| BCL-XL | Stabilise (AGI-183 — UNVERIFIED) | Inhibit (navitoclax, A-1331852) |
| Biomarker direction | pAkt/pBad-**low** predicts *benefit from enhancement* | pAKT-**high** predicts *sensitivity to inhibition* (Rice et al. 2019 — preclinical, NSCLC cell lines) |
| Shared | BCL-2 family biology; BH3 profiling / dynamic BH3 profiling methodology; Akt/Bad signalling | Same |

**Correction (peer review R6, 8 Nov 2026):** an earlier draft of the table above listed venetoclax as an MCL1 inhibitor. It is not: venetoclax (ABT-199) is a **BCL-2-selective** inhibitor (Ki <0.01 nM for BCL-2 vs 48 nM for BCL-XL and >440 nM for MCL-1; Souers et al., *Nat Med* 2013 — see PMC5457565). In oncology it is used *in combination with* MCL1 inhibitors, never as an MCL1 inhibitor itself. Venetoclax remains correctly cited below as proof that a BCL-2-family drug can reach patients (FDA-approved for AML and CLL).

**What crosses over (legitimate):** (a) BH3 profiling and dynamic BH3 profiling as a functional assay platform — the same EC50/priming readouts, the same peptide panels, transferable protocols and analysis code; (b) the Akt/Bad stratification *logic* — a phosphorylation-state biomarker that predicts response to a BCL-2-family intervention, with the sign of the prediction reversed; (c) the clinical failure record as a shared safety knowledge base — cardiotoxicity (AZD5991) and thrombocytopenia (navitoclax) are on-target class effects of *inhibition* that any BCL-2-family programme must design around; (d) venetoclax as proof that a BCL-2-family drug can reach patients (FDA-approved for AML and CLL), which bounds what is plausible without promising anything for neurodegeneration.

**What does NOT cross over:** no drug, no dose, no indication. An MCL1 inhibitor that kills cancer cells cannot be repurposed into MPS; a BCL-XL stabiliser (if it existed) would be an anti-cancer liability, not an asset. Navitoclax thrombocytopenia (N = 26: 14/26 = 53.8% all-grade, 10/26 = 38.5% grade 3/4 — de Vos et al. 2021, V9) is a toxicity of BCL-XL **inhibition**; it is cited in MPS only as the contrast that motivates stabilisation, never as a risk of the proposed intervention. AZD5991's troponin signal is likewise a toxicity of MCL1 **inhibition**, not of MCL1 gain-of-function. Any document, pitch, or conversation that implies the neurodegeneration and cancer programmes share a drug is wrong and must be corrected.

### 2.6 The Bye-vs-Answer-ALS discrepancy (GAP-ALS-13) — a standing warning for every design in this method

Answer ALS's 1,000-line iPS analysis did not report survival deficits, TDP-43 pathology, or pharmacological rescue in sporadic-ALS motor neurons, and its omics analyses did not separate sporadic ALS from controls — in open discrepancy with the Bye result (V13), in which motor neurons from a 100-donor sporadic-ALS library (drug screen run on **7–16 donors**; combination validation on 15 — see V13) did show reduced survival, neurite degeneration correlating with donor survival, and pharmacological rescue. A stratification that works in the TDP-43 Q331K familial model and fails in a sporadic-ALS model is a model-specific finding, and MPS must report it that way.

## §3 — The method itself: the MPS protocol

This section is the method. It is written as a protocol: inputs, measurements, decision rules, controls, and acceptance criteria. It is a design, not a result. Concentrations, doses, and cut-points that the source documents do not fix are specified here as *procedures for fixing them* (pilot, then lock), not as invented numbers.

### 3.1 Inputs and prerequisites

MPS requires, before any confirmatory experiment:

1. **A characterised BCL-XL modulator arm** — either the flagship's AGI-183 with D1 resolved (§7), or a named replacement stabiliser that has passed the Phase 0 gates. MPS does not run its factorial with an uncharacterised compound. The gene-therapy arm (Intervention B) can proceed independently if D1 fails (§6.6).
2. **A characterised MCL1 vector** — AAV6-hSyn-MCL1 (or a justified replacement serotype per GAP-FLAG-3) with verified MCL1 overexpression, neuron specificity, and an empty-vector control at the same MOI.
3. **Version-pinned differentiation protocols** for each neuronal type (§4), with a published protocol named and its version recorded in the pre-registration (§5).
4. **Validated phospho assays** — phospho-flow or quantitative Western / immunofluorescence for pAkt (S473), pBad (S136), pBad (S112), total Akt, total Bad, with phosphatase controls. The pilot phase (§3.7) validates the assay's dynamic range and reproducibility before the confirmatory phase uses it to assign strata. An assay that cannot separate low from high with a stated coefficient of variation cannot assign strata, and the confirmatory phase does not start until it can.
5. **A BH3-profiling assay adapted to post-mitotic neurons** — the pilot validates mitochondrial yield, peptide delivery, and the JC-1 / cytochrome-c readouts in neuronal cultures (§3.4, Appendix C). BH3 profiling is a functional assay, not a cancer-specific one, but its transfer to neurons is the method's central methodological contribution and must be demonstrated, not assumed.

### 3.2 The two measurements

**Measurement 1 — BH3 profiling (functional priming).** Mitochondria (or permeabilised cells) are exposed to a panel of BH3-domain peptides at a titration series, and MOMP is measured as cytochrome c release or loss of membrane potential (e.g. JC-1). The readout is the **BH3 EC50**: the peptide concentration producing half-maximal MOMP. A falling EC50 over time under stress means rising priming (the neuron is moving toward its apoptotic threshold); a held or rising EC50 means priming is attenuated. The peptide panel is:

| Peptide | What it reads out | Interpretation in MPS |
|---|---|---|
| BIM BH3 | Overall priming | Primary EC50 trajectory endpoint |
| BAD BH3 | BCL-2 / BCL-XL / BCL-w dependence | Tests the BCL-XL arm: stabilisation should raise the BAD-peptide EC50 (more BAD peptide needed to drive MOMP) |
| NOXA BH3 | MCL-1 dependence | Tests the MCL1 arm: MCL1 gain-of-function should raise the NOXA-peptide EC50 |
| PUMA BH3 | Direct activator sensitivity | Secondary; tracks the PUMA effector route |
| HRK / BMF (optional) | BCL-XL / BCL-w selectivity | Exploratory; only if the pilot shows added information over BAD |

Dynamic BH3 profiling (DBP) is the longitudinal form: the same panel repeated at pre-specified timepoints, so the method measures *changes* in priming induced by treatment and stress, not a single snapshot. The model prediction to test (H-MPS-1c) is a trajectory, and only a trajectory can test it.

**Measurement 2 — Akt/Bad phosphorylation (molecular stratifier).** Baseline pAkt (S473), pBad (S136), pBad (S112, secondary), total Akt, and total Bad, measured **before** treatment, in the same cells that will be treated. Ratios (pAkt/total Akt; pBad/total Bad) are computed and reported alongside absolute phospho signals, because total protein can vary by line and by stress; the pre-registration (§5) states which form is the stratification variable and which is reported as a sensitivity analysis. The pAkt:tAkt ratio is also the form the Ovadia patent uses (§10), which makes it the right sensitivity variable for comparability with that prior art.

**Timing rule (non-negotiable):** the stratifier is measured before treatment. A phosphorylation state measured after treatment is a response marker, not a stratification biomarker, and cannot test H-MPS-1. Any culture whose baseline measurement fails quality control is excluded before randomisation, by a rule written in the pre-registration — never after seeing the outcome.

### 3.3 Stratification: the primary analysis is continuous (red-team upgrade of F1)

The red team found the original F1 — a median-split treatment × stratum interaction — the weakest test in the method, because a null interaction cannot discriminate between (i) a wrong cut-point, (ii) a noisy phospho assay, (iii) the wrong phospho site (S136 vs S112 vs S473), or (iv) a genuinely false hypothesis, and the test reports all four as the same outcome ("biomarker fails"). MPS therefore adopts the strengthened design:

1. **Primary stratification analysis — continuous, not dichotomised.** The primary test is the treatment × **continuous** pBad (S136) interaction on the EC50 trajectory (and, as a second pre-specified test, treatment × continuous pAkt (S473)), with a pre-registered quantitative minimum meaningful interaction: **the EC50 difference between the lowest and highest pBad quartiles must be at least 20% of the pre-stress baseline BH3 EC50** (the same 20% magnitude as the stabiliser bar in §6.7), with its confidence interval excluding that minimum. **Interim bar, owner + deadline (peer-review fix R1, 8 Nov 2026):** this 20% figure is the pre-registered bar as of this document. The executing lab's principal investigator is the owner: at pre-registration, from pilot variance, the PI confirms this 20% bar or replaces it with a pilot-justified number, records the choice and its rationale, and locks it. **Confirmatory data collection cannot start until the number is locked.** No dichotomisation is involved in the primary test, so no cut-point can be blamed for a null.
2. **Median-split analysis — demoted to a sensitivity check.** The pilot-cohort median split (rule 3 below) is retained only as a pre-specified sensitivity analysis, reported alongside the continuous result. If the continuous interaction is significant but the median-split interaction is not (or vice versa), both are reported and the discrepancy is discussed, not hidden.
3. **Cut-point rule (for the sensitivity analysis only):** the cut-point is the **median** of the pilot cohort for each marker separately, locked in the pre-registration before the confirmatory phase starts.
4. **Testing:** the pAkt stratum and the pBad stratum are tested **separately** as two pre-specified interaction tests (§4.5), with Holm correction across the two. pBad (S112) is exploratory only.
5. **What is not permitted:** choosing a cut-point that maximises the interaction in the confirmatory data; dichotomising by a post-hoc tertile split; pooling pAkt and pBad into a single composite score unless that composite was pre-registered with its own rule. A composite (e.g. low on either marker vs high on both) may be pre-specified as a secondary analysis, but it is not the primary test.
6. **Reporting:** the full continuous distribution of pAkt and pBad is reported alongside the dichotomised sensitivity analysis. The interaction is also tested on the continuous scale as the primary — see rule 1 above.
7. **Within-line mechanistic manipulation (the discrimination the median split lacks).** A PI3K-inhibitor arm pharmacologically lowers pAkt/pBad in the *same* donor lines (§4.2, §4.4): **inhibitor LY294002** (reversible PI3K inhibitor, IC50 1.4 µM; 10 µM reduces p-Akt+ neurons in neural-progenitor culture, *J Neurosci* 2013;33:14318–14330; 50 µM abolishes PI3K activity without apparent toxicity, Vlahos et al., *J Biol Chem* 1994;269:5241–5248). Dose is set by pilot titration across 10–50 µM and locked at pre-registration, with the target of **≥50% reduction in pAkt (S473) and pBad (S136) vs vehicle**, confirmed by phospho assay in the same lines. **Controls:** an inhibitor-only arm (LY294002 without stabiliser) and a vehicle arm in the same lines, so a general toxicity effect of PI3K inhibition is separated from a chain-specific effect — if LY294002 alone kills neurons at the chosen dose, the manipulation is uninterpretable, not a fail of the chain. **Quantitative bar (peer-review fix R8):** the manipulation passes only if the stabiliser arm's benefit with LY294002 exceeds its benefit without LY294002 by **≥20% on the Day 42 BH3 EC50**, with the confidence interval excluding zero. If it does not, the chain is wrong regardless of what the between-line stratification shows. This arm bypasses stratification noise entirely, and it is part of the primary falsification design, not an optional extra.
8. **Second-stressor replication.** The interaction must replicate under a second stressor (oxidative stress, not only glutamate in the ALS rung) before any biomarker language is used in external descriptions (§6.1).

| Readout | Definition | Type |
|---|---|---|

| **BH3 EC50 (BAD, NOXA)** | As above, peptide-specific | **Primary** (arm-specific: BAD for the BCL-XL arm, NOXA for the MCL1 arm) |

Per-timepoint Bliss, peptide-panel readouts beyond BIM/BAD/NOXA, S112, neurite integrity in the ALS arm, and PUMA/BIM induction are secondary or exploratory and labelled as such. A secondary endpoint that "works" while both primaries fail does not rescue the hypothesis.

### 3.5 Threshold and decision rules (what counts as a pass)

These rules are fixed before the confirmatory phase. They use the pre-registered minimum meaningful effect, not a p-value alone:

1. **Stratification (H-MPS-1):** PASS if the treatment × **continuous** pBad (S136) interaction (primary) — or the treatment × continuous pAkt (S473) interaction (second pre-specified test) — is significant (Holm-corrected p < 0.05) **and** the estimated interaction effect is at least the pre-registered minimum meaningful effect — an EC50 difference between the lowest and highest pBad quartiles of **≥20% of the pre-stress baseline BH3 EC50** (interim bar, §3.3 rule 1; confirmed or replaced by the executing-lab PI from pilot variance at pre-registration, before confirmatory lock) — with its confidence interval excluding that minimum. The interaction must be in the predicted direction (lower pBad → greater benefit). A significant interaction in the opposite direction is a FAIL of H-MPS-1 as stated, not a "reverse discovery". The median-split interaction is a sensitivity check only (§3.3); it cannot pass or fail H-MPS-1 on its own. **And the interaction alone is not sufficient for biomarker language:** the interaction must also replicate under the second stressor (§3.3 rule 8) and the within-line PI3K-inhibitor manipulation (§3.3 rule 7) must move the stabiliser arm's benefit in the predicted direction by at least 20% on the Day 42 BH3 EC50 (LY294002 arm, CI excluding zero). If the between-line interaction passes but the within-line manipulation fails, the chain (§1.2 Step 4) is wrong and the stratification is a correlate, not a mechanism — reported as such.
2. **Combination benefit (H-MPS-1b):** PASS if the combination arm beats the best single arm on either primary endpoint at Day 42 (EC50 difference CI excludes 0; survival hazard-ratio CI excludes 1). The combination need not beat both single arms on both endpoints; the pre-registration states which comparison is primary.
3. **Priming attenuation (H-MPS-1c):** PASS if the combination arm's BH3 EC50 trajectory is significantly flatter than control's (treatment × time interaction on EC50, p < 0.05) **and** the Day 42 EC50 difference is in the predicted direction. This is the endpoint test, not the synergy test: it can pass while Bliss synergy fails, and vice versa.
4. **Synergy (H-MPS-1a/1b):** synergy is claimed at a timepoint only where the Bliss excess 95% bootstrap CI excludes 0. Acute near-additivity is the *prediction* for Days 1–2 (Bliss ≈ 0 to +0.01), so an acute Bliss near zero **confirms** H-MPS-1a; it does not fail it. Chronic synergy is the prediction for ~Day 13 onward. A Bliss ≤ 0 across the whole 42-day window, *combined with* single arms not individually suppressing acute MOMP, is F5 (§6).
5. **Assay validity (gate, every run):** the BCL-XL inhibitor control must move priming in the opposite direction (lower EC50 / higher priming). If it does not, the run is uninterpretable (F3, §6), not negative — it is re-run, not reinterpreted.
6. **Minimum meaningful effect:** the pre-registration states a minimum meaningful effect for the interaction and for the Day 42 EC50 difference *before* data collection, in the units of the readout. The pilot estimates the variance; the minimum meaningful effect is a scientific judgement (what size of stratification would matter for a future programme), not a statistical artefact of the pilot. The pre-registered numbers are: interaction ≥20% of the pre-stress baseline BH3 EC50 between the lowest and highest pBad quartiles (§3.3 rule 1); Day 42 EC50 difference ≥20% of baseline EC50. Both are interim bars confirmed or replaced by the executing-lab PI from pilot variance at pre-registration, before confirmatory lock — confirmatory data collection cannot start until the numbers are locked.

### 3.6 Controls (every confirmatory run includes all of these)

| Control | Purpose | Expected result | If it fails |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vehicle | Baseline under stress | EC50 falls; survival falls | Run invalid; investigate stressor dose |
| Empty AAV vector (same MOI) | Vector-effect control | Indistinguishable from vehicle | Vector itself is toxic or protective — the MCL1 arm is uninterpretable until resolved |
| Inactive AGI-183 analogue / scaffold control (if synthesis permits) | Compound-specificity control | Indistinguishable from vehicle | Effect is scaffold/off-target, not BCL-XL stabilisation |
| **BCL-XL inhibitor arm** (navitoclax or A-1331852, sub-lethal dose) | Mechanistic contrast + assay validity | **Increases priming / decreases EC50** — the opposite of the stabiliser prediction | **F3: assay failure.** Run uninterpretable; re-run, do not reinterpret |
| MCL1 knockdown / knockout subset | MCL1-dependence of the vector arm | Vector-arm benefit abolished or reduced | Vector-arm benefit is not MCL1-mediated |
| PUMA knockdown (ALS/AD arms) / BIM knockdown (PD arm) | BH3-only mechanism test (§6.3) | Phenocopies protection if the BH3-only route is the mechanism | Mechanistic story wrong even if protection observed |
| No-stress arm per line | Stress-dependent baseline | No EC50 fall; no survival deficit | Stressor is not the driver; the model is mis-specified |
| Isogenic corrected control (ALS arm) | Mutation vs donor background | Corrected lines show less stress sensitivity | Effect is donor background, not the TDP-43 mutation |
| Phosphatase control (phospho assays) | Assay specificity | Phospho signal abolished | Phospho readout is non-specific; strata invalid |

### 3.7 Phases of the method

MPS runs in phases, in order, with gates between them. Nothing downstream starts until its gate passes.

**Phase 0 — Compound and vector qualification (gating milestone).** Detailed in §7.2. Gate 0a (identity), Gate 0b (target engagement + functional enhancement), Gate 0c (vector). If Gate 0a fails, the programme stops and reports "AGI-183 could not be established as a characterised compound" — a publishable negative result, not a failure to be hidden. **Sequencing (peer review B1-G4, answered in one sentence): the vector-only (AAV-MCL1) arm may start before D1 resolves; the small-molecule factorial waits for D1 — the programme does not idle on D1, only the stabiliser arm does.**

**Phase 1 — Pilot (variance estimation, assay validation).** Design: 2×2 factorial (vehicle / stabiliser / AAV-MCL1 / combination) × stress, in one donor line + its isogenic control, 3 independent differentiations (biological replicates, not technical replicates of one differentiation). Endpoints: longitudinal BH3 profiling (BIM/NOXA/BAD panels) + time-resolved survival. Purpose: estimate variance, confirm assay dynamic range (does BH3 EC50 actually move under stress in this model? does the combination arm separate from single arms?), validate the pAkt/pBad assay, and fix the continuous-analysis plan and the median-split sensitivity rule (§3.3). **Between-line variance gate (peer review B1-G3):** the pilot must show that baseline pBad (S136) varies between donor lines by more than the assay's within-line measurement noise — operationalised as an intraclass correlation (ICC) ≥0.5 for baseline pBad across lines, or equivalently between-line SD ≥ within-line measurement SD. If the lines barely differ (ICC <0.5), the between-line stratification is untestable regardless of N: the pilot reports this, the confirmatory phase does not proceed on the between-line claim, and the programme falls back to the within-line manipulation (F1 leg (b)) as the only stratification test. **MOMP-dependence check (peer review B1-G5):** the pilot tests whether glutamate-induced death in this model is BAX/BAK-dependent, using a BAX/BAK-inhibitor rescue arm. If a BAX/BAK inhibitor does not rescue glutamate death, the *model* fails the check — the stressor is replaced or the rung stops — not the hypothesis; the proportion of glutamate death that is BAX/BAK-dependent is a measured pilot quantity, and no published proportion was found in this pass to pre-state it (open item, §12.3). **No hypothesis testing on pilot data.** The pilot sizes the confirmatory phase and locks the analysis plan. The model-predicted effect sizes are **not** used as the power calculation's effect size — model outputs systematically overstate real effects. If the pilot shows the model over-predicted (the expected outcome), the confirmatory phase is sized on the pilot effect, or the hypothesis is revised. This rule is the protection against designing a study that can only "confirm" the model.

**Phase 2 — Confirmatory (pre-registered).** §4.2. Pre-registration before any confirmatory data collection (§5). Randomised plate layout, blinded outcome assessment (treatment identity hidden from the analyst until the database is locked).

**Phase 3 — Safety and specificity (parallel with Phase 2, reported together).** §4.7. Off-target expression, tumourigenicity screen in dividing cells, AGI-183 off-target selectivity panel, and a second-stressor generalisation cohort (e.g. oxidative stress in the ALS arm). A benefit that exists only under glutamate is a glutamate finding, not an ALS finding — and must be reported that way.

**Phase 4 — Cross-disease extension (AD, then PD).** §4.3–4.4. Gated on the ALS confirmatory phase completing (not necessarily passing — a clean ALS null with a valid assay is informative for the extension's design, and the extension's pre-registration states whether it proceeds after an ALS null and why).

## §4 — Experimental design: the cross-disease validation ladder

The ladder runs in order: **ALS → Alzheimer's → Parkinson's**. Each rung is a full experiment with its own pre-registration, its own controls (§3.6), and its own falsification verdict (§6). A rung's result does not depend on the previous rung's success, but its *design* does: each rung's pre-registration states what was learned from the previous rung and what changed as a result. The ladder tests H-MPS-1 in the disease it was designed for, then tests H-MPS-2 — whether the stratification generalises — in two further diseases. The ladder is the method's answer to the question "is this an ALS finding, or a neuronal finding?"

### 4.1 Common design elements (all rungs)

- **Factorial structure:** 2×2 (vehicle / BCL-XL stabiliser / AAV-MCL1 / combination) × stress (stress / no-stress), with the controls of §3.6 in every run.
- **Timepoints:** Days 1, 2, 7, 13/14, 21, 28, 42, matching the model's horizon, so the predicted Bliss timecourse (peak ~Day 13) can be tested rather than assumed. The Day 13/14 timepoint is placed to bracket the predicted peak; the pre-registration states whether Day 13 or Day 14 is used, and does not move it after seeing data.
- **Replicates:** minimum 3 donor lines × 3 independent differentiations per arm (biological replicates). A single donor line cannot distinguish a treatment effect from a line artefact.
- **Randomisation and blinding:** randomised plate layout; blinded outcome assessment until database lock.
- **Analysis:** linear mixed-effects model for longitudinal BH3 EC50 (fixed effects: treatment arm, time, stratum, treatment × stratum × time interaction; random effects: donor line, differentiation batch); Kaplan–Meier curves per arm and a Cox proportional-hazards model (or a parametric survival model if proportional hazards fail) with the same interaction structure; Bliss excess per timepoint with bootstrap confidence intervals.
- **Multiplicity:** primary endpoints (EC50 trajectory, survival) tested at α = 0.05 with Holm correction across the two families; all other comparisons secondary/exploratory and labelled as such.
- **Reporting:** all results reported including nulls; raw data (BH3 profiling curves, survival times, phospho measurements) deposited in a public repository with the pre-registration.

### 4.2 Rung 1 — ALS: the 2×2 factorial (carried forward, not redesigned)

This is the flagship programme's design (flagship §§5–7), carried forward unchanged except for the corrections in §2.5 and the tightened rules in §3. It is not redesigned here, because redesigning a design that has not yet been run would be activity without information.

- **Cells:** human iPSC-derived motor neurons carrying TDP-43 Q331K (familial ALS mutation), differentiated by a published, version-pinned protocol. Minimum **three independent donor lines** (Q331K) plus **three isogenic corrected controls** (CRISPR-corrected Q331K→WT from the same donors). Rationale: isogenic controls separate the mutation's effect from donor background; the Bye-vs-Answer-ALS discrepancy (GAP-ALS-13, §2.6) shows iPSC ALS models do not yet agree with each other. **Sporadic-ALS lines (peer review B1-G12): two sporadic-ALS donor lines from the Bye-style panel run in parallel as a second cohort in the confirmatory phase — they enter before the ALS verdict is generalised, not after it.** The ALS verdict is stated separately for the familial (Q331K) cohort and the sporadic cohort; a stratification that passes in one cohort only is reported as cohort-specific.
- **Stress:** chronic glutamate stress (excitotoxicity), dose and schedule fixed in the pilot and locked for the confirmatory phase. A no-glutamate arm per line establishes the stress-dependent baseline. Glutamate is a model stressor, not a claim about ALS aetiology.
- **Readout window:** 42 days, timepoints per §4.1.
- **Stratification:** baseline pAkt (S473) and pBad (S136; S112 secondary), measured before treatment. Primary analysis on the **continuous** scale (§3.3, §4.5); median-split (pilot median) as a sensitivity check only. Within-line PI3K-inhibitor manipulation arm included (see §3.3 rule 7).
- **Primary test:** the treatment × stratum interaction (§4.5).
- **Mechanistic test:** PUMA knockdown as the primary BH3-only test (Steckley 2007, V11: PUMA dominates oxidative-stress neuronal apoptosis in cortical neurons; glutamate stress converges on oxidative stress), BIM knockdown secondary.
- **Within-line manipulation (red-team upgrade, part of the primary falsification design):** a PI3K-inhibitor arm (LY294002, 10–50 µM by pilot titration; bar in §3.3 rule 7) that pharmacologically lowers pAkt/pBad in the *same* donor lines. Pre-specified prediction: lowering pAkt increases the stabiliser arm's benefit within the same cells. If it does not, the Akt→Bad→BCL-XL chain is wrong regardless of what the between-line stratification shows (§6.1, F1-strengthened).
- **Second-stressor cohort (Phase 3):** oxidative stress (e.g. a defined oxidative insult, dose fixed in pilot) in a small extension cohort, to test whether the combination's benefit generalises beyond glutamate.
- **What this rung decides:** whether H-MPS-1 holds in the disease and model it was designed for. It does not decide H-MPS-2.

### 4.3 Rung 2 — Alzheimer's extension

- **Cells:** iPSC-derived cortical neurons (preferred, for human relevance and for comparability with the ALS rung's iPSC system) — or rat primary cortical neurons, matching the model system of the Alzheimer's patent's own preclinical work and of Steckley et al. 2007 (V11). The pre-registration names one primary system and, if the other is used, states whether it is a replication cohort or the primary. Mixing systems without naming a primary is not permitted.
- **Stress:** Aβ1-42, dose and schedule fixed in the pilot and locked for the confirmatory phase. A no-Aβ arm per line establishes the stress-dependent baseline.
- **Measurements:** (a) baseline pAkt (S473) / pBad (S136) and BH3-profiling EC50 trajectories, measured before and during stress; (b) whether BCL-XL stabilisation / MCL1 gain-of-function attenuates Aβ-induced MOMP and neurite loss; (c) whether the pAkt/pBad-low stratum gains the most — the same stratification prediction as H-MPS-1, in a second disease model.
- **Primary endpoints:** neurite integrity joins survival as a primary endpoint in this rung (neurite loss is the disease-relevant functional readout for cortical neurons under Aβ stress), alongside the BH3 EC50 trajectory. The pre-registration states the endpoint hierarchy; it is not reordered after data collection.
- **Controls:** BCL-XL inhibitor arm (should worsen priming), empty vector, PUMA knockdown (primary — PUMA is the dominant oxidative-stress effector in cortical neurons, V11) and BIM knockdown (secondary).
- **Background result the rung builds on:** Bcl-2 overexpression in 3xTg-AD mice limited caspase-9 activation, reduced plaques/tangles, and improved memory (Rohn et al. 2008, V15 — Grade B). That result supports "anti-apoptotic enhancement can protect AD neurons" in a mouse; it does not support the MPS interventions or the stratification prediction, which is what this rung tests. **Developmental confound (peer review B1-G8):** in Rohn's mice Bcl-2 was overexpressed from development in all postmitotic neurons, so the protection could be a developmental neuroprotection effect rather than an adult-intervention effect. This rung tests adult intervention (treatment applied to differentiated neurons), so a positive Rohn result does not predict the rung outcome, and a rung null does not contradict Rohn — the two test different things, and the reporting states that.
- **What this rung decides:** whether H-MPS-2 holds in Alzheimer's. If the low stratum does not gain the most here, the cross-disease extension fails for AD while the ALS programme can still stand on its own (§6.6).
- **Confound to carry:** Aβ stress is a model stressor. A benefit under Aβ1-42 is an Aβ finding until replicated under a second AD-relevant stressor (e.g. oxidative stress, tau pathology) — the rung's pre-registration states whether a second stressor cohort is included, and if not, the limitation is stated in the reporting.

### 4.4 Rung 3 — Parkinson's extension

- **Cells:** iPSC-derived dopaminergic neurons, differentiated by a published, version-pinned protocol.
- **Stress:** MPP+ (the active metabolite of MPTP) or rotenone, dose and schedule fixed in the pilot and locked for the confirmatory phase. MPP+ is preferred as primary because the BIM mechanism (V12) is established in the MPTP/MPP+ system; rotenone is the secondary stressor cohort if included.
- **Pre-specified mechanistic readout:** whether BIM induction (the published MPTP mechanism, V12) is the priming signal that BCL-XL/MCL1 enhancement counteracts. BIM mRNA + protein are measured longitudinally alongside the BH3 EC50 trajectory, and the analysis tests whether the EC50 fall tracks BIM induction in time.
- **Controls:** BCL-XL inhibitor arm, empty vector, BIM knockdown (primary in this rung — the published mechanism is BIM, not PUMA) and PUMA knockdown (secondary). A PI3K-inhibitor arm (pharmacologically lowering Akt) is included as a mechanistic control: if lowering Akt *increases* the benefit of BCL-XL stabilisation, the Akt→Bad→BCL-XL chain is confirmed in a second disease by direct manipulation, not only by observational stratification. This arm is the strongest mechanistic test in the ladder, and it is pre-specified here rather than added post hoc.
- **Confound to carry — the GBA/GCase lysosomal axis (GAP-XD-10).** The GBA/GCase lysosomal axis is a separate Parkinson's mechanism: LTI-291 doubled GCase in Phase 1b but failed Phase IIb ACTIVATE (273 GBA-PD patients) and Bial discontinued it in June 2026. A PD experiment that does not stratify or control for GBA status risks attributing a lysosomal effect to the apoptosis axis, or vice versa. The rung's pre-registration therefore states the GBA status of every donor line (GBA variant carrier vs non-carrier, from genotyping) and includes GBA status as a covariate (or a stratification variable, if the line count permits) in the analysis. A PD result reported without GBA status is incomplete, and the reporting must say so.
- **What this rung decides:** whether H-MPS-2 holds in Parkinson's, and whether the BIM mechanism is the priming signal. If BIM induction does not track the EC50 fall, H-MPS-3's mechanistic account fails in PD even if protection is observed.

### 4.5 Statistical approach — the primary test

**The primary test of H-MPS-1 is the treatment × continuous-phospho interaction.** Not the main effect of treatment, not the main effect of stratum, not a subgroup p-value, and — following the red-team upgrade — not a median-split interaction. The distinction matters:

- A **main effect of treatment** (combination beats vehicle overall) shows the intervention works on average. It says nothing about stratification.
- A **main effect of stratum** (low-stratum neurons die faster overall) shows the marker is **prognostic**. It says nothing about whether the marker predicts *response to this treatment*. (This is exactly what the Ovadia patent already claims for pAkt — §10 — which is why a main effect of stratum would add nothing new.)
- A **treatment × stratum interaction** (the treatment benefit is larger in the low stratum than in the high stratum) is the only result that shows the marker is **predictive** for this intervention. This is the hypothesis.
- A **median-split interaction** cannot discriminate a wrong cut-point, a noisy phospho assay, the wrong phospho site, or a genuinely false hypothesis — all four read as the same null. The primary test is therefore the treatment × **continuous** pBad (S136) interaction, with the median-split analysis demoted to a sensitivity check (§3.3). The minimum meaningful interaction is quantified before data collection as an EC50 difference between the lowest and highest pBad quartiles of ≥20% of the pre-stress baseline BH3 EC50 (§3.3 rule 1; interim bar confirmed or replaced by the executing-lab PI from pilot variance before confirmatory lock).

**Models (pre-specified):**

- **BH3 EC50 trajectory:** linear mixed-effects model. Fixed effects: treatment arm (4 levels), time (as a factor at the pre-specified timepoints, not a linear trend — the predicted Bliss timecourse is non-monotone, peaking ~Day 13), **continuous pBad (S136)** (primary) / continuous pAkt (S473) (second pre-specified test), and the treatment × phospho × time interaction. Random effects: donor line, differentiation batch. The primary contrast is the treatment × continuous-phospho interaction at Day 42 (and, as a secondary contrast, the interaction on the trajectory as a whole). The median-split interaction is computed as a sensitivity check with the same model structure and the dichotomised stratum in place of the continuous variable.
- **Survival:** Kaplan–Meier curves per arm; Cox proportional-hazards model with treatment arm, **continuous pBad (S136)** (primary) / continuous pAkt (S473) (second test), and treatment × phospho interaction, with donor line as a strata or frailty term; a parametric survival model (e.g. Weibull) if proportional hazards fail, with the failure of proportional hazards reported, not hidden. The primary contrast is the interaction hazard ratio.
- **Stratification variables tested separately:** the interaction is tested on continuous pBad (S136) and continuous pAkt (S473) as two pre-specified tests, Holm-corrected. S112 is exploratory. The hypothesis predicts the combination's benefit — and specifically the AGI-183 single-arm benefit — is larger at lower phospho levels. The single-arm interaction (stabiliser × continuous phospho) is a pre-specified secondary test: it is the test that localises the stratification to the BCL-XL arm, as the mechanism (§1.2 Step 4) predicts.
- **Within-line manipulation test:** the PI3K-inhibitor arm (LY294002; §4.2, §4.4) is analysed as a within-donor-line contrast: stabiliser benefit with PI3K inhibition minus stabiliser benefit without it, in the same lines. The contrast passes only if it meets the §3.3 rule 7 bar (≥20% increase in stabiliser benefit on Day 42 EC50 with LY294002, CI excluding zero, with ≥50% pAkt/pBad reduction confirmed and the inhibitor-only control showing no standalone toxicity at the chosen dose). A positive contrast meeting the bar supports the chain; a null or negative contrast fails the chain regardless of the between-line interaction (§6.1).
- **Bliss formalism:** Bliss excess per timepoint = observed combination effect − expected effect under Bliss independence, computed from single-arm and combination survival/MOMP at that timepoint, with bootstrap 95% confidence intervals (resampling at the level of the independent differentiation, not the individual neuron — neurons within a differentiation are not independent observations). Synergy is claimed only where the 95% CI excludes 0. The Bliss analysis is secondary to the interaction test: a synergy result without a stratification result does not confirm H-MPS-1, and a stratification result without synergy does not refute it (the combination can stratify without being synergistic — see §6.6).

**Power approach (stated, not invented):** no confirmatory sample size is stated as fact in this document, because the variance is unknown until the pilot. The sizing rule is pre-specified: using pilot-derived variance, size the confirmatory phase for **80% power at two-sided α = 0.05 for the primary interaction test**, with the multiplicity control of §4.1, and a minimum of 3 donor lines × 3 independent differentiations per arm regardless of what the calculation returns. The model-predicted effect sizes (MOMP 0.764 → 0.040; EC50 15.84 → ~112.42) are **model predictions, not yet validated in wet lab** and must **not** be used as the power calculation's effect size. The power calculation is performed on the pilot effect, with the pilot's variance, and the calculation itself (inputs, formula, software, version) is deposited with the pre-registration. If the pilot effect is too small to power feasibly, the correct outcome is to revise the hypothesis or stop — not to power on the model's numbers. The unit of replication is the independent differentiation; the unit of analysis for the interaction is the culture (donor × differentiation × arm × stratum cell), with neurons nested within cultures. Any analysis that treats individual neurons as independent replicates is pseudoreplication and is not permitted.

### 4.6 Cancer counter-test (the inversion check)

The same BH3-profiling panel is run on a cancer cell line with the *inhibitor* direction (e.g. a BCL-XL/MCL1 inhibitor combination, per Rice et al. 2019, V2): the assay should read increased priming / decreased EC50, and high pAKT should mark sensitivity. This confirms the assay reads both directions and that the neurodegeneration programme's stabilisation logic is genuinely the mirror image, not a relabelling. The counter-test is a method-validation experiment, not a cancer programme, and it is reported as such. It does not need to be run before the ALS rung, but it must be run before any claim that MPS's assay "transfers from oncology" is made in an external description.

### 4.7 Safety and specificity (Phase 3, parallel with Phase 2)

- **Off-target expression:** quantify MCL1 overexpression in non-neuronal cells in the culture and, if any in-vivo work is ever proposed, in non-target tissues. hSyn restriction is a mitigation, not a proof.
- **Tumourigenicity screen:** because MCL-1 overexpression drives lymphomagenesis in mice (V5), any proposal to move beyond post-mitotic neurons must include a proliferation assay in dividing cells exposed to the vector and a long-term expression study. The in-vitro scope of MPS does not include dividing-cell exposure, and this document states that rather than implying the question is settled.
- **AGI-183 off-target profile:** broad selectivity panel against other BCL-2 family members (BCL-2, BCL-w, BFL1/A1) and a standard safety panel, once a characterised compound exists. A "BCL-XL stabiliser" that also stabilises MCL-1 or BCL-2 is a different drug with a different risk profile.
- **Glutamate-stress specificity:** test whether the combination's benefit generalises to a second stressor (e.g. oxidative stress) in a small extension cohort. A benefit that exists only under glutamate is a glutamate finding, not an ALS finding — and must be reported that way.
- **AAV serotype (GAP-FLAG-3):** the AAV6 choice is unjustified vs AAV9/PHP.eB. **Citation correction (red team):** Hordeaux et al. 2018 (*Mol Ther* 26:664–668) and Matsuzaki et al. 2018 (*Neurosci Lett* 665:182–188) tested **AAV-PHP.B, not PHP.eB** — the BBB advantage of PHP.B over AAV9 did not translate to BALB/c mice or to a non-human primate (marmoset) model. PHP.eB is a further-evolved PHP.B derivative (Chan et al. 2017) that inherits the Ly6a dependence: Hordeaux et al. 2019 (*Mol Ther* 27:912–921) showed the GPI-linked protein LY6A drives AAV-PHP.B transport across the blood–brain barrier, and LY6A is a mouse-specific endothelial protein absent from primate endothelium, which explains the transfer failure. Cite the 2018 papers for PHP.B and Chan 2017 + Hordeaux 2019 for the PHP.eB Ly6a link. The conclusion (strain-specific, no primate transfer) is unchanged. Before any in-vivo work, a capsid bake-off (AAV6 vs AAV9 vs PHP.eB, each carrying hSyn-MCL1 and an hSyn-GFP reporter) in human iPSC-derived motor, cortical, and dopaminergic neurons, plus non-neuronal controls (astrocytes, cardiomyocyte-lineage cells as the off-target sentinel, given the MCL1 cardiac history), with the decision rule strengthened per the red team: a pre-specified **absolute bar** — neuron-to-cardiomyocyte transduction ratio ≥10:1 **and** absolute motor-neuron transduction ≥50% at a stated MOI. If no capsid clears the bar, the gene-therapy arm stops; no "best of a bad lot" advance on a relative ranking. A **BBB-transwell arm** (human brain-endothelial monolayer) is included as a delivery gate alongside the tropism assay, so the bake-off measures both reaching the CNS and entering the right cells — transducing iPSC cardiomyocyte-lineage cells in a dish says nothing about biodistribution after dosing, and there is no BBB component in a tropism assay alone. Include AAV9 with a clinically relevant route proxy in the comparison set, and state the route (intrathecal vs IV) the bake-off is meant to inform before it runs.

## §5 — Pre-registration template

The confirmatory phase of each rung is pre-registered **before any confirmatory data collection**, on OSF (or equivalent) with a timestamped, uneditable record. Analysis code and the model code that generated the predictions are deposited alongside (GAP-FLAG-1 closes only when the model is reproducible). The fields below are the template; Appendix A gives the copy-ready field list. Every field must be filled before the confirmatory phase starts. An empty field is a missing pre-registration, not a detail to fill in later.

1. **Hypothesis:** H-MPS-1 (and H-MPS-2 / H-MPS-3 if the rung tests them), stated verbatim from §1.1, with the predicted direction.
2. **Model system:** cell type, donor lines (IDs), isogenic controls, differentiation protocol (name + version), passage/age at experiment start.
3. **Stressor:** agent, dose, schedule, duration; no-stress control definition.
4. **Arms:** the 2×2 factorial plus every control in §3.6, with doses/MOIs locked from Phase 0/1 and the lock date recorded.
5. **Stratification:** markers (pAkt S473; pBad S136; S112 secondary), assay method, phosphatase controls, **primary analysis on the continuous scale** with the quantitative minimum meaningful interaction (EC50 difference between the lowest and highest pBad quartiles ≥20% of the pre-stress baseline BH3 EC50; interim bar confirmed or replaced by the executing-lab PI from pilot variance before confirmatory lock — confirmatory data collection cannot start until the number is locked); median-split cut-point rule (pilot median) for the sensitivity analysis only, with the pilot cohort the median came from and the lock date.
6. **Primary endpoints:** BH3 EC50 trajectory (BIM; BAD; NOXA) and time-resolved survival, with readout definitions from §3.4 and the endpoint hierarchy.
7. **Secondary / exploratory endpoints:** Bliss excess timecourse, neurite integrity (AD/PD primary — state per rung), PUMA/BIM induction, S112, HRK/BMF panel.
8. **Timepoints:** Days 1, 2, 7, 13/14, 21, 28, 42 (state Day 13 vs Day 14).
9. **Statistical models:** the mixed-effects model, the survival model, the interaction contrasts (primary: combination × continuous pBad; second pre-specified: combination × continuous pAkt; secondary: stabiliser-arm × continuous phospho), the within-line PI3K-inhibitor contrast, the multiplicity correction (Holm across the two primary families; Holm across the two phospho tests), the minimum meaningful effect for the interaction and for the Day 42 EC50 difference, and the power calculation (pilot variance, 80% power, α = 0.05, minimum 3×3 replicates, calculation deposited).
10. **Bliss formalism:** the exact Bliss formula, the effect measure it is computed from (survival/MOMP), the bootstrap resampling unit (independent differentiation), and the rule that synergy is claimed only where the 95% CI excludes 0.
11. **Exclusion criteria:** pre-treatment exclusions (failed baseline phospho QC, failed differentiation QC, contamination) and post-treatment exclusions (none, unless a rule is written here — post-hoc exclusion by outcome is not permitted).
12. **Falsifiability thresholds:** F1–F6 (§6) plus the rung-specific tests, stated with the verdict each outcome triggers (kill vs narrow, §6.6).
13. **Blinding and randomisation:** plate layout randomisation method, blinding procedure, database-lock procedure.
14. **Data deposition:** repository, data formats, code repository, model code (or the statement that GAP-FLAG-1 remains open).
15. **Decision points:** D1 status at the time of registration; what happens to this rung if D1 fails mid-run (the vector-only fallback, §6.6); GBA status handling (PD rung).
16. **Deviations log:** any deviation from this registration after data collection starts is recorded here with date and reason. An unlogged deviation is a breach of the registration, and the reporting must say so.

## §6 — Falsification battery: what kills the method outright vs what narrows it

Pre-registered falsifiability thresholds. If any of F1–F3 holds in the confirmatory phase, H-MPS-1 is rejected as stated — and the rejection is published. A null result on H-MPS-1 does **not** falsify the underlying BCL-2 biology (V3–V10 are independent of this programme), and a positive result in one donor line does **not** confirm the hypothesis — confirmation requires the interaction to replicate across donor lines and under a second stressor.

### 6.1 F1 — No stratification (strengthened; red-team upgrade)

**Original form (weak, superseded):** the median-split treatment × stratum interaction is null for both pAkt and pBad strata.

**Why it was weak:** a null median-split interaction cannot discriminate a wrong cut-point, a noisy phospho assay, the wrong phospho site, or a genuinely false hypothesis — all four read as the same outcome. No minimum meaningful interaction effect was quantified. A null F1 would be argued about, not accepted.

**Strengthened form (this document's F1):** F1 holds — and H-MPS-1 is rejected as stated — only if **all three** of the following are true:

- (a) The treatment × **continuous** pBad (S136) interaction **and** the treatment × continuous pAkt (S473) interaction are both null (Holm-corrected p > 0.05, and the estimated interaction effects are small with confidence intervals excluding the pre-registered minimum meaningful effect — EC50 difference between the lowest and highest pBad quartiles ≥20% of the pre-stress baseline BH3 EC50, §3.3 rule 1); **and**
- (b) The **within-line PI3K-inhibitor manipulation** fails: pharmacologically lowering pAkt/pBad in the same donor lines (LY294002, §3.3 rule 7) does **not** increase the stabiliser arm's benefit by the §3.3 rule 7 bar (≥20% on Day 42 EC50, with ≥50% pAkt/pBad reduction confirmed); **and**
- (c) The interaction is null under the **second stressor** (oxidative stress in the ALS rung) as well as under the primary stressor.

**Verdict logic:** if (a) holds but (b) fails (i.e. the within-line manipulation *does* move the benefit in the predicted direction by the §3.3 rule 7 bar), the chain is supported and the between-line stratification failed for a measurement reason — assay noise, wrong site, or insufficient between-line phospho variance — and the verdict is "stratification not demonstrated *between lines*; mechanism supported *within lines*", a narrowing, not a kill. If (a) and (b) both hold, the chain itself is wrong and H-MPS-1 is rejected outright. **Undefined cases, now defined (peer review R8):** if (a) and (b) both hold but (c) fails — the interaction is null under the second stressor — the verdict is **NARROW, not KILL**: the stratification holds under the primary stressor only, the biomarker claim is stressor-specific and is reported as such, and no cross-stressor biomarker language is used. If (a) fails but (b) holds — the interaction is detected but below the minimum meaningful effect while the within-line manipulation works — the verdict is also **NARROW**: the mechanism is supported within lines, but the stratification is not demonstrated at a meaningful magnitude between lines; the stratifier is re-designed (or N increased on a pilot-justified basis) and the biomarker is not claimed. If the interaction is significant in the **opposite** direction (high phospho gains more), H-MPS-1 is rejected as stated regardless of (b) and (c) — the mechanistic chain in §1.2 Step 4 is wrong, and a new mechanism is required, not a re-interpretation.

**What F1 kills:** the biomarker hypothesis fails even if the combination works overall. A treatment that works equally in all strata is a treatment without a stratification biomarker, and MPS must say so.

### 6.2 F2 — No combination benefit

The combination arm does not beat the best single arm on either primary endpoint at Day 42 (EC50 difference CI includes 0; survival HR CI includes 1). The synergy programme fails even if single arms work. **Kill vs narrow:** F2 kills the *combination* claim, not the stratification claim: if a single arm (e.g. the MCL1 vector alone) works and stratifies, H-MPS-1 can be re-stated for the single arm — but only as a new pre-registered hypothesis in a new experiment, not by re-analysing the same data.

### 6.3 F3 — Wrong direction on priming (assay validity)

BH3 EC50 in the combination arm falls with stress like control (no attenuation of priming), **or** the BCL-XL inhibitor control does not move priming in the opposite direction. **Verdict:** the experiment is uninterpretable, not merely negative, and must be re-run, not reinterpreted. F3 is an assay failure, not a hypothesis failure — but a repeated F3 across two independent runs, with the inhibitor control failing both times, falsifies H-MPS-3 (the assay-transfer claim) and stops the ladder until the assay is fixed.

### 6.4 F4 — Target failure (Phase 0)

AGI-183 (or its replacement) shows no BCL-XL binding, no enhancement of BAX retrotranslocation, or no EC50 shift at any tolerated dose. **The stabiliser premise fails; the programme stops the small-molecule arm and reports.** See §7 for the quantitative bar. F4 kills the small-molecule arm only; the gene-therapy arm survives (§6.6).

### 6.5 F5 — Model failure

Bliss excess is ≤ 0 across the whole 42-day window (no synergy at any timepoint) *and* the acute floor-effect explanation is contradicted by single arms *not* individually suppressing acute MOMP. Discoveries 1/2's model is wrong, not merely unconfirmed. **Kill vs narrow:** F5 kills the model's synergy prediction and the "antagonism-as-resilience" interpretation. It does not kill H-MPS-1: a combination can be additive and still stratify. The stratification test and the synergy test are independent verdicts, and MPS reports them independently.

### 6.6 F6 — Novelty failure (already triggered)

**F6 has already fired.** The independent literature search (GAP-FLAG-2, re-run in this pass and by the red team — §10) found a prior proposal of pAkt as an ALS prognostic and treatment-responsiveness biomarker (Ovadia et al., patent family, priority 2011). Discovery 5's novelty claim is withdrawn; H-MPS-1 itself is unaffected, because the hypothesis does not depend on being first. F6's trigger condition is therefore recorded as **met**, its consequence already applied (the reframe in §10.5), and F6 is closed — it is not re-tested.

### 6.7 The stabiliser test (quantitative bar; red-team upgrade)

A candidate BCL-XL "stabiliser" must be shown to *enhance* BCL-XL function — binding alone is not stabilisation. The red team found the original stabiliser test set no quantitative bar for what counts as enhancement. The strengthened bar, pre-specified before Phase 0b runs:

- **≥1.5× increase in BAX retrotranslocation rate** (FLIP assay, Edlich et al. 2011 method) at a stated concentration, with a dose–response across at least 5 concentrations; **and**
- **≥20% increase in BH3 EC50** (BIM peptide, treated neurons vs vehicle) at that concentration; **and**
- **On-target proof:** the effect is abolished by BCL-XL knockdown. A compound whose EC50 shift survives BCL-XL knockdown is acting off-target, whatever it binds.
- **Direction check:** the compound must not behave as an inhibitor at any tested dose (no EC50 decrease, no retrotranslocation decrease). A compound that stabilises at low dose and inhibits at high dose is not a stabiliser with a therapeutic window — it is a dose-dependent inhibitor, and the programme must say so.

If no candidate clears this bar, the small-molecule arm collapses (the gene-therapy arm survives independently).

### 6.8 The PUMA/BIM knockdown test

If PUMA knockdown (ALS/AD arms) or BIM knockdown (PD arm) does **not** phenocopy protection, the BH3-only route is not the mechanism, and the method's mechanistic story is wrong even if protection is observed. **Kill vs narrow:** this kills the *mechanistic account* (H-MPS-1's "because" clause), not necessarily the *stratification* — a treatment can stratify by pAkt/pBad through a different downstream route. If the knockdown test fails, MPS reports protection (if any) as mechanistically unexplained and the PUMA/BIM language is removed from the method's claims.

### 6.9 The model-transfer test (GAP-ALS-13)

If the flagship's factorial reproduces in the Bye-style assay but not in the Answer ALS model (or vice versa), the result is model-dependent and the ranking does not transfer — the programme must say so, not pick the favourable model. **Procedure:** the key result (the interaction, if it passes) is re-run in a second model system (sporadic-ALS donor lines in the Bye-style assay, or the Answer ALS lines) with the same pre-registered analysis. A result that reproduces in one model only is reported as model-specific, with the model named in the same sentence as the result.

### 6.10 The AD/PD extension test

If the low-phospho stratum does **not** gain the most in Aβ-stressed cortical neurons (AD rung) and MPP+-stressed dopaminergic neurons (PD rung), the cross-disease extension fails while the ALS programme can still stand on its own. **Kill vs narrow, per rung:** an AD failure narrows H-MPS-2 to "ALS + PD" (if PD passes) or to "ALS only" (if both fail). A PD failure with a GBA confound unresolved is reported as "untested in GBA-stratified PD", not as a clean failure — the confound (§4.4) must be discharged before a PD verdict is final.

### 6.11 Kill vs narrow — summary table

| Outcome | Verdict |
|---|---|
| F1 strengthened (a)+(b)+(c) all hold | **KILL H-MPS-1 outright.** Biomarker hypothesis rejected as stated. |
| F1 (a) holds, (b) fails (within-line manipulation works) | **NARROW.** Mechanism supported within lines; between-line stratification not demonstrated. Re-design the stratifier, do not re-claim the biomarker. |
| F1 (a)+(b) hold, (c) fails (second-stressor null) | **NARROW.** Stratification holds under the primary stressor only; biomarker claim is stressor-specific; no cross-stressor biomarker language. |
| F1 (a) fails, (b) holds (interaction below the minimum meaningful effect) | **NARROW.** Mechanism supported within lines; stratification not demonstrated at a meaningful magnitude between lines. Re-design the stratifier or increase N on a pilot-justified basis; do not claim the biomarker. |
| F1 opposite-direction interaction | **KILL H-MPS-1 as stated.** Chain wrong; new mechanism required. |
| F2 (no combination benefit) | **KILL the combination claim.** Single-arm re-statement only as a new pre-registered hypothesis. |
| F3 (assay failure) | **UNINTERPRETABLE.** Re-run. Repeated F3 across two runs kills H-MPS-3 (assay transfer). |

| F5 (model failure) | **KILL the model's synergy prediction.** Stratification verdict independent. |

## §7 — The AGI-183 gate (D1), Phase 0 gates, and decision D3

### 7.1 D1: the central gating fact

**Decision D1: Does AGI-183 exist and engage BCL-XL?** The answer today is: **not established — and the gap is deeper than one compound.**

- The flagship's AGI-183 is named only in the internal paper (`als_4_8.pdf`, Apr 2026), with no structure, synthesis, characterisation, or external record. A public web search (7 Nov 2026, flagship) found nothing; an independent re-run (red team, 8 Nov 2026, two search phrasings) found nothing. **GAP-FLAG-4 stands.**
- The catalog **does** list an AGI-183 (entry 183, bromine CRBN inhibitor targeting FLIP, MW 353.32) — **a different compound; two records, one ID, unresolved.** The catalog entry does not verify the flagship compound, and the flagship compound does not describe the catalog entry. They are carried as two separate records until a structure (SMILES/InChI) for the paper's compound is produced.
- **There is no BCL-XL stabiliser pharmacology known to exist at all.** The entire known BCL-XL small-molecule field is inhibitory (navitoclax, A-1155463, A-1331852, WEHI-539). The nearest real biology is RNF183, an E3 ubiquitin ligase that degrades Bcl-xL during prolonged ER stress (PNAS 2018;115:E2762–E2771, PMID 29507230) — a protein, not a compound, running in the opposite direction. Unrelated hits returned by the AGI-183 searches (AG-183/Tyrphostin 51, an EGFR kinase inhibitor; an E3-ligase linker conjugate) confirm the absence by showing what the search *does* find instead.
- **Consequence:** the programme is not a drug-development problem but a drug-discovery problem. It must invent a new pharmacology before it can test any hypothesis that depends on the small-molecule arm. Everything downstream — the Bliss synergy timecourse, the MOMP 0.764 → 0.040 prediction, the pAkt/pBad stratification of the stabiliser arm — is conditional on that invention, and the model numbers come from code that is unavailable and cannot be reproduced (GAP-FLAG-1). **As designed, the programme is currently untestable, not merely untested.** That is the strongest objection to the programme as a whole, and this document carries it as its central gating fact rather than burying it in a gaps register. The correct answer to "what would you test first?" is the Phase 0 gates below, in order — and the programme leads with them, not with model predictions.

### 7.2 Phase 0 gates (nothing downstream starts until these pass)

**Gate 0a — Identity.** Obtain AGI-183's structure, synthesis route, purity (HPLC/MS, NMR), and a certificate of analysis. If no structure or synthesis exists, the programme stops here and the outcome is reported as "AGI-183 could not be established as a characterised compound" — a publishable negative result, not a failure to be hidden. **D1 = No** on this gate alone.

**Gate 0b — Target engagement + functional enhancement (the hardest gate).** Demonstrate AGI-183 binds BCL-XL (biophysical assay, e.g. fluorescence polarisation competition with a BH3 peptide) **and** *enhances* BCL-XL function, against the quantitative bar in §6.7: ≥1.5× BAX retrotranslocation rate (FLIP assay per Edlich et al. 2011, V6) and ≥20% BH3 EC50 shift at a stated concentration, with a dose–response across at least 5 concentrations, and abolition by BCL-XL knockdown. A binder that does not enhance function is not a stabiliser. A compound that antagonises BCL-XL is an inhibitor wearing the wrong label. **D1 = No** if this gate fails at any tolerated dose.

**Gate 0c — Vector.** AAV6-hSyn-MCL1 — verify MCL1 overexpression (qPCR/Western, fold-change vs endogenous), neuron specificity of the hSyn promoter in this culture (co-stain MCL1 with neuronal markers; quantify off-target expression in any non-neuronal cells present), and absence of toxicity from the empty vector at the chosen MOI. Dose–response for AGI-183 (at least 5 concentrations) and MOI titration for the vector are established in Phase 0; the confirmatory phase uses one locked dose per arm, chosen from Phase 0, not tuned later.

**Replacement rule:** if D1 fails, the programme may substitute a *named, characterised* BCL-XL modulator that passes Gates 0a–0b — but the substitution is a new Phase 0, not a continuation: the replacement's identity, engagement, and enhancement must be demonstrated from scratch, and the pre-registration names the replacement compound, not "a stabiliser". There is no generic "stabiliser arm" that survives the loss of its only candidate.

### 7.3 Decision D3: the catalog's BCL-2 entries (AGI-006 / AGI-010)

**Decision D3: Do AGI-006/010 engage Bcl-2/Bcl-xL in a validated assay?** The catalog lists AGI-003 (Mcl-1) and AGI-006 / AGI-010 (Bcl-2/Bcl-xL) as BCL-2-family-relevant entries; best on paper is AGI-010, Bcl-xL CF3 (predicted IC50 0.3 µM, goodness 0.91, ΔG −9.2 kcal/mol — **predicted (document) values, not measured**). **Assay and bar (peer review B1-G9):** engagement is tested by fluorescence-polarisation competition binding against a BH3 peptide (or an equivalent biophysical assay), with a stated Kd/IC50; engagement alone is not a pass. A D3 "yes" requires engagement **plus** the §6.7 enhancement bar (≥1.5× retrotranslocation, ≥20% EC50 shift, abolished by BCL-XL knockdown) before any catalog entry joins the stabilisation arm — engagement without stabilisation is a D3 "no" for the stabilisation arm, though the entry may still be recorded as an engager. If yes, they enter the flagship's factorial design as comparator arms. If no, the ALS DAO's compound arm closes and the flagship proceeds on AGI-183 alone (if D1 passes). **D3 is subordinate to D1:** comparator arms do not substitute for the lead arm, and D3 cannot rescue a D1 failure. Note also the direction problem: the catalog's BCL-2 entries are CRBN-inhibitor-family structures whose stated mechanism is inhibition-relevant; whether any of them *stabilises* BCL-XL is a separate question from whether it *engages* BCL-XL, and D3 as stated tests engagement only. A D3 "yes" on engagement must be followed by the §6.7 enhancement bar before any catalog entry joins the stabilisation arm.

### 7.4 The catalog as it stands

The AGI compound catalog (DOC 2, `AGI_Compound_001.pdf`, 450 pp) runs to **413 numbered entries (AGI-001–AGI-413)** built from only **24 distinct SMILES strings** (text-level dedupe; RDKit canonicalisation can only reduce this number further), with **336 HYPOTHETICAL structures and 0 verified entries** in the loop's working count. Its POC/POM claims (in-vitro IC50s, xenograft shrinkage, Western blot, co-IP) have **zero citations** in the source text and are unverified document claims. The companion variation files contain documented SMILES/duplication errors and parent-drug failures (the stated "venetoclax SMILES" is not venetoclax; real venetoclax is C45H50ClN7O7S, MW 868.44, PubChem CID 49846579). The "300 variations" / "250 compounds" counts must never be quoted — only RDKit-validated unique counts, and the RDKit pass has not landed (RDKit not installed in this environment). **Reconciliation of the three counts (peer review R7, 8 Nov 2026):** **413** = numbered catalog entries (AGI-001–AGI-413), the catalog's own numbering, which includes duplicates, variation records, and entries without a usable structure. **336** = entries the loop's working count classes as HYPOTHETICAL structures (entries presenting a proposed chemical structure). **24** = distinct SMILES strings among the entries that carry one (text-level dedupe; RDKit canonicalisation can only reduce this number further). The 77-entry difference (413 − 336) is therefore entries *not* counted as hypothetical structures — duplicate/variation records and entries without a parseable structure — but the exact composition of that remainder has not been itemised in this pass; it is recorded as open item GAP-MPS-3, not as a verified breakdown. Until that itemisation lands, quote the counts only with their definitions in this paragraph. **Consequence for MPS:** no catalog entry is used as a characterised compound anywhere in this method. Catalog entries enter MPS only as *hypothetical modelling inputs* after phase-zero labelling, never as arms, comparators, or evidence. The AGI-183 ID collision (§7.1) is carried as two separate records.

## §8 — Scoring MPS against the 5-criterion ranking rubric

The rubric is from deep dive #3 (Pattern 3): five criteria, each scored 0–2 (0 = absent, 1 = partial, 2 = met), with pre-specified evidence thresholds. MPS is scored here against it, as the task requires — and the rubric's own weaknesses, identified by the red team, are recorded alongside the score rather than hidden by it.

### 8.1 MPS scored

| # | Criterion | MPS score | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Phenotypic evidence in a human disease model | **0** | No human-cell result yet. The programme is designed, not run. (A target hypothesis / computational model scores 0 by the rubric's own thresholds.) |
| 2 | Synergy formally demonstrated | **0** | Synergy is model-*predicted* (Bliss peak +0.0305 day 13.25), not demonstrated. No combination data exist. The Bye combination, the rubric's best phenotypic case, itself scores only partial here: combination benefit demonstrated, formal Bliss synergy **not** demonstrated (§2.1, V13). |
| 3 | Complementary mechanisms | **1** | Partial. Two arms cover different failure modes on paper (BCL-XL/BAX/MODE 2 vs MCL1/BAK), but they are two nodes of the *same* pathway — less complementary than two distinct failure modes. The rubric's threshold for 2 requires distinct pathways/stressors. |
| 4 | Human safety + CNS exposure at the proposed dose | **0** | AGI-183 unverified and no stabiliser pharmacology known to exist (§7.1); AAV6 delivery unjustified vs AAV9/PHP.eB (GAP-FLAG-3); MCL1 overexpression safety unsupported (V8). |
| 5 | Stratification biomarker | **2** | Met as a *design*: pre-registered biomarker (pAkt S473 + pBad S136) with a continuous-interaction primary test, a within-line manipulation, and a second-stressor replication requirement (§3.3, §4.5). No other instance in the loop's set has a stratification design at all — but a designed biomarker is not a demonstrated one, and the score records the design, not a result. |

**Total: 3 / 10.** The rubric ranks MPS last of the loop's instances today — below PXT864 (4 of 5 criteria met) and below the Bye combination (3.5 of 5 criteria met) — and says exactly what would move it up: Phase 0 gates (D1), delivery justification (GAP-FLAG-3), and a phenotypic result. That is the rubric working as intended: it is a to-do list, not a verdict on the programme.

### 8.2 The rubric's own weaknesses (red team — recorded, not adopted away)

The red team identified two defects in the rubric itself, and MPS records them because a method scored by a flawed rubric must say so:

1. **It measures how much testing has already been done, not how good the idea is.** Criteria 1 and 2 are defined so that any designed-but-unrun programme scores ~0 by construction, while any already-tested combination scores highly — so the rubric systematically penalises novelty and rewards incumbency, which is the opposite of what a discovery programme needs. MPS's 3/10 is therefore partly a statement about its age (designed, not run), not only about its quality. The score must be read with that in mind, and MPS must not be deprioritised against an already-tested combination on the rubric score alone.
2. **It never scores interaction risk.** The rubric's own rationale states that a combination's risk is *dominated by interaction risk rather than single-drug risk* — yet none of its five criteria scores interaction risk. Safety is scored per drug, in isolation, which is precisely the quantity the rubric says does not dominate. A rubric that cannot distinguish "promising but untested" from "bad idea", and that never scores the risk it claims matters most, will mislead any experiment-selection decision it is used for.
3. **Its retrospective validation is circular** (deep dive #3, Test 1: the rubric was built from its own test cases, n = 5, unblinded). MPS does not rely on the rubric's validation claim. If the rubric is ever used to choose experiments, it should first be tested blind on a held-out set of ≥10 historical repurposing combinations with a pre-specified rank correlation — a test that does not exist today.

### 8.3 PXT864 — status correction applied to the rubric's Instance 1

Deep dive #3's Instance 1 (PXT864, baclofen + acamprosate) was scored when its later development status was recorded as "UNVERIFIED". The red team resolved it: **Pharnext stopped PXT864 development in Alzheimer's on 14 Feb 2023** ("insufficient for commercialization in the short or medium term", no licensing partner); no Phase 3 ever occurred; the Phase 2 efficacy signal rested on a historical-placebo comparison with no concurrent control (V18). PXT864 remains a valid preclinical + exploratory Phase 2a case (Chumakov et al. 2015 verified), but it is now a **stopped programme**, not a live precedent — and its rubric score, if re-scored today, would carry that status. MPS cites PXT864 only with this status.

## §9 — What must be true for it to matter; limitations; what would kill it; decision points

### 9.1 What must be true for it to matter

1. **A characterised BCL-XL stabiliser exists (binding ≠ stabilisation)** — currently not established, and no such pharmacology is known to exist at all (§7.1, D1). This is the programme's hardest gate, not a detail.
2. **MCL1 gain-of-function is safe in post-mitotic neurons over the long term** — currently unsupported by its citation; an open safety question (V8), with a known oncogenic signal in mice running the other way.
3. **The MODE 1 / MODE 2 sequestration framework holds in neurons** — assumed, not shown (established in cancer/developmental models, Llambi et al. 2011, V7).
4. **pAkt (S473) and pBad (S136; S112 secondary) are measured before treatment, in the same cells, and tested separately** — they must not be assumed interchangeable. The oncology marker is pAKT; the mechanistic variable is pBad.
5. **BH3 profiling transfers to neurons** — no paper in this pass reports a BH3-profiling EC50 in an Alzheimer's or Parkinson's model. That absence is the gap, not a finding — and the BH3-profiling search in §10.4 survived this pass, which strengthens the gap claim without proving it.
6. **Neuronal death in ALS, Alzheimer's, and Parkinson's actually proceeds through BAX/BAK-mediated MOMP in the relevant human neurons** — not merely in rodent cortical neurons under oxidative stress. The PUMA/BIM evidence is rodent and model-based; human iPSC evidence is the missing link, and the ladder (§4) is designed to supply it or to fail trying.
7. **The stratification replicates across donor lines and across diseases** — a stratification that works in one donor line or one disease is a lead, not a biomarker (GAP-ALS-13).

### 9.2 Limitations

- **No wet lab, no wet-lab partner, no funding, no timeline.** The Foundation has no wet lab. Validation requires a partner lab, a CRO, or a built facility — none identified, none costed, none approached. A dossier is not a lab.
- **Familial model, sporadic disease.** The ALS rung uses TDP-43 Q331K, a familial-mutation model. Sporadic ALS, the large majority of cases, is a generalisation the programme must earn, not assume — and the Bye-vs-Answer-ALS discrepancy (§2.6) shows sporadic-ALS iPSC models do not yet agree with each other.
- **In-vitro scope only.** Even if every test passes, the result is a validated in-vitro mechanism and a stratification design — not a treatment. Translation would need animal work, delivery, GMP manufacturing, and regulatory engagement the Foundation does not have (§11).
- **No clinical biomarker route.** For clinical relevance (far downstream), the biomarker would need to be measurable in patients (CSF, blood, or imaging). No such assay exists today, and this document does not propose one. The in-vitro stratification is a research tool first.
- **The model's numbers are unreproduced.** GAP-FLAG-1: the model code and inputs are not available. Until reproduced, the five numbers are single-source model outputs.
- **The rubric score (3/10) is partly an age effect** (§8.2): the rubric penalises unrun programmes by construction.

### 9.3 What would kill it (summary)

- **D1 fails** (no stabiliser can be characterised): the small-molecule arm dies; the method continues on the gene-therapy arm alone, re-scoped and re-pre-registered as a single-arm stratification test.
- **F1 strengthened fires** (§6.1): the biomarker hypothesis is rejected as stated.
- **F3 repeats across two runs:** the assay-transfer claim (H-MPS-3) dies; the ladder stops until the assay is fixed.
- **Model-transfer fails:** the result is model-specific; no cross-model claim is made.
- **AD and PD extensions both fail:** H-MPS-2 dies; the method is an ALS-only method, if the ALS rung passed.
- **No capsid clears the absolute bar** (§4.7): the gene-therapy arm stops; no "best of a bad lot" advance.
- **The strongest objection, stated plainly:** there is no drug, no delivery, and a payload with a known oncogenic signal in mice. If Phase 0 cannot solve the first of those, the programme has nothing to test — and the correct outcome is to report that, not to run a factorial around an uncharacterised compound.

### 9.4 Decision points

- **D1:** Does AGI-183 exist and engage BCL-XL? No → the programme redesigns around a characterised BCL-XL modulator or stops the small-molecule arm. Yes → Phase 0 gates open. **Status: UNVERIFIED; the bar is higher than loop 1 stated, because no stabiliser pharmacology is known to exist at all (§7.1).**
- **D3:** Do AGI-006/010 engage Bcl-2/Bcl-xL in a validated assay? ### 10.2 What was found: the Ovadia patent family

**Ovadia, Cohen, Herkel, Margalit & Pevsner-Fischer — "Use of Akt phosphorylation as a biomarker for prognosing neurodegenerative diseases and treating same."** Patent family, priority **23 May 2011**:

- WO2012160563A2, filed 23 May 2012, published 29 Nov 2012.
- **EP 3263127 B1, granted 21 Aug 2019.**
- **US 10,288,622 B2, granted 14 May 2019, status Active** (assignees Yeda Research and Development Co. Ltd. / Universitätsklinikum Hamburg-Eppendorf).
- CA2836503A1 published 2012-11-29; CA2836503C granted 2020-09-22.
- Claims (quoted from the patent text): *"a method for prognosticating the progression of a neurodegenerative disease in a subject, comprising: (a) assessing the value of at least one marker selected from: pAkt and pAkt:tAkt ratio, in a bodily sample derived from the subject"*; *"a level of pAkt or pAkt:tAkt ratio significantly below a control value indicates a rapid disease"*; and a method *"for assessing responsiveness to treatment"* with the peptide of the invention using pAkt/pAkt:tAkt. The specification states: *"Akt phosphorylation and pAkt:tAkt ratio distinguish between fast progression to low progression of ALS"* and *"high levels of pAkt and high levels of pAkt:tAkt ratio (e.g. above 1) correlate with responsiveness to treatment of ALS."* Supporting data in the patent: pAkt correlates with disease state in SOD1 mice (P = 0.01); the Stressin-1 peptide (LPPLPYP, SEQ ID NO: 1) raises pAkt.
- The descriptive foundation is peer-reviewed and old: Leger et al., *FASEB J* 2006;20(3):583–585 (lower phosphorylated Akt protein in ALS patient skeletal muscle vs controls; PMID 16507768) and Dewil et al., *Neuropathol Appl Neurobiol* 2007;33(5):499–509 (Akt pathway dysfunction in motor neurons of sporadic and familial ALS patients). Those papers describe Akt dysfunction; they do not propose a biomarker.

**Direction:** the patent's proposal runs in the **opposite direction** to MPS — high pAkt → responsiveness to an Akt-*raising* peptide — vs MPS's low pAkt/pBad → benefit from BCL-XL stabilisation. Different intervention, opposite direction of the prediction. The patent does not propose the flagship's specific mechanism.

**Internal tension (found in this pass):** the patent's description elsewhere states the peptides' therapeutic effect "would be significantly higher in subjects having low levels of pAkt". The patent text as returned therefore contains both directions in different sections. MPS records the tension and does not rely on the patent for its direction; MPS's direction comes from the Datta mechanism (§1.2) and is tested by the interaction.

MPS never uses that phrasing.

- **No peer-reviewed journal article** by Ovadia et al. proposing pAkt as an ALS biomarker was found across the red team's five search phrasings. The literal sentence "no *peer-reviewed study* proposes…" was therefore not directly falsified by a journal article.

## §11 — Translational boundary, safety, and the Cancer DAO crossover

### 11.1 Translational steps (context only — no clinical claim, no timeline)

1. **Delivery:** the programme's neuron-targeting logic is AAV6 with the neuron-specific human synapsin (hSyn) promoter, chosen to avoid the systemic exposure that caused cardiotoxicity with small-molecule MCL1 inhibitors (V1, V5). Flagged openly: AAV6 is not the standard CNS serotype (AAV9 and engineered capsids such as PHP.eB are the usual CNS choices); the source document does not justify AAV6 over AAV9, and the dossier records this as an open design question (GAP-FLAG-3), not a settled choice. Any in-vivo route (intrathecal, intracerebroventricular, intraparenchymal) is undecided and would require its own biodistribution and safety programme. The capsid bake-off (§4.7) with its absolute bar and BBB-transwell gate is the next step, not a formality.
2. **Stabilisation vs inhibition:** the toxicity record the programme is designed around (cardiotoxicity for MCL1 inhibition, thrombocytopenia for BCL-XL inhibition) belongs to inhibitors. A stabiliser has no clinical toxicity record at all — neither reassuring nor alarming. First-in-human considerations (if ever reached) would start from zero, not from the inhibitor record. And a stabiliser has no *preclinical* toxicity record either, because no stabiliser is known to exist (§7.1).
3. **AAV manufacturing / regulatory path (context only):** AAV gene therapy requires GMP vector manufacturing, potency assays, biodistribution and shedding studies, long-term expression and insertional-safety assessment, and regulatory engagement (FDA/EMA) — none of which the Foundation has, and none of which is costed or scheduled here. The Foundation's current role is research coordination and dossier-building, not drug development. Do not present any of this as underway. Cost anchor: 4D Molecular Therapeutics' FY2022 R&D burn was ~$80M/yr at industry scale — ground all cost/runway claims against real gene-therapy economics, never invented figures.
4. **Sequencing:** compound characterisation (Phase 0) → in-vitro validation (§4) → independent replication in a second lab → animal-model work (not designed here) → only then any translational conversation. Each step is gated on the previous one passing; there is no compressed timeline. The first step is currently blocked on D1.

### 11.2 Safety summary

- MCL1 overexpression safety in post-mitotic neurons is an open question (V8), with a mouse lymphomagenesis signal running the other way. Neuron-restricted expression is a mitigation, not a proof. The Phase 3 safety arm (§4.7) is mandatory, not optional.
- A BCL-XL stabiliser has no safety record of any kind. Its risks cannot be inferred from the inhibitor record in either direction: thrombocytopenia is a toxicity of *inhibition*, and stabilisation could in principle carry opposite risks (e.g. apoptosis resistance in the wrong cells) that no existing data address. MPS does not speculate on what those risks would be; it states that they are unknown.
- The PI3K-inhibitor manipulation arm (§4.2, §4.4) is an in-vitro mechanistic tool, not a therapeutic proposal. Lowering Akt in a patient would be expected to be harmful; the arm exists to test the chain in a dish, and nothing in this document proposes it as an intervention.

### 11.3 Cancer DAO crossover — the direction is INVERTED

See §1.5 for the full table. The bridge is shared biology and shared methodology — **not the same drug direction**. The Cancer DAO's own hypothesis (H-CAN1, cross-cohort signature portability) is a separate programme; the two share the BCL-2 knowledge base and the BH3-profiling tooling, and nothing else. The DacomiLertib entry (a hypothetical, unverified, ChatGPT-generated BCL-XL *inhibitor*, entry #201, SMILES invalid as written, provisional label `invalid`) belongs to the cancer (inhibition) side of this bridge only — never to the MPS stabilisation programme — and it is not dacomitinib (the real FDA-approved EGFR inhibitor, C24H25ClFN5O2, MW 469.94).

## Appendix C — Assay definitions and acceptance criteria

- **BH3 profiling:** mitochondria or permeabilised cells exposed to a BH3-peptide titration series; MOMP read as cytochrome c release or JC-1 membrane-potential loss; EC50 fitted per timepoint per culture. Acceptance: the BIM EC50 moves under stress in the pilot (dynamic range demonstrated); the BCL-XL inhibitor control moves EC50 in the opposite direction to the stabiliser prediction in every run; peptide-panel mapping (NOXA → MCL-1, BAD → BCL-XL) is reported per rung, not assumed. **Numeric acceptance criteria (peer review B1-G6, pilot-confirmed):** JC-1 dynamic range ≥2-fold signal change vs the FCCP depolarisation control; coefficient of variation ≤20% across technical replicates; positive-control (BCL-XL inhibitor) EC50 shift ≥20%; mitochondrial yield and purity reported per run, with the pilot's values as the locked reference range. A pilot that cannot meet these criteria fails the BH3 transfer gate — the assay is fixed before any confirmatory run, not tuned around the data.
- **Phospho assays (pAkt S473, pBad S136, pBad S112, total Akt, total Bad):** phospho-flow or quantitative Western / immunofluorescence, with phosphatase controls. Acceptance: the assay separates low from high with a stated coefficient of variation in the pilot; baseline measurement precedes treatment in the same cells; failed-QC cultures are excluded before randomisation by a pre-registered rule.
- **FLIP (BAX retrotranslocation):** fluorescence loss in photobleaching per Edlich et al. 2011. Acceptance: wild-type BAX retrotranslocates; tethered-BAX control (if used) does not; BCL-XL overexpression increases the rate (positive control for the assay itself).
- **Survival:** longitudinal live-cell imaging of individual neurons; time-to-event analysis; caspase activation and membrane-potential loss as secondary readouts. Acceptance: the stressor produces a survival deficit vs the no-stress arm in the pilot; the unit of replication is the independent differentiation.
- **Neurite integrity (AD/PD rungs):** total neurite length / branching per neuron, longitudinally tracked; LD50-style neurite-retraction time where the model supports it (cf. Bye et al. 2026, 7–16-donor screen; combination validation on 15 donors). Acceptance: pre-registered as primary (AD/PD) or secondary (ALS) per rung; not reordered after data collection.
- **Vector QC:** MCL1 fold-change (qPCR/Western), hSyn specificity (co-stain), empty-vector toxicity check at the chosen MOI. Acceptance: stated in Gate 0c (§7.2).
