## 1. WAVE / Bioacoustics

### 1.1 Verified findings

**BirdNET (Cornell K. Lisa Yang Center for Conservation Bioacoustics + Chemnitz University of Technology)** — open-source bird/animal sound identification, trained on curated Xeno-canto and Macaulay Library recordings. Free apps; BirdNET-Analyzer tool for batch analysis. This is WAVE's live predictor.
- v2.4 production, March 2025: **6,522 bird species** (+ smaller mammal/anuran sets). Sources: https://birdnet.cornell.edu/about/ (scraped 7 Nov 2026); Zenodo model release (Mar 2025); Funosas et al., *Ecological Indicators* 182 (2026) 114550.
- V3.0 generation ("BirdNET+"): Developer Preview 3.1, June 2026, "11K Species"; repo `birdnet-team/birdnet-V3.0-dev` (created 2025-11-10; latest commit 16 June 2026); model DOI 10.5281/zenodo.20703646. Explicitly a developer preview: "Models, labels, and code will change before final release."
- BirdNET Live app (Sept 2026): **8,927 birds, 268 mammals, 254 insects, 340 amphibians**, real time, no internet/cellular, free on Android/iOS, UI in 12 languages. Source: https://www.birds.cornell.edu/ccb/soundbytes-september-26/
- Licence: BirdNET-Analyzer source code **MIT**; models **CC BY-NC-SA 4.0** (non-commercial; educational/research use freely permitted; commercial use needs a Cornell licence; custom models trained with it inherit the same terms). No hosted API — downloadable models/tools; free apps. Contact: ccb-birdnet@cornell.edu. Source: https://github.com/birdnet-team/birdnet-analyzer/blob/HEAD/README.md

**Google Perch (Google DeepMind / Google Research)** — bioacoustics foundation model; embedding + classifier for transfer learning ("agile modeling"). Basis for SurfPerch (coral reefs) and Google's whale classifiers. **NOT connected to WAVE** — candidate/comparison model only.
- Perch 2.0, released **7 August 2025**: **14,795 classes (14,597 species + 198 general sound-event classes, FSD50K)**; 1,536-dimensional embeddings; ~12M-parameter EfficientNet-B3-class model. Training data (paper Table 1; downloads Mar–Apr 2025): **1,542,778 recordings** — Xeno-canto 896,255; iNaturalist 571,698; Tierstimmenarchiv 33,859; FSD50K 40,966. Benchmarks: BirdSet AUROC ~0.907–0.908; state-of-the-art on BEANS tasks (paper: arXiv:2508.04665, revised Jan 2026). Limitation stated in the paper: almost no marine hydrophone training data (only a few dozen cetacean recordings).
- Perch 1.0 (2023): EfficientNet-B1 trained on the full Xeno-canto corpus downloaded July 2022; >250,000 downloads (per DeepMind).
- Licence/access: code **Apache-2.0** (`google-research/perch`); models free via **Kaggle Models** (`google/bird-vocalization-classifier`, variant `perch_v2`; SurfPerch at `google/surfperch`). No hosted API; self-hosted download.
- Sources: https://github.com/google-research/perch (scraped 7 Nov 2026); https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.04665; https://www.kaggle.com/models/google/bird-vocalization-classifier

**NatureLM-audio (Earth Species Project)** — audio-language foundation model: audio encoder linked to a large language model (built on LLaMA 3.1); recordings queryable in plain English. Species, call-type and life-stage classification, detection, captioning, counting, including zero-shot generalisation. **A research model, not a translation device** — ESP and independent commentators stress it finds patterns, not confirmed meanings. **NOT connected to WAVE.**
- Paper: Robinson et al., arXiv:2411.07186, **November 2024**.
- Training dataset: **>26 million (audio, text) pairs; >2 million audio files; 22,100 hours; 44 bioacoustic tasks**; sources include Xeno-canto, iNaturalist, Watkins, Animal Sound Archive, plus human speech/music/environmental sound. Hugging Face `EarthSpeciesProject/NatureLM-audio-training` (**26.4M rows**, verified by scrape 7 Nov 2026).
- NatureLM-audio 2.0 "to be released soon" per ESP blog, 30 Sept 2026; model-merging work (NeurIPS 2025 workshop) improved unseen-family F1 by >200% relative and is folded into 2.0. **alp-data** (July 2026): ESP's shared data layer uniting **35+ ML-ready bioacoustic datasets**.
- Licence: training data is composite — **per-sample licences** (e.g. CC-BY-NC, CC0); the dataset as a whole has no single licence. Model weights licence **not verified**.
- Sources: https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.07186; https://huggingface.co/datasets/EarthSpeciesProject/NatureLM-audio-training (scraped 7 Nov 2026); https://earthspecies.org/2026/09/30/what-if-a-model-knows-more-than-it-can-say/; https://www.earthspecies.org/2026/07/22/introducing-alp-data-a-shared-data-layer-for-animal-language-processing/

**Xeno-canto** — community archive of wildlife sounds (birds, grasshoppers, bats, frogs, land mammals), run by the Xeno-canto Foundation (Willem-Pier Vellinga, Bob Planqué, Sander Pieterse). Principal training source for BirdNET, Merlin, Perch, NatureLM-audio. Public archive, **not owned by WAVE**.
- **1,111,111 recordings** — homepage milestone, scraped 7 Nov 2026; **~13,400 species**; >13,000 recordists; used in >6,000 publications (Google Scholar, 2026). See Figure Alignment — this supersedes ~1,040,000.
- Licence: individual Creative Commons licences per recording (most commonly CC BY-NC-SA). Free open API: https://xeno-canto.org/explore/api (also `xenopy` Python wrapper). New in 2026: "Annota-JSON" annotations, exportable via API; Spectrolipi integration; bot pressure noted late 2026.
- Sources: https://xeno-canto.org/ (scraped 7 Nov 2026); https://www.few.vu.nl/~rplanque/XC/index.html

**Macaulay Library (Cornell Lab)** — world's largest and oldest scientific archive of wildlife media; founded 1929 (first recordings 18 May 1929, Ithaca); integrated with eBird; other principal training source for BirdNET. Public archive, **not owned by WAVE**.
- **3.2 million+ audio recordings; 84 million+ photographs; 300,000+ videos; covering 96% of the world's bird species** (Wikipedia, citing Cornell Lab sources; scraped 7 Nov 2026). Consistent with Cornell's job posting (>77M images, 3M sound recordings, 350k videos, >80k contributors) and Cornell Alumni (Mar 2025: >2.7M audio, 330k videos, 72M images; >500,000 audio recordings uploaded in 2024 alone). **500,000th video** reached 12 July 2026.
- Licence/access: free to browse/listen via https://www.macaulaylibrary.org/ and eBird; media remain contributors' copyright. **Exact current reuse/download terms were not verified** — do not assert a licence; link to the library's terms.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macaulay_Library (scraped 7 Nov 2026); https://alumni.cornell.edu/cornellians/macaulay-library/ (Mar 2025)

**Watkins Marine Mammal Sound Database (WHOI)** — William A. Watkins Marine Mammal Sound Database, Woods Hole Oceanographic Institution; historic reference collection built over 40+ years; spans seven decades (1940s–2000s), including the first recordings of 51 marine mammal species. Public resource, **not owned by WAVE**.
- **~2,000 unique recordings of 60+ marine mammal species, with >15,000 annotated digital sound clips** (Sayigh et al. 2016, *Proc. Meetings on Acoustics* 27:040013). "Best of" subset: **1,694 high-quality cuts, 32 species**. Physical collection at the New Bedford Whaling Museum: ~18,000 calls from 70+ species, 2,300 reel-to-reel tapes.
- Access caveat: official site (`cis.whoi.edu/science/B/whalesounds/`, mirror `whoicf2.whoi.edu`) was **offline as of 19 Sept 2026** (both hosts redirect to `maintenance.whoi.edu`). Working fallbacks verified in a Sept 2026 catalogue: **BEANS GCS zip** (`storage.googleapis.com/ml-bioacoustics-datasets/watkins.zip`, 882 MB, WAV + annotations.csv, 31 classes) and Hugging Face mirrors (`DBD-research-group/beans_watkins`; `ivangtorre/watkins-marine-mammal-full-cuts`, 15,200 recordings, 54 species per card) and an Internet Archive full-cuts copy. The Kaggle mirror (`shreyj1729/best-of-watkins-marine-mammal-sound-database`) **returns 404 — do not link it**.
- Licence: free download, no registration, under Watkins site terms (when live); BEANS/HF mirrors inherit Watkins terms (non-commercial in the underlying collection). No per-clip CC licence verified.
- Sources: https://www.researchgate.net/publication/315366631_The_Watkins_Marine_Mammal_Sound_Database_An_online_freely_accessible_resource; https://github.com/mallhw/keiko-hackmit/blob/HEAD/training/dataset/catalog/survey_registries_endpoints.md (offline status verified 2026-09-19)

**iNaturalist Sounds** — audio recordings attached as evidence to observations on iNaturalist (California Academy of Sciences / National Geographic Society).
- Solid sourced figure: **571,698 iNaturalist recordings** were in Perch 2.0's training data (research-grade observations via GBIF, downloaded March 2025). **A current platform-wide total of sounds was not verified** — iNaturalist publishes observation totals, not a sounds total, in the sources checked.
- Licence: default for new accounts **CC BY-NC** for all three content types (observation data, images, audio); users may choose CC0, CC BY, CC BY-NC-SA, CC BY-NC-ND, CC BY-ND, CC BY-SA, or all rights reserved — licences can differ within a single observation. Only CC-licensed media are shared with GBIF. Open-licensed media bulk-accessible via **iNaturalist Open Data on AWS** (`inaturalist-open-data`). As of July 2026, ~23% of observations are not under an open observation licence and therefore cannot be redistributed via GBIF (Callaghan et al., *BioScience* 2026, doi:10.1093/biosci/biag135).
- Sources: https://academic.oup.com/bioscience/advance-article/doi/10.1093/biosci/biag135/8820383; https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.04665; https://www.inaturalist.org/pages/api+reference

**Earth Species Library** — ESP's open repository of ML-ready, multi-modal (primarily acoustic) datasets, hosted on GitHub. Distinct from the NatureLM-audio training set, though related. Public resource, **not owned by WAVE**.
- Contents: "A diverse collection of multi-modal (primarily acoustic) datasets … available to the public, easily downloadable, and preprocessed for machine learning." Examples: **Bird Song** — 686 recordings, 87 labels (with PyTorch dataloaders); **Egyptian Fruit Bat** — 300,000 unique vocalizations, 90k labeled annotations. Datasets archived on the Internet Archive. Hugging Face org `EarthSpeciesProject` hosts derived datasets.
- Note: the ESP website Library page (earthspecies.org/what-we-do/library) returned **"Page not found"** when scraped 7 Nov 2026 — the ESP site appears to be migrating (staging domain `earthspeciesproject.wpcomstaging.com`). Use the GitHub repo as the live home, not the old website URL.
- Sources: https://github.com/earthspecies/library; https://raw.githubusercontent.com/earthspecies/library/main/README.md

**Project CETI** — Cetacean Translation Initiative; nonprofit (founded 2020, based in Dominica, 8 institutions, ~50 scientists) applying ML, robotics, linguistics, and underwater acoustics to sperm whale communication. Studies codas (rhythmic click patterns), not bird song. **No translation has been achieved** — CETI's researchers are explicit.
- May 2024: "sperm whale phonetic alphabet" — **8,719 codas from ~60 individual whales**; **156 distinct codas** with combinatorial structure (*Nature Communications* 15:3393, doi:10.1038/s41467-024-47221-8).
- Nov 2025: vowel- and diphthong-like spectral patterns in codas (Beguš et al., UC Berkeley / CETI). Apr 2026: "The phonology of sperm whale coda vowels" (*Proc. R. Soc. B* 293(2069):20252994). Mar 2026: first complete drone + hydrophone record of a sperm whale birth (Unit A, Dominica), published in *Science* and *Scientific Reports* (6+ hours of underwater audio + aerial video). Dec 2025: open-source bio-logger (Harvard SEAS) — high-fidelity multi-channel audio + behaviour/environment sensors, hardware and software open-source (Vogt et al., *PLOS ONE*, doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0337093). Autonomous underwater glider for real-time tracking developed May 2026. "Whale Tales" analysis software "soon" available (per CETI site, scraped 7 Nov 2026).
- CETI is a member of the **AWS Open Data Program** and partners with Google Research and Microsoft Research.
- What data is public: papers, open-source bio-logger designs/code, selected footage/audio released with publications. **No verified public bulk download of the coda corpus was found** — the AWS Open Data dataset was not located/confirmed. Do not claim the whale recordings are downloadable.
- Sources: https://www.projectceti.org/research/index (scraped 7 Nov 2026); https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-47221-8

**Animal Sound Archive (Tierstimmenarchiv, Berlin)** — Museum für Naturkunde Berlin (Leibniz Institute), founded **1951** by Günter Tembrock. One of the world's oldest and third-largest bioacoustic archives; birds, mammals, fish, amphibians, reptiles, insects. Public archive, **not owned by WAVE**.
- **~120,000–130,000 recordings**: GBIF dataset description says "around 130 000 records of animal voices"; Museum's 75th-anniversary report (Oct 2026) says "**more than 120,000 recordings** … from approximately **1,800 bird species and 580 mammal species**", plus amphibians, reptiles, fish, insects. 75th anniversary colloquium 7 Nov 2026. Perch 2.0 used 33,859 Tierstimmenarchiv recordings (Apr 2025).
- Access: search portal at `suche.tierstimmenarchiv.de`; dataset via GBIF (doi:10.15468/0bpalr). Older sources: representative sections of >10,000 recordings listenable online; complete recordings "can be requested for scientific purposes free of charge." A 2025 review table lists access as "**Contact required**" — conflicts with "open access", so treat bulk access as gated/unclear. **The Museum's research page URL returned 404 when scraped 7 Nov 2026** (site reorganisation); use the search portal and GBIF record instead.
- Licence: **not verified** — do not assert one.
- Sources: https://www.gbif.org/dataset/b7ec1bf8-819b-11e2-bad2-00145eb45e9a; https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.04665

**GBIF** — Global Biodiversity Information Facility; intergovernmental open-data infrastructure (founded 2001) aggregating species occurrence records via Darwin Core. An **index/aggregator, not a sound archive itself** — but where the open-licensed media of iNaturalist, Xeno-canto, Macaulay Library, and the Animal Sound Archive become jointly searchable/downloadable.
- **3.8 billion+ occurrence records** as of August 2026 (Miller et al., "The Global Biodiversity Information Facility at 25", *Biodiversity Data Journal* 14:e208528, Oct 2026; 70 participating countries, data providers in 120+ countries, 2,700 institutions). Under 5% of records are machine observations (camera traps, acoustic monitors). **Current number of audio-enabled (sound) records was not verified** — last sourced figure is from 2017 (Xeno-canto's addition of 170,041 audio records more than doubled a base of ~100,000 sound records). Nine years stale; do not quote as current.
- Licence/access: free, open access; data under CC0 / CC BY / CC BY-NC as set by publishers; occurrence and species APIs; Darwin Core Archives.
- Sources: https://doi.org/10.3897/BDJ.14.e208528; https://www.gbif.org/analytics/global

**Other verified open bioacoustics datasets / models**
- **BEANS** (Benchmark of Animal Sounds) — ESP, ICASSP 2023 (Hagiwara et al.). 12 datasets across birds, land/marine mammals, amphibians, insects. Code: https://github.com/earthspecies/beans ; mirrors on Hugging Face (`DBD-research-group/beans_*`). **BEANS-Zero** (Robinson et al. 2025) is the zero-shot benchmark for NatureLM-audio.
- **BirdSet** — ~520,000 Xeno-canto recordings for training, 400+ hours of annotated passive recordings for testing (Rauch et al. 2025). https://huggingface.co/datasets/DBD-research-group/BirdSet
- **SurfPerch** — Google's reef-adapted Perch model, Kaggle `google/surfperch`, paper arXiv:2404.16436; trained on ReefSet.
- **Google whale models** — multispecies whale classifier (8 species / 12 classes) and humpback whale model, both on Kaggle Models (`google/multispecies-whale`, `google/humpback-whale`); training data from PIFSC, MBARI, DFO, NEFSC.
- **DolphinGemma** — Google DeepMind + Wild Dolphin Project + Georgia Tech; ~400M-parameter audio language model trained on 40 years of Atlantic spotted dolphin recordings (Bahamas), SoundStream tokeniser, runs on Pixel phones. Announced April 2025; Google said it would be openly released. **Status unresolved: DeepMind's own page (accessed Nov 2026) says "currently in development. On release, it will be openly available"; Wikipedia likewise notes "no model or data have been publicly released"; some secondary outlets claim a summer-2025 release. Do not claim it is released or available.**
- **AVES / AVES2 (ESP)** — bioacoustic encoder models, trained on Xeno-canto + iNaturalist + Watkins + AudioSet; Hugging Face (`EarthSpeciesProject/esp-aves2-*`); paper arXiv:2508.11845. BEANS classification probe accuracy 0.788 (reported on model card).
- **FSD50K** — 40,966 general audio clips (Fonseca et al. 2022); the non-species portion of Perch 2.0 training.
- **AnuraSet** — anuran (frog/toad) call dataset (Cañas et al. 2023).
- **NOAA NCEI passive-bioacoustic cloud bucket** (`noaa-passive-bioacoustic` on Google Cloud) — richest anonymous marine endpoint per a Sept 2026 verified catalogue: DCLDE 2011/2013/2015/2018/2022, NEFSC, PIFSC, SanctSound, ADEON, JASCO, etc., listable with no credentials. Worth a follow-up pass for WAVE's marine side.
- **Australian Acoustic Observatory** — searchable continental-scale passive acoustic archive (cited as a Perch 2.0 data source; not independently verified this pass).

### 2.1 Verified findings

**Molecule — the IP-tokenisation layer.** Molecule is the infrastructure layer for decentralised science (DeSci). Core innovation: attaching legal IP rights to an NFT — an **IP-NFT** — so a research asset can be co-owned and governed by a community. It is the tokenisation engine behind the Bio Protocol / BioDAO ecosystem.
- **Bio Protocol (bio.xyz, BIO token)** is the meta-protocol incubated by Molecule and stewarded by the Bio.xyz Association. Communities form **BioDAOs** (one therapeutic area each), tokenise IP as **IP-Tokens (IPTs)**, and more recently launch AI research agents (**BioAgents**) with their own tokens. BIO is deployed on Ethereum, Base, BNB Chain and Solana.
- **IP-Tokens:** fractional ERC-20 tokens against an IP-NFT (later, against a Lab). The Bio Protocol documentation is explicit that an IPT carries **governance and information rights but no guaranteed financial return**; whether IPT holders are paid on commercialisation is left to the IP owner. Do not present IPTs as equity, revenue shares, or investment returns.
- Current status (Oct–Nov 2026): Molecule's first-generation IP-NFT stack is now legacy — its contract index marks the Ethereum/Sepolia IP-NFT sections **"UNMAINTAINED"** and lists the live deployment as **"Molecule Labs core (v0.1.0)" on Base**.
- **V3 — the Lab:** the primitive is now a **Lab**: "an NFT (ERC-721) that is permanently bound to its own smart contract wallet (ERC-6551)" with account abstraction (ERC-4337), installable modules (ERC-7579) and an attestation registry (ERC-7484). The research project is an account holding its own treasury, data anchor and licences; transferring the Lab NFT hands over the whole thing.
- **V3 traction (on-chain, as reported 12 Oct 2026):** LabNFT contract reported **151 Labs**, the first minted **8 July 2026**. The OclTokenizer had issued **five IP Tokens**, the first on **18 September 2026**, none since 6 October 2026 (as of the 12 Oct 2026 check). Early, thin adoption — do not describe V3 as widely deployed.
- IP-NFT contracts reached **V2 in 2024**; in 2025 Molecule extended toward bridging tokenised IP into the real-world-asset (RWA) market. Molecule founder/CEO Paul Kohlhaas also launched **Pump.science**, a Solana tokenised-experiment launchpad funding longevity compound trials through freely tradeable tokens.
- Bio Protocol V2 replaced the earlier curation/auction launchpad model with a fixed-price **Ignition Sale** plus post-launch liquidity mechanisms. The launchpad contracts themselves are **not public**; six published audit reports are the only technical description. The BioDAO building blocks (governance token, vesting, Merkle airdrop — three Solidity projects — plus one Solana governance program) **are** open source under the bio-xyz organisation.
- BIO token: initial supply **3,320,000,000**, described as uncapped (issuing more would require deploying a new token contract). Four-token economy: BIO, vBIO, veBIO, BioXP. Governance is still transitioning from BIO/vBIO voting to veBIO.
- What is open vs licensed/gated: **Open** — Molecule IP-NFT V2 contracts (open source); BioDAO token/vesting/airdrop contracts (open source, Foundry projects); Bio Protocol documentation. **Gated / not open** — the V2 launchpad contracts (closed source, audit reports only); IP itself — an IP-NFT/Lab represents legal IP rights, which are owned, licensed and governed, not public-domain data. IPTs give governance/information rights, not a right to the underlying data or a guaranteed payout. An IPT is not a licence to use the IP commercially; commercialisation terms sit with the IP owner / Lab.

**Genomic data-sharing initiatives.**
- **GA4GH (Global Alliance for Genomics and Health):** global standards-setting organisation for genomics, formed **2013**, uniting **500+** leading institutions. Develops technical standards, policy frameworks and open-source tools for voluntary, secure genomic data sharing. Open standards: **Data Use Ontology (DUO)**, implemented by the Broad and Sanger Institutes and used in annotation of **over 200,000 datasets worldwide** (Cell Genomics, Nov 2021, still cited 2026); **GA4GH Passport** standard for data authorisation/access; a data-sharing lexicon (including "data visiting" and "federated data analysis") developed by a study group formed Sep 2023, draft Apr 2024, **finalised Jul 2025** (Human Genomics 2025, 19:80). Model: "data visiting" — shared data is analysed inside the provider's computing environment rather than downloaded. GA4GH does not hold genomic data itself.
- **All of Us Research Program (NIH, USA):** NIH programme to gather data from **1 million+ people** in the US, prioritising historically underrepresented populations. Current status (May–Jun 2026, NIH Council of Councils update, 14 May 2026): **880,000+ participants** from all 50 states; **22,580+ registered researchers** from **1,360+ organisations**; **1,350+ publications** (>500 in 2025); **14.7 million biosample aliquots**; **499,000+ EHRs**; 2.9M survey responses; DNA results returned to **733,000+ individuals / 277,000+ participants** (deck's own phrasing is ambiguous on the DNA-results count; cite as "733,000+ individual DNA results returned"). Goal: enrol 1 million participants by end of 2026 and expand data to include whole genomes for 1 million participants. As of Dec 2025: **872,000+ consented, 613,000+ providing biospecimens**. Genomes: whole-genome sequencing data released for **over 245,000 participants** (per a 2025 review cited by Capgemini, 2026); a Jun 2026 data release (Researcher Workbench V8, 2025) is described as the world's largest genomic + EHR dataset. An Oct 2026 Exeter study analysed **nearly 1 million whole genomes** from UK Biobank + All of Us combined (Genome Medicine, 22 Oct 2026). Access: data via the **Researcher Workbench** (cloud); aggregate data publicly searchable via the Data Browser. **No access fee, but user-borne computational costs** (Genomics, Proteomics & Bioinformatics, Nov 2025, Table 1). Individual-level genomic data is controlled access, not open download. Consent is broad, with return of DNA results to participants.
  - Source: https://dpcpsi.nih.gov/sites/dpcpsi/files/2026-05/1045AM-May-15-All-of-Us-Update-508.pdf (NIH Council of Councils, 14 May 2026)
- **UK Biobank:** large-scale biomedical database of **~500,000 volunteers** (UK), with genotyping, imaging, EHR linkage and whole-genome sequencing. Whole-genome sequencing of **490,640 participants** reported (Nature, 2025). Data available to eligible researchers worldwide (academic, charity, government, commercial) for health-related research in the public interest. Access terms (**verified by Firecrawl scrape of the official access page, 7 Nov 2026, success=true**): eligibility requires a track record of legitimate health-related research, affiliation with a recognised research organisation, and operation from a country complying with international regulations and not subject to UK/US/EU sanctions. Background checks on every applicant. Proposals raising concerns go to an expert **Access Committee**, consulting the Ethics Advisory Committee and Participant Advisory Group. **Since Jan 2025, applications from insurance companies for direct access to de-identified data are no longer approved** (they may collaborate with / fund approved researchers, or use published findings). Researchers must sign a legal agreement (Material Transfer Agreement); they are **prohibited from trying to re-identify participants and from sharing data with non-collaborators**. Since **2024, access is by default via the UK Biobank Research Analysis Platform (UKB-RAP)**, a restricted cloud platform — downloads are no longer the default — and researchers agree to delete data when the project ends. Tiered access fees apply (per the Nov 2025 data-sharing review). Summary/aggregate findings are public; individual-level genomic + phenotypic data are **controlled access, not open**.
  - Source: https://www.ukbiobank.ac.uk/about-us/how-we-work/access-to-uk-biobank-data/ (official; Firecrawl-scraped 7 Nov 2026, success=true)
- **openSNP — CLOSED (important).** Open-source site (founded 2011 by Bastian Greshake) where users uploaded consumer genotype files (23andMe, Ancestry, FamilyTreeDNA, deCODEme) under **CC0** (public domain), code on GitHub under MIT. ~13,000 people shared data; the site warned users there was zero privacy/anonymity. **Dissolved / shut down.** Announced 31 Mar 2025; **all user data deleted 10 Apr 2025** (announced date was 30 Apr 2025; the farewell page says 10 Apr). Cause cited: the **23andMe Chapter 11 bankruptcy (23 Mar 2025)** and public fears of DNA misuse; the founder judged that "the risk/benefit calculus of providing free & open access to individual genetic data in 2025 is very different compared to 14 years ago" and that the transformative use of consumer genetic data had turned out to be law-enforcement genealogy rather than biomedical research. **Do not cite openSNP as a live open-genomics resource.** Its archived data still appears in research (e.g. a May 2026 arXiv benchmarking paper across 80 openSNP phenotypes, using pre-shutdown data). Lesson for Genomic DAO / S27 (proposed): the openSNP closure is the clearest recent evidence that fully open, CC0 individual-level genomic data is now regarded by its own stewards as unsafe to hold. Any proposed genomic data-sharing model should be framed as controlled / federated / data-visiting (GA4GH model), with consent and re-identification protections — not as open download.
  - Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenSNP and http://rip.so/opensnp.html
- **"Open Genes" — not verified.** No distinct, live resource called "Open Genes" was verified as an open genomics dataset in this run. There is a longevity-genetics database sometimes called Open Genes / OpenGenes (human ageing genetics), but its current status, licence and contents were **not verified** — do not cite it.

**Drug-compound resources — the open stack for AGI Compounds (proposed).**
- **ChEMBL (EMBL-EBI):** manually curated database of bioactive, drug-like molecules; the standard curated bioactivity resource for drug discovery. **ChEMBL 37, prepared 1 May 2026** (release notes, EBI FTP): **2,921,148 compounds** (2,897,819 with mol files), **3,824,604 compound records** (non-unique), **24,527,044 activities**, **1,970,438 assays**, **101,100 documents**. (ChEMBL 36, Sep 2025: 2,878,000 compounds, 24,270,000 activities — for the growth trend.) Licence/access: **CC BY-SA 3.0**; ChEMBL asks that ChEMBL IDs and the release number be preserved on reuse. Free REST API (JSON/XML), bulk download via FTP (TSV/SDF/SQL). Funded by Wellcome Trust, EMBL member states, Open Targets, EU IMI and EU Framework 7. ChEMBL incorporates data from sources including BindingDB and SureChEMBL patent bioactivity data and PubChem BioAssays — downstream reuse must respect the original sources where they differ.
  - Source: https://ftp.ebi.ac.uk/pub/databases/chembl/ChEMBLdb/releases/chembl_37/chembl_37_release_notes.txt
- **PubChem (NCBI / NLM / NIH, USA):** world's largest free general chemistry database; compounds, substances, bioassays, properties, patents, toxicity. Current size (checked Oct 2026): **~124 million compounds, ~349 million substances** (PubChem homepage, Oct 2026). Peer-reviewed PubChem 2025 update reported **118.6 million compounds as of Sep 2024** (Kim et al., Nucleic Acids Res. 2025, doi:10.1093/nar/gkae1059). A Jun 2026 catalogue cites 114M compounds / 302M substances / 304M bioactivities across 903 sources — older snapshot; prefer the Oct 2026 homepage figures. Licence/access: **free to use; US federal public-domain resource** — but reuse rights follow each depositor/source, so check the source before redistributing a given record. API: PUG-REST, PUG-View; bulk via PUG Download (up to 250,000 records/request) or FTP. Rate limits ~5 requests/sec, 400/min.
- **DrugBank:** comprehensive database of drug and drug-target information (approved, investigational, experimental drugs; drug–drug interactions). Current version — **5.1.22, released 27 Jun 2026** (go.drugbank.com/releases/latest): "All drugs" dataset 204 MB; full drug structures (SDF) 11 MB; structure external links 1.98 MB. Datasets released under **CC BY-NC 4.0** (Attribution–NonCommercial). Access — gated, and currently paused: **"All Academic downloads are temporarily paused"** while DrugBank updates how it distributes data (notice on the releases page, Jun 2026; also reported May 2026). API access remains available (paid API; registration required). Academic licence is free for qualifying researchers; **commercial use requires a commercial licence**. The full database is not open for commercial reuse. Scale (conflicting — see Figure Alignment): DrugBank 6.0 (2024) is cited as **11,891 drug entries, incl. 4,563 approved and 6,231 investigational drugs, plus 1.41M drug–drug interactions**. A Jun 2026 catalogue cites ~15,000 drugs. Use the 6.0 figures with their date; do not present a single current drug count as verified.
  - Source: https://go.drugbank.com/releases/latest
- What this means for AGI Compounds (proposed): ChEMBL and PubChem are the legitimate open foundations for a compound library — ChEMBL (CC BY-SA, attribution + share-alike + preserve IDs/release) and PubChem (public domain, per-source reuse terms). Any compound data the ALS agent serves should cite its source database, version and date — exactly as ChEMBL requires. DrugBank is **not** a free input for a commercial product: CC BY-NC, academic downloads paused, commercial licence required. Do not build a commercial compound product on DrugBank data without a licence. None of these databases is a treatment resource: they are chemistry/bioactivity data. The ALS agent's "not a medical device" line must stay attached to any compound output.

## 5. Cross-cutting notes

1. **The honest model is controlled/federated, not open-download.** UK Biobank (RAP, 2024), All of Us (Workbench, user-borne compute), TCGA controlled data (dbGaP) and GA4GH data-visiting all point the same way — and openSNP's Apr 2025 closure (data deleted) is the cautionary tale for any proposed Genomic DAO (S27, proposed) that imagines CC0 individual genomes. Frame S27/S28 as consent-gated, federated, GA4GH-aligned — not as open data.
2. **The honest IP model is DAO-governed licensing, not open IP.** Molecule/Bio Protocol/VitaDAO/AthenaDAO all keep the IP itself owned and licensed; what is open is the governance, the contracts, and (sometimes) the data. IPTs carry governance/information rights with no guaranteed return. Any proposed BioDAO (S18, proposed) should copy that distinction, not blur it.
3. **Governance is thinning in the BioDAO cohort.** VitaDAO (no Snapshot proposal since 9 Sep 2025) and AthenaDAO (none since Sep 2025) both show a pause in on-chain voting even as their science/equity vehicles continue.
