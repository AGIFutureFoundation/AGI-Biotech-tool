## Part A — Delivery-Technology Reference

### A1. The Therapeutic Vector Evolution platform

4DMT describes itself as "a clinical-stage biotherapeutics company harnessing the power of directed evolution for genetic medicines targeting large market diseases." Its proprietary invention platform, **Therapeutic Vector Evolution**, "combines the power of the Nobel Prize-winning technology, directed evolution, with approximately one billion synthetic AAV capsid-derived sequences to invent customized and evolved vectors."

The platform's stated design goals for its vectors — quoted from the document — are that they be:

- delivered at relatively low doses;
- delivered through clinically routine, well-tolerated, and minimally invasive routes of administration;
- able to transduce diseased cells in target tissues efficiently;
- of reduced immunogenicity; and,
- where relevant, resistant to pre-existing antibodies.

The company reports that the platform was validated over the past year with "clinical efficacy proof-of-concept signals with all three of our lead proprietary vectors invented at 4DMT" and "robust clinical gene delivery, tolerability and initial biological activity with first three customized and evolved vectors."

### A2. Proprietary vectors

"All of our vectors are proprietary to 4DMT and were invented at 4DMT, including the vectors utilized in our clinical-stage and preclinical pipeline product candidates: **R100, A101, and C102**."

- **R100** — intravitreal vector used in the ophthalmology products (4D-150 and preclinical 4D-175).
- **A101** — aerosol-delivered vector used in the pulmonology products (4D-710 and preclinical 4D-725).
- **C102** — named as a proprietary vector in the pipeline; the release does not state which product candidate uses it.

### A3. Pipeline (verified against the document)

4DMT states it has **seven total product candidates in development**, **five clinical-stage** product candidates addressing unmet medical needs in **seven patient populations**, across three therapeutic areas (ophthalmology, pulmonology, cardiology). The release adds the explicit disclaimer: "4D-150, 4D-710, 4D-310, 4D-125, and 4D-110 are our product candidates in clinical development and have not yet been approved for marketing by the US FDA or any other regulatory authority. No representation is made as to the safety or effectiveness of [them] for the therapeutic uses for which they are being studied."

#### Ophthalmology

- **4D-150 — wet age-related macular degeneration (wet AMD) and diabetic macular edema (DME), intravitreal, R100 vector.**
  - Phase 1/2 **PRISM** trial (wet AMD). Interim data from **Cohort 1 (n=5)** presented November 2022: 4D-150 "was demonstrated to be safe and well-tolerated, and resulted in **>90% reduction in annualized anti-VEGF injection rates**."
  - **The >90% figure is a small early cohort (n=5).** It must be cited with the n every time it is used; it is an interim Cohort 1 result, not a trial outcome.
  - Enrollment of the PRISM Dose Exploration stage completed (**n=15, 3 dose levels**); enrollment in the Phase 2 Dose Expansion stage (**n=50**) began January 2023.
  - New IND application submitted to FDA for the Phase 2 **SPECTRA** trial of intravitreal 4D-150 for DME; IND cleared February 2023.
- **4D-175 — geographic atrophy (GA), preclinical**, leveraging the same R100 intravitreal vector. GA is described in the release as a "large market."

#### Pulmonology

- **4D-710 — cystic fibrosis lung disease, aerosol, A101 vector.**
  - Interim data from **Cohort 1 (n=3, 1E15 vg)** of the Phase 1/2 trial, presented at the North American Cystic Fibrosis Conference, November 2022: "demonstrated safety, tolerability and widespread expression of CFTRΔR transgene within lung airway cells."
  - Enrollment of **Cohort 2 (n=3, 2E15 vg)** completed February 2023.
  - Preclinical R&D initiated on 4D-710 in combination with CFTR modulators.
- **4D-725 — alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency lung disease (AATLD), preclinical**, leveraging the same A101 aerosol-delivered vector used in 4D-710.

#### Cardiology

- **4D-310 — Fabry disease cardiomyopathy, C102-era pipeline (vector not specified for this candidate in the release), INGLAXA Phase 1/2 trial.**
  - Interim safety data (**n=6**) and efficacy data on cardiac endpoints at 12 months (**n=3**) presented. "Demonstrated meaningful improvements in multiple FDA-recommended Phase 3 cardiac endpoints (n=3)."
  - Cardiac biopsy demonstrated "selective and widespread transgene expression in cardiomyocytes (**n=1**)."
  - Received "favorable protocol and scientific advice from both FDA and EMA on the Phase 3 study design, primary and secondary endpoints, as well as CMC and nonclinical topics."
  - **CLINICAL HOLD — stated plainly:** 4DMT is "engaging with FDA to lift clinical hold and resume enrollment with highly effective rituximab/sirolimus immunosuppressive regimen and updated exclusion criteria." The 2023 milestones state the company will "submit complete response letter to FDA that is intended to address all clinical hold items by end Q1" 2023, with "FDA feedback on response letter expected in Q2" and a program update after 12-month data on all six currently enrolled patients in 2H 2023. **The pipeline is not unblemished: the 4D-310 hold is a live regulatory fact as of this document, and the hold had not been lifted at the time of the release.**
- **4D-125 — X-linked retinitis pigmentosa (XLRP), clinical-stage.** Named in the release as one of the five clinical-stage candidates; no trial data or updates are given for it in this document.
- **4D-110 — choroideremia, clinical-stage.** Named in the release as one of the five clinical-stage candidates; no trial data or updates are given for it in this document.

### A4. Manufacturing

4DMT "completed build of commercial-scale GMP manufacturing facility to provide clinical trial material for 4DMT product candidates. These added capabilities are expected to reduce product development timelines, costs, and risk, and improve quality and internal control." (Company expectation, not a verified outcome.)

### A5. Relevance to the ALS program's AAV6-hSyn-MCL1 delivery — genuine benchmark vs. analogy only

**The ALS delivery context (from `als_4_8.pdf`).** The ALS paper tests AGI-183, a BCL-XL stabilizer, combined with **AAV6-hSyn-MCL1** — an AAV6 vector carrying MCL1 under the neuron-specific human synapsin (hSyn) promoter — in iPSC-derived TDP-43 Q331K motor neurons under chronic glutamate stress. The rationale for neuron-targeted delivery is explicit in the paper: systemic MCL1 inhibitors have uniformly failed clinically on on-target cardiotoxicity (AZD5991 Phase I: troponin elevation in 83% of patients, 54/65, with only a 3.9% objective response rate, 3/78), and systemic BCL-XL inhibition carries thrombocytopenia risk. The paper's strategy is therefore neuron-targeted MCL1 gain-of-function plus BCL-XL stabilization, designed to circumvent systemic toxicity. The paper's results are computational-model predictions (Bliss synergy, BH3 EC50 trajectories), not wet-lab results, and no in-vivo delivery data for AAV6-hSyn-MCL1 is reported in it.

**What is a genuine benchmark.** 4DMT's program is a genuine benchmark for the *design goals* any AAV delivery work should be measured against, because 4DMT states those goals explicitly and reports early clinical data against them:

1. **Capsid choice matters — and is treated as the core invention.** 4DMT's entire platform is built on inventing capsids (directed evolution over ~1 billion synthetic AAV capsid-derived sequences) rather than accepting a standard serotype. The ALS program uses AAV6, a standard serotype, with neuron specificity supplied by the hSyn promoter rather than by the capsid. The benchmark question this raises for the ALS work is: *is promoter-based specificity sufficient, or does the program need a capsid-level targeting strategy?* That is a design question to answer, not a conclusion — 4DMT's data do not show that AAV6 is inadequate.
2. **Low dose, routine minimally invasive route, efficient target-tissue transduction.** 4DMT's stated goals map directly onto the ALS delivery problem: motor-neuron transduction must be efficient enough to matter at a dose that is tolerable. 4DMT's reported evidence — >90% reduction in annualized anti-VEGF injections in Cohort 1 (n=5) for 4D-150, and widespread CFTRΔR expression in lung airway cells at 1E15 vg (n=3) for 4D-710 — is the *shape* of evidence to aim for: a quantitative functional readout plus a transgene-expression readout, reported with dose and n. The ALS program currently has neither (its results are computational predictions in iPSC-derived neurons).
3. **Reduced immunogenicity and resistance to pre-existing antibodies.** 4DMT lists these as design goals; the 4D-310 clinical hold is itself a reminder of why they matter — the hold is being addressed with a "highly effective rituximab/sirolimus immunosuppressive regimen and updated exclusion criteria," i.e., immunogenicity management is a live, program-level problem even for an evolved-vector company. Any ALS AAV program must plan for immunogenicity and pre-existing anti-AAV immunity as a first-class risk, not an afterthought.
4. **Manufacturing as a program asset.** 4DMT completed a commercial-scale GMP facility to supply clinical-trial material, explicitly to reduce timelines, costs, and risk. This is a benchmark for planning: delivery is not only a capsid question but a CMC/manufacturing question, and it should be costed as such (see Part B).

**What is only analogous — do not over-claim.**

- **Different tissues, different routes, different diseases.** 4DMT's clinical evidence is intravitreal (eye) and aerosol (lung) delivery, plus cardiac delivery for 4D-310. None of it is CNS or motor-neuron delivery, and none of it validates AAV6, the hSyn promoter, or MCL1 overexpression in neurons. The analogy is at the level of *platform discipline* (evolved capsid, defined design goals, quantitative readouts), not at the level of transferable efficacy or safety data.
- **Early, small cohorts.** The strongest 4DMT efficacy signals in this document are Cohort 1 (n=5) for 4D-150, Cohort 1 (n=3) for 4D-710, and cardiac endpoints at n=3 (biopsy n=1) for 4D-310. These are proof-of-concept signals, in the company's own framing — not established efficacy. Citing them as if they proved the platform would repeat the error of citing the ALS paper's computational predictions as results.
- **No ALS, BCL-2, or neuroprotection data.** 4DMT's release contains nothing about ALS, MCL1, BCL-XL, or neurodegeneration. Any link between 4DMT's work and the ALS program is a design-benchmark link made by this brief, not a claim in either source document.
- **The clinical hold cuts both ways.** 4DMT is a useful benchmark precisely because it is not a clean success story: a lead program on clinical hold, addressed with an immunosuppressive regimen, shows that even a directed-evolution platform with early efficacy signals faces immunogenicity-driven regulatory setbacks. That is the honest frame for any internal delivery plan.

**Benchmark checklist for the ALS AAV work (derived from 4DMT's stated goals):**

- [ ] Capsid rationale documented: why AAV6, and what capsid-level alternatives were considered?
- [ ] Specificity mechanism stated: promoter (hSyn) vs capsid tropism vs both — and what evidence supports it?
- [ ] Dose and route defined with a quantitative transduction readout (expression in target cells) and a functional readout, reported with n.
- [ ] Immunogenicity plan: pre-existing anti-AAV antibodies, immune response to the vector, and any immunosuppression strategy — with the 4D-310 hold as the cautionary reference.
- [ ] Manufacturing/CMC considered early, not deferred.

### B2. The comparable note — grounding cost and runway claims

**The headline comparable: a real clinical-stage gene-therapy company burns ~$80M per year on R&D alone.**

- 4DMT spent **$80.3M on R&D in FY2022** (up from $61.4M in FY2021, a ~31% year-on-year increase as five clinical-stage programs advanced) and **$113.2M in total operating expenses**, against **$3.1M of revenue** and a **$107.5M net loss**. Its cash position fell from $315.4M to $218.5M over the year — a ~$97M cash decline, consistent with the operating burn.
- Over its life to December 31, 2022, the company had accumulated a **deficit of $314.5M**. That is the scale of capital a directed-evolution AAV platform with five clinical-stage candidates had consumed by the time it could report first proof-of-concept signals in small cohorts.

**How to use this comparable — and how not to:**

1. **Ground any cost/runway claim against it.** Any internal estimate of what an AAV delivery program, a gene-therapy platform, or a clinical-stage pipeline costs must be sanity-checked against $80M/yr R&D and ~$113M/yr total operating expense for a five-program clinical-stage company. If an internal plan implies a materially lower burn for comparable scope, the difference must be explained (fewer programs, preclinical-only stage, outsourced manufacturing, different geography) — not assumed.
2. **Never invent our own burn figures.** No burn rate, runway, valuation, or cost estimate for our own programs is stated in this brief, because no source document in this brief supports one. Any figure the monetization workstream produces must cite its own source; 4DMT's numbers are a *reference point*, not a substitute for our own costing.
3. **Stage matters.** 4DMT's $80.3M R&D year was a year with five clinical-stage programs running simultaneously (4D-150, 4D-710, 4D-310, 4D-125, 4D-110) plus two preclinical programs and a GMP facility build. A preclinical-only program, or a single-candidate program, would cost less — but the document does not break out per-program spend, so no per-program figure can be derived from it. Do not divide $80.3M by five and call it a per-program cost.
4. **Revenue is negligible at this stage.** $3.1M of revenue against $113.2M of operating expense, with the prior collaboration (Roche) terminated in September 2021, is the normal shape of a clinical-stage gene-therapy company: it is funded by capital, not by product or collaboration revenue. Monetization planning should assume a long pre-revenue period funded by equity/capital, not near-term revenue.
5. **The runway statement is a company expectation, not a guarantee.** "Sufficient to fund operations into the first half of 2025" is forward-looking (the release's forward-looking-statements disclaimer applies). Treat it as the company's stated expectation as of March 15, 2023, not as a verified fact about the future.
6. **Manufacturing is a capital item, not an afterthought.** 4DMT built a commercial-scale GMP facility during this period. The monetization work should cost CMC/manufacturing explicitly; 4DMT's example shows it is a major, deliberate investment inside the R&D/operating spend, not a line item that appears only at commercialization.
