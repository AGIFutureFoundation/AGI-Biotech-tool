- **The transfer is a design hypothesis, not a finding.** Nothing in this document shows that a three-network combination works in ALS, Parkinson's disease (PD), or multiple sclerosis (MS). Stage 1 maps published evidence for the three networks in each disease. Stage 2 (designed here, not run) would test combinations in cells.

All three have credible human vascular evidence. What Stage 1 does instead is **redefine the second leg in all three diseases**: the evidence supports endothelial/barrier protection and normalisation of dysregulated angiogenesis, not promotion of angiogenesis. A drug chosen to *promote* angiogenesis would point in the wrong direction in PD and MS, where the documented angiogenesis is pathological. Synaptic, vascular, and stress failure are all documented in MS, but none of them is the primary driver. ## 1. The hypothesis under test, and what Stage 1 was asked to decide

Pharnext's JP6062995B2 frames Alzheimer's disease as a failure of three networks at once — synaptic function, angiogenesis, and cellular stress response — and claims combinations of repurposed drugs that modulate all three. The transfer hypothesis (deep dive #2, Grade C) is that the same three-network framing applies to ALS, Parkinson's, and MS, and that a combination chosen to cover all three networks should outperform a combination chosen within one network in each disease.

Stage 1 was asked to decide, per disease, whether the three-network framing survives — by mapping published evidence for each network — and to design Stage 2 only for surviving diseases. The task specified the decision rule explicitly: if a disease has no credible angiogenesis evidence, say so plainly and narrow the framing to two networks for that disease *before* any combination is proposed.

### 1.1 The red-team correction applied to Stage 1 itself

The loop-2 verification red team identified Stage 1 as deep dive #2's weakest test, for two reasons this study adopts as design constraints rather than disputing:

1. **A literature review with no pre-specified evidence bar is a judgement call, not a falsification test.** Two reviewers could reach opposite verdicts from the same literature. This study therefore pre-registers its mapping protocol (§1.2) and its evidence bar *before* presenting the tables, states the bar in a form that can be checked, and records where the bar was and was not met.
2. **"Narrow to two networks" after a failed leg is goalpost-moving if the narrowing is silent.** This study records the rule as: if a leg fails the bar, the three-network claim for that disease is recorded as **falsified as stated**, and any two-network successor is a **new hypothesis with new predictions**, not the same hypothesis with a leg removed. As it turns out, no leg failed the bar outright (see §6), so this rule is not triggered — but the redefinition of leg 2 in §6 is recorded as a modification of the hypothesis, not as a silent narrowing, and the original "angiogenesis promotion" reading of leg 2 is recorded as falsified in PD and MS.

One red-team constraint cannot be fully met by a single-worker study and is recorded as a limitation rather than fudged: the red team asked for a second independent rater with a stated disagreement rule. This study had one rater. The mitigation is transparency — every table entry names its source, so a second rater can re-grade each entry without re-running the searches — and a named open item (GAP-ST1-1) requiring independent re-grading before Stage 2 is funded.

### 1.2 Pre-registered mapping protocol (written before the tables were built)

**Databases and method.** `browser.search` over the public web and PubMed/PMC-indexed sources, 7–8 November 2026. Queries per disease × network are recorded in §9. Primary sources were preferred; where a review was the source, the review is cited as a review and the underlying primary paper is named only where the review text identified it. No citation, statistic, or URL is invented. Where a search found nothing for a specific drug, that is recorded as "no data found in this pass" with the query stated, not as evidence of absence.

**Evidence bar for a network leg (per disease).** A leg is recorded as **supported** if there is at least one of: (a) human postmortem or biomarker evidence *plus* an animal or human-cell study showing the same process is present before or independent of end-stage neuronal loss; or (b) a human genetic or interventional finding linking the process to the disease. A leg is recorded as **supported but directionally qualified** if the evidence is strong but points the opposite way from the patent's implicit direction (promotion of angiogenesis). A leg is recorded as **failed** only if no peer-reviewed human or animal evidence meeting (a) or (b) was found after the recorded searches. Correlation alone (a marker elevated in patients with no temporal or causal anchor) earns at most Grade B and is flagged as such in the table.

**Grades (from `cross-disease-patterns.md`, applied per table entry).**

| Grade | Meaning in this study |
|---|---|
| **A** | Direct published evidence in the relevant disease and context: human tissue/biomarker data with a temporal or causal anchor, or a replicated interventional result. |
| **B** | Mechanistic plausibility from adjacent evidence: human correlative data without a causal anchor, animal-model causal data without human confirmation, or a mechanism demonstrated in a different disease/model. |
| **C** | Speculative or patent-reported only: coherent with known biology, or reported in a patent without peer review, but no direct peer-reviewed evidence for the claim as stated. |
| **UNVERIFIED** | No data found in this pass for the claim as stated. Not evidence of absence. |

**What Stage 1 cannot do.** It cannot establish causality for any network in any disease. It cannot rank drugs. It cannot validate a combination. A Grade A network leg means "this process is really present and early in this disease"; it does not mean "modulating it will help patients". That distinction is maintained in every verdict.

### 2.1 PXT864 — development stopped 14 February 2023 (red team, independently confirmed here)

Pharnext's press release of 14 February 2023 states that its Board "has decided to stop the development of its drug candidate PXT864 in Alzheimer's disease", because the results, though "interesting and encouraging", remained "insufficient for commercialization in the short or medium term", and further development would require a clinical plan the company did not wish to finance. Pharnext said it would continue seeking an industrial partner for PXT864 in Alzheimer's and was considering development in ALS (in-licensing, or internally after PXT3003 Phase 3). The highest phase reached was Phase 2: PLEODIAL-I (NCT02361424) and PLEODIAL-II (NCT02361242), completed; drug databases list clinical status Inactive. No Phase 3 occurred.

**Consequences for this study.** PXT864 (baclofen + acamprosate) is retained as the lead *precedent* combination for Stage 2 design — it is the only patent-logic combination with human data of any kind — but it is now labelled a **stopped programme** in every place it appears. Two further qualifications attach to its Phase 2 signal and are carried into Stage 2 design: the PLEODIAL efficacy comparison was against a **historical placebo, with no concurrent control arm** (45 treatment-naïve mild-AD patients, 36 weeks, single-blind, CTAD 2016 company reporting, not a peer-reviewed Phase 2 result), and the preclinical leg (Chumakov et al. 2015, *Scientific Reports* 5:7608 — synergistic protection in vitro and cognitive benefit in two rodent AD models) is peer-reviewed and stands on its own. The stopping reason was commercial (no licensing partner; cost of further development), not a safety failure or a demonstrated lack of efficacy — but a commercial stop after an uncontrolled Phase 2a is not a validation either. Stage 2 treats PXT864 as a combination worth re-testing in cells, not as a combination with human efficacy evidence.

### 2.2 GAP-ALZ-3 RESOLVED — the +78% figure belongs to VEGF, not to a drug

The programme document recorded that a 10 nM pretreatment protected the HBMEC capillary network by +78% versus amyloid injury, but that the extracted patent text did not cleanly attribute the figure to a named drug. The live Google Patents page for JP6062995B2 (crawled 8 November 2026) resolves this: the +78% figure is the **model-validation positive control — 10 nM VEGF-165 pre-treatment for 1 hour** — not a result for any of the 38 listed drugs. The programme document's caution was correct; the figure must never be quoted with a drug name. The seven single drugs with significant HBMEC dose–response protection in the same figure set are sulfisoxazole, levosimendan, terbinafine, baclofen, aminocaproic acid, sulodexide, and fenoldopam (ANOVA + Dunnett's post-hoc, p < 0.05 versus Aβ1-42). GAP-ALZ-3 is closed.

### 2.3 GAP-ALZ-2 RESOLVED (with a correction to the programme document) — the sulfisoxazole combinations' singles data exist

The programme document recorded that the figure captions for sulfisoxazole + levosimendan and sulfisoxazole + terbinafine were truncated mid-sentence in the machine-translated PDF, so the singles' effects at the tested concentration could not be determined, and only four of six combinations could be claimed as showing the synergy pattern. The live Google Patents page (English machine translation, same patent) gives clean captions for all six combinations, and for the two sulfisoxazole combinations it states the same pattern as the other four: the combination significantly prevented Aβ1-42 damage (aggregated human Aβ1-42 2.5 µM, >40% damage versus vehicle), while at the same concentration each single drug alone had **no significant effect** on the damage. The programme document's "4 of 6 clean" statement is therefore corrected to **6 of 6 combinations showing the qualitative synergy pattern in the HBMEC assay as reported in the patent** — with the standing qualification that this is a qualitative pattern ("combination significant where singles are not"), not a formal interaction analysis: no Bliss, Loewe, or Chou–Talalay index is reported in the patent, and no dose–response matrix, replicate count, or effect size beyond the qualitative captions is available from the patent text. GAP-ALZ-2 is closed as a caption problem; the *formal-synergy* gap remains open and is exactly what H-ALZ1/Stage 2 must generate. The neuronal-assay combination data remain unreported in the patent: combinations were tested in HBMEC only, not in neurons. That asymmetry is carried into Stage 2 (the neuronal combination test is new work, not replication).

### 2.4 JP6062995B2 legal status — GAP-ALZ-1 REMAINS OPEN (no freedom-to-operate claim)

**What was found in this pass.** The live Google Patents page for JP6062995B2 was read on 8 November 2026 (figure captions and description text, 2,402 lines). No legal-status field surfaced in the extracted page text (searches for "Expired" and "Legal status" within the page returned no match), so no live status was obtained from that page. The status as printed in the loop-1 source PDF remains the only status on record: **"Expired – Fee Related", anticipated expiration 2030-10-29**. A web search for the patent number returned no J-PlatPat record (J-PlatPat's history-inquiry pages are form-driven and were not directly queryable in this pass), and no Espacenet legal-status result was retrieved.

**What is also on record, and must not be confused with JP6062995B2's status.** A Korean family member, KR20180043844A (baclofen + acamprosate therapy of neurological disorders, same inventors, Pharnext), shows on Google Patents the status "Expired – Fee Related" with anticipated expiration 2032-03-01. That is a *different family member in a different jurisdiction* and says nothing about the Japanese patent's status. ### 3.1 ALS narrative: the strongest two legs, and a split second leg

**Leg 1 is the strongest leg in the dossier's ALS case, and it is not synaptic in the Alzheimer's sense.** In AD the synaptic lesion is loss of synaptic density correlating with cognitive impairment. In ALS the corresponding lesion is distal: dying-back axonal degeneration with NMJ denervation preceding somatic motor-neuron death, plus cortical hyperexcitability and glutamate excitotoxicity. The 2026 NMJ-pathology review and the 2024 neuronal-circuit review converge on the same temporal claim — distal synaptic failure is early, not a late byproduct — and the axonal-transport review (Front. Neurosci. 2026) supplies a convergent upstream mechanism across all four major ALS genotypes (SOD1, TARDBP, FUS, C9orf72), detectable pre-symptomatically. The Bye et al. 2026 screen adds the human-cell anchor: sporadic-ALS iPSC motor neurons show reduced survival and accelerated neurite degeneration correlating with donor survival (100-donor library; screen run on 7–16 SALS donors; combination validation on 15 SALS donors — peer-review fix R2, 8 Nov 2026). Grade A is justified for the *presence and earliness* of synaptic/axonal failure. It is not a claim that fixing synapses fixes ALS.

**Leg 2 is where Stage 1 earns its keep, and it splits.** The barrier half is Grade A and is arguably the best-supported vascular finding in any disease in this study: Zhong et al. 2008 shows BSCB breakdown with 40–60% loss of ZO-1/occludin/claudin-5, microhaemorrhages, and hypoperfusion *prior to* motor-neuron degeneration in three SOD1 mutant lines, and human postmortem SALS tissue confirms endothelial damage, pericyte degeneration, IgG leakage, and tight-junction loss. A 2026 single-nucleus vascular atlas (reported via Bioengineer, GSE287257: 3,303 ALS vs 1,303 control spinal-cord nuclei) further reports spinal-cord endothelial barrier-loss signatures with perivascular fibroblast activation — consistent, though this pass did not read the primary paper, so it is cited as corroboration, not as a graded primary source. The angiogenesis half, by contrast, is Grade C as a *driver*: Yamadera et al. 2015 documents increased microvascular density with decreased pericyte coverage in the ventral horn — aberrant angiogenesis — but no study in this pass shows that this angiogenesis causes neuronal death rather than responding to hypoperfusion or inflammation. The VEGF literature sharpens the ambiguity rather than resolving it: VEGF is neuroprotective for motor neurons in animals, VEGF-B protects without VEGF-A's permeability effects, yet human VEGF levels are inconsistently reported and the only human VEGF trial found was a Phase 1 safety study (sNN0029, 18 patients) with no efficacy outcome verified. The correct Stage 1 statement for ALS leg 2 is therefore: **vascular-barrier dysfunction is established and early; angiogenesis as a therapeutic target is not.** Any Stage 2 vascular drug for ALS must be justifiable as an endothelial/barrier protector (or a VEGF-pathway normaliser), not as an angiogenesis promoter — and the minus-one-drug arm (§8) is the experiment that tests whether the vascular drug contributes anything at all.

**Leg 3 is Grade A for presence, with a driver-vs-amplifier caveat.** TDP-43 mislocalisation in ~97% of ALS patients, ER enlargement with UPR markers, oxidative damage across four biofluids/tissues, and a 2025–2026 literature that now frames TDP-43 aggregation as a kinetic cascade of misfolding → phase separation → UPS/ALP clearance failure — this is a mature, multi-line evidence base. Sephin1's 2025 result (reduced TDP-43 mislocalisation, improved motor-neuron survival in SOD1 mice and TDP-43 zebrafish) is the closest thing in the ALS leg-3 literature to a causal rescue through the stress pathway, and it is preclinical. The caveat is the same as leg 1: presence plus a preclinical rescue is not proof that stress is the primary driver. For Stage 2 purposes that does not matter — Stage 2 tests whether a stress-modulating drug adds to a combination, not whether stress causes ALS.

### 3.2 ALS verdict (preliminary; consolidated in §6)

The three-network framing **survives for ALS in redefined form**: legs 1 and 3 are Grade A, leg 2 is Grade A as barrier dysfunction and Grade C as angiogenesis promotion. The original patent reading of leg 2 ("modulate angiogenesis", implicitly protect/promote capillary networks) is **not supported as a driver claim in ALS**; the supported reading is endothelial/barrier protection. No narrowing to two networks is triggered, because the vascular evidence clears the bar — but the vascular drug in any Stage 2 ALS combination must be chosen and justified under the redefined leg, and the minus-one-drug arm must be able to kill the leg.

### 4.1 PD narrative: the best-documented synaptic leg, and a vascular leg that points backwards

**Leg 1 is Grade A and is the cleanest synaptic story in this study.** α-Synuclein is a presynaptic protein by normal function; its pathological forms disrupt the vesicle cycle (clustering, acidification, SNARE assembly, docking) before neurons die; and five PD risk genes converge on vesicle trafficking. The 2026 synaptic-vesicle review's central claim — presynaptic failure as an early, potentially initiating event — is stated with its own limitation attached (mouse models do not recapitulate full human PD), which is the right level of caution. For Stage 2 this leg supplies both a model (iPSC dopaminergic neurons, MPP+/rotenone or α-synuclein stress) and a mechanism-specific readout (vesicle cycling / dopamine release alongside survival).

**Leg 2 is Grade B, and the grade understates the important finding.** The human evidence for vascular involvement is strong in volume: postmortem angiogenic vessels, a 100-patient CSF study with a coherent marker pattern (VEGF/PlGF/sVEGFR-2 up, Ang2 down) tied to BBB permeability, white-matter lesions, microbleeds, and neurofilament light. But the *sign* of the finding reverses the patent's logic. In the patent's HBMEC assay, protecting a capillary network from Aβ damage is good, and VEGF is the positive control (+78%). In PD, VEGF-driven angiogenesis is associated with leakiness, microbleeds, and worse gait, and the causal experiment (A53T mouse + in-vitro BBB model, Glia 2022) shows that **blocking** VEGF-A signalling protects the barrier. The patent's leg-2 assay measures protection of endothelium; the PD literature says the endothelium needs protection *from* VEGF-driven dysregulation. Those two statements are compatible only if leg 2 is redefined as **vascular normalisation / endothelial protection**, with the explicit consequence that a VEGF-promoting drug is excluded from Stage 2 PD combinations and a VEGF-pathway *normaliser* (or a barrier-strengthening drug such as cilostazol, §7) is the only admissible vascular-leg candidate. A combination that "covers angiogenesis" by adding a pro-angiogenic drug would be testing the wrong direction. This is the single most consequential Stage 1 finding for PD, and it is why the PD verdict (§6) is "survives, redefined" rather than "survives as stated".

**Leg 3 is Grade A, with the field's own failure mode attached.** Oxidative stress, ER stress (human postmortem pPERK/peIF2α), and proteostasis failure (PINK1/Parkin mitophagy, α-synuclein UPS/ALP overload) are each documented in human PD tissue and in patient iPSC dopaminergic neurons. The caution is that this leg has already failed clinically in its simplest form: broad-spectrum antioxidants have failed in PD trials, attributed in the 2026 literature to limited specificity and poor cerebral bioavailability. Stage 2 must therefore not test "an antioxidant" generically; it must test a specific stress-response modulator with a defined mechanism (e.g. UPR modulation, NRF2/NOX4 targeting, or mitophagy support) and a CNS-exposure case (§7). The GBA confound is a design requirement, not a footnote: any PD Stage 2 experiment must record GBA status of donor lines and stratify or exclude GBA-PD lines, or a lysosomal effect will be misattributed to the stress/apoptosis axis.

### 5.1 MS narrative: the framing survives, but the disease is not a three-network disease

**Leg 1 is Grade A with a scope note.** Grey-matter synaptopathy independent of demyelination is the finding that makes MS a legitimate member of this study at all — without it, MS would be a demyelination disease with secondary axonal loss and no synaptic leg. The 2023 review's claim (early, demyelination-independent, cognition-correlated) plus the 2017 axonal-damage study's finding that focal axonal degeneration precedes transection and does not require demyelination together establish a synaptic/axonal failure process that is at least partly autonomous. The scope note is that disability correlates with axonal loss, and the independent contribution of synaptopathy to *progression* (as opposed to cognition) is unquantified.

**Leg 2 is Grade A and is the study's clearest case of a leg that is strong and directionally reversed.** Angiogenesis in MS is not a deficit to be corrected; it is an early, immune-driven, VEGF-mediated process that produces leaky vessels and BBB breakdown, and Argaw et al. 2012 provides the causal anchor (astrocytic *Vegfa* deletion → less barrier breakdown, less infiltration, less paralysis). Shahriar et al. 2024 then complicates the story in the useful direction: blocking VEGF-A stops the neoangiogenesis but does *not* restore the barrier or improve EAE, so the vascular pathology has at least two dissociable components (new-vessel growth vs barrier failure). And the late-phase literature reverses the sign a second time: VEGF supports OPC survival and repair, so chronic VEGF blockade could impair remyelination. The Stage 1 conclusion for MS leg 2 is therefore threefold: (i) the vascular process is real, early, and causal for barrier disruption; (ii) the admissible drug direction is **normalisation/protection, timed to phase**, never unqualified promotion; (iii) a vascular drug must be tested for barrier function *and* for OPC/myelin effects, because a drug that fixes vessels and harms repair fails the MS test even if it passes a neuronal one.

**Leg 3 is Grade A with a cell-type correction.** The UPR literature in MS is oligodendrocyte-centred: the secretory load of myelination makes oligodendrocytes uniquely ER-stress-vulnerable, UPR markers are upregulated across lesion cell types, and UPR manipulation changes EAE/cuprizone outcomes. Oxidative stress is likewise established, with iron and microglial ROS as MS-specific amplifiers. The correction is that the three-network framing, as inherited from Alzheimer's, is neuron-centred; in MS the stress leg's decisive cell is the oligodendrocyte. Stage 2 for MS must therefore include oligodendrocyte/OPC survival and myelination readouts as primary endpoints alongside neuronal ones, or it will test a neuronal hypothesis in a disease whose stress biology is glial. This is a design consequence, not a reason to drop the leg.

**The immune-driver caveat (applies to the whole MS verdict).** None of the three networks is MS's primary driver. MS is an immune-mediated disease: perivenous infiltration through a disrupted BBB in relapsing disease, compartmentalised CD8⁺/B-cell inflammation in progressive disease. The three networks describe *how* tissue is damaged and fails to repair once the immune process is underway; they do not describe *why* the immune process starts or how it is sustained. A three-network combination, even if it worked perfectly in Stage 2, would be an adjunct neuroprotective/repair strategy, not a disease-modifying MS therapy in the sense the field uses the term. Any document, pitch, or funding case that presents the MS transfer without this caveat misrepresents the disease. This caveat does not apply to ALS or PD in the same form (both have immune/inflammatory components, but neither is defined as a primary autoimmune disease).

### 5.2 A patent-reported MS result Stage 1 found that the base document had missed

The base document treated MS as "named in the patent list only; no evidence attached". A targeted search in this pass found a second Pharnext patent family — EP2796132 / EP2727588 and its Korean counterpart KR20180043844A, "Baclofen and acamprosate based therapy of neurological disorders" — which reports **in-vivo EAE data for baclofen + acamprosate**: in MOG-immunised C57BL/6J mice with chronic progressive EAE, baclofen 30 mg/kg/day + acamprosate 2 mg/kg/day induced a significant protective effect against EAE development, with more than 30% reduction of symptoms by clinical score, and the same family reports the combination protecting cortical neurons from glutamate toxicity (>200% improvement vs acamprosate alone, >47% vs baclofen alone, as stated in the patent). **Grade: C** — patent-reported, not peer-reviewed, no dose–response, no replicate counts, no formal synergy analysis, and the >200%/>47% figures are patent-text percentages whose denominators are not defined in the extracted text. But it changes MS's status in this programme from "no evidence attached" to "patent-reported in-vivo EAE efficacy for the lead combination exists and is the single most directly relevant prior result for Stage 2 MS design". Stage 2 MS (§8.3) is built as a replication-plus-extension of this result, with the replication labelled as such and the extension (BH3 profiling, minus-one-drug arm, OPC readouts) as new work. The same family also names ALS, PD, Huntington's disease, neuropathic pain, and spinal-cord injury as indications — again, a claim scope, not evidence, for the non-MS indications.

### 6.2 What Stage 1 actually decided — and what it did not

**The narrowing trigger was not met for any disease.** The task's decision rule was: no credible angiogenesis evidence → narrow to two networks. Every disease cleared the credibility bar for a vascular process. That is a substantive finding, not a failure to decide: loop 1's GAP-XD-7 ("the angiogenesis leg has no credible evidence base in ALS/PD") is **closed as stated for ALS and PD** — the evidence base exists and is substantial — and replaced by a sharper gap (GAP-ST1-2): the evidence supports barrier protection/normalisation, and the *driver* status of angiogenesis (as opposed to barrier failure) remains unproven in ALS and PD. The transfer's weakest leg is no longer "no evidence"; it is "evidence in the wrong direction for the patent's implicit reading".

**The redefinition of leg 2 is recorded as a modification, per the red-team rule.** Original leg 2 (as inherited from the patent's HBMEC assay): *protect/promote capillary networks; VEGF is the positive control.* Redefined leg 2 (Stage 1): *protect the endothelium and normalise dysregulated angiogenesis and barrier function; the admissible drug direction is protection/normalisation, and in PD and MS a pro-angiogenic drug is excluded on the evidence.* The original reading is recorded as **falsified for PD and MS** (where pathological angiogenesis is documented and VEGF blockade is protective) and **unsupported as a driver claim for ALS** (where barrier failure is established and angiogenesis-as-driver is not). A two-network successor hypothesis was not needed and is not proposed for any disease.

**What Stage 1 did not decide.** It did not decide whether any combination works — that is Stage 2. It did not decide the causal ordering of the networks within any disease (in PD, α-synuclein drives VEGF-A-mediated barrier disruption, which makes leg 2 downstream of leg 1/3 in that model; in ALS, barrier breakdown precedes motor-neuron loss, which makes leg 2 early but not necessarily causal; in MS, immune infiltration drives both angiogenesis and barrier failure). A combination can in principle work even if the networks are downstream amplifiers rather than initiators — but the claim "the disease is a failure of three networks" is stronger than "three failure processes are present", and Stage 1 supports only the weaker claim. Stage 2's minus-one-drug arm (§8) is the experiment that tests whether each leg *contributes* to protection, which is the closest in-vitro proxy for the causal question.

**Cross-disease pattern worth recording.** The three diseases converge on the same vascular *lesion* (barrier failure + dysregulated angiogenesis) through different upstream causes: SOD1-mediated tight-junction loss in ALS, α-synuclein-driven astrocytic VEGF-A in PD, immune-driven VEGF in MS. That convergence is itself a finding in favour of a shared vascular *readout* (barrier integrity, endothelial survival) across Stage 2 models, even though the upstream biology differs. It also predicts that a vascular drug that works by a single upstream mechanism (e.g. VEGF blockade) will not transfer across all three diseases, while a drug that works at the endothelial/barrier level might. Stage 2 tests the barrier-level claim, not the upstream claim.

### 7.1 The BBB verdict on the patent's six combinations

- **In vitro, all six combinations remain testable.** BBB penetration is irrelevant in HBMEC and neuronal cultures, and the patent's qualitative synergy pattern (6 of 6, §2.3) is the reason to replicate them in the dish.
- **For any systemic (animal or human) step, four of the six combinations are compromised on current evidence:** every levosimendan-containing combination (four of six) pairs a poorly penetrating drug with a second drug whose CNS exposure is poor (baclofen), unverified (sulfisoxazole, aminocaproic acid), or discordant (terbinafine). The two non-levosimendan combinations (terbinafine + aminocaproic acid) pair discordant tissue/CSF penetration with unverified exposure. **No patent synergy combination currently has a demonstrated CNS-exposure case for both members at a plausible systemic dose.** That is a Stage 1 finding about the *combinations as development candidates*, not about the network logic: the framing can survive with re-chosen drugs, and §8 re-chooses them. The re-chosen Stage 2 trios use drugs with the least-bad exposure cases (baclofen, acamprosate, cilostazol, tadalafil, zonisamide) and keep the patent pairs as in-vitro controls.
- **Aminocaproic acid deserves a separate note.** If its role is endothelial (acting on the blood side of the BBB, as the HBMEC assay suggests), brain penetration may be unnecessary for the vascular leg. That is a testable design point — an HBMEC + BBB-transwell experiment can test whether aminocaproic acid protects endothelium from the luminal side — and it is the only way the aminocaproic-acid combinations survive as more than dish results. It is stated here as a hypothesis for Stage 2's network-attribution arm, not as a fact.

## 8. Stage 2 designs for the surviving diseases

Stage 2 is designed here, not run. All three diseases survive Stage 1 (redefined), so all three get a Stage 2 design. Each design follows the same skeleton — the differences are the model, the stressor, the drug trio, and the disease-specific confounds — so that results are comparable across diseases through the shared endpoints. Common elements first, then per-disease designs.

### 8.1 Common Stage 2 skeleton (all diseases)

**Pre-registration (mandatory, before any experiment).** Concentrations, dose matrices, readouts, statistics, replicate numbers, donor lines, pass/fail thresholds, and the minimum meaningful effect (§8.2) are written and locked before the first plate. The pre-registration includes the Bliss analysis plan and the minus-one-drug comparison. Post-hoc synergy claims are the repurposing literature's most common failure mode (deep dive #3); this design does not permit them.

**Design: 2×2 factorial plus minus-one arm.** For each disease: (a) vehicle; (b) best single-network drug (the strongest single leg, chosen per disease below); (c) two-network combination; (d) three-network combination; (e) **three-network combination minus the vascular drug** (the red-team's direct falsifier for leg 2 — if (d) equals (e) on survival and priming, leg 2 contributes zero and the three-network claim fails for that disease even if the combination works); (f) BCL-XL inhibitor control arm for the BH3 assay-validity check (from deep dive #1, falsifiability F3 — the inhibitor must move priming in the opposite direction, or the BH3 readout is not measuring what the programme thinks it measures).

**Shared endpoints (all diseases).** Primary: cell survival (longitudinal live-cell imaging, LD50-style time-to-50%-decline where the model supports it, as in Bye et al. 2026) and neurite integrity (total neurite length). Shared mechanistic endpoint: **BH3-profiling EC50** — BIM peptide for overall priming, NOXA for MCL-1 dependence, BAD for BCL-2/BCL-XL/BCL-w dependence — measured longitudinally (Days 1, 7, 14, 21, 28, 42 where the model supports it; the flagship's Day 1/2/7/13–14/21/28/42 schedule is the template). This is the shared readout with the BCL-2 programme by design (cross-disease Pattern 5): the same EC50 trajectory, the same peptide panels, three neuronal types, three stressors, reported side by side. Secondary: pAkt (S473) and pBad (S136; S112 secondary) measured **before** treatment in the same cells, tested as a continuous treatment × pBad interaction (red-team strengthening of F1 — median split demoted to a sensitivity check), plus a within-line PI3K-inhibitor arm that pharmacologically lowers pAkt/pBad in the same donor lines (if lowering pAkt increases the combination's benefit within the same cells, the Akt→Bad→BCL-XL chain is confirmed directly; if not, the chain is wrong regardless of between-line stratification).

**Synergy criterion (pre-specified).** **Bliss excess with 95% confidence interval excluding 0** on the primary survival endpoint, computed from a full dose–response matrix (not single concentrations), **plus** a minimum meaningful effect: the three-network combination must exceed the best single-network drug by ≥10 percentage points of survival at the pre-specified timepoint (or, for the BH3 endpoint, an EC50 shift ≥20% relative to the best single-network arm). The minimum-effect clause is new in this document (adopted from the red-team critique of deep dive #2): a Bliss excess whose interval excludes zero but whose effect size is trivial is a publishable result and a useless treatment, and this design refuses to call it a success. The 10-point / 20% thresholds are proposed here and must be locked — or replaced with justified alternatives — at pre-registration; they must not be adjusted after seeing data.

**Network-attribution arm (mandatory).** The patent does not demonstrate which network each drug acts on. Each Stage 2 design therefore includes an arm testing whether each drug in a winning combination actually acts on its assigned network, using the patent's own selection-method logic: synaptic readout (neurite/vesicle measures), vascular readout (HBMEC capillary-network protection + BBB-transwell TEER/permeability), stress readout (UPR markers pPERK/peIF2α/XBP1 splicing, ROS, TDP-43/α-synuclein aggregation measures as disease-appropriate). If a combination works but no drug acts on its assigned network, the *combination* result stands and the *network rationale* is recorded as unproven — the two are not collapsed.

**Combination-toxicity arm (mandatory).** Viability of unstressed neurons and HBMEC across the same dose matrix; known-profile interaction check (CNS depression for baclofen, cardiac effects for levosimendan/cilostazol, hepatic metabolism for terbinafine, bleeding risk for cilostazol, hypotension for tadalafil). Repurposed drugs are not risk-free in combination, and the patent reports no combination-toxicity data.

**Model-transfer guard (GAP-ALS-13).** Any ALS result must state which iPSC model it used (Bye-style 100-donor-library/7–16-donor-screen assay vs Answer ALS 1,000-line analysis, which did not report survival deficits or pharmacological rescue) and must be run in a second model before any ranking or transfer claim is made. If the flagship's factorial reproduces in one model but not the other, the result is model-dependent and the programme must say so, not pick the favourable model.

**What Stage 2 deliberately does not include.** No animal work, no human work, no clinical-trial design. An in-vivo step would need its own design, ethics approval, and funding — none exists. No new molecule, no CRBN/BCL-2 crossover: Stage 2 tests repurposed combinations only, and a result here must never be presented as support for the BCL-2 programme (or vice versa).

### 8.2 Stage 2 — ALS

**Model and stressor.** Human iPSC motor neurons, TDP-43 Q331K (flagship model) plus at least two sporadic-ALS donor lines from the Bye-style panel; chronic glutamate stress (flagship stressor). The Bye screen's 7–16-donor screen n and the 15-donor combination-validation n are stated in every report of Bye-derived numbers (peer-review fix R2, 8 Nov 2026).

**Drug trio (working assignments — to be tested in the network-attribution arm, not asserted):** synaptic/E-I leg — **baclofen** (GABA-B agonist; PXT864 component; poor BBB noted, admissible in vitro); vascular/barrier leg — **cilostazol** (PDE3 inhibitor; barrier-strengthening in brain endothelium, cAMP/PKA; §7); stress leg — **acamprosate** (NMDA-tone restoration; crosses BBB in rats). **In-vitro positive-control pair:** terbinafine + levosimendan (patent synergy combination; BBB-irrelevant in the dish). **Comparator single-network drug (arm b):** baclofen alone at the trio's baclofen concentration — the strongest single leg on the PXT864 precedent. Rationale for re-choosing over the patent pairs: all four levosimendan pairs are BBB-compromised for any downstream step (§7.1), and cilostazol is the only candidate with direct barrier-protective data, which is what the redefined ALS leg 2 requires.

**Endpoints (in addition to §8.1).** NMJ-relevant: neurite length and branching (dying-back readout); TDP-43 cytoplasmic mislocalisation fraction; UPR markers (pPERK, peIF2α, XBP1 splicing); mitochondrial ROS. BH3 EC50 trajectory as the shared endpoint; PUMA-dependence check (PUMA knockdown should phenocopy protection if the BH3-only route is the mechanism — deep dive #1 falsification).

**Falsification (ALS-specific).** If the trio shows no Bliss excess with 95% CI excluding 0 over baclofen alone, the transfer fails for ALS. If trio-minus-cilostazol equals the trio on survival and EC50, leg 2 contributes zero and the three-network claim fails for ALS even if the trio works. If cilostazol protects HBMEC/barrier readouts but changes nothing in motor neurons, the vascular result stands as an assay result and the disease result fails — recorded separately, not collapsed.

### 8.3 Stage 2 — Parkinson's disease

**Model and stressor.** Human iPSC dopaminergic neurons; MPP+ stress (flagship PD stressor) with a rotenone arm as second stressor (the red-team's second-stressor replication requirement for any biomarker claim). α-Synuclein pre-formed-fibril arm as a disease-specific stressor in the extension phase, not the primary screen.

**Drug trio (working assignments):** synaptic leg — **zonisamide** (quantified brain uptake, 28% index; vesicle/sodium-channel modulation); vascular/normalisation leg — **tadalafil** (measured BBB crossing in mice; PDE5/cGMP vascular action; pro-angiogenic direction excluded by design — tadalafil is included as a vascular *normaliser* via cGMP/endothelial function, and its effect on barrier integrity is a pre-specified readout, not an assumption); stress leg — **acamprosate** (NMDA-tone/E-I; also the PXT864 bridge to the AD precedent). **In-vitro positive-control pair:** baclofen + levosimendan (patent synergy combination). **Comparator single-network drug:** zonisamide alone. **GBA stratification (mandatory):** donor-line GBA status recorded for every line; GBA-PD lines analysed as a separate stratum (GAP-XD-10). A PD experiment that does not stratify for GBA risks attributing a lysosomal effect to the apoptosis/stress axis or vice versa.

**Endpoints (in addition to §8.1).** Dopamine release / vesicle-cycling readout (leg-1 mechanism); α-synuclein aggregation (leg-3 proteostasis); UPR markers pPERK/peIF2α (the Hoozemans human anchor, now as an in-vitro readout); mitochondrial complex I function and ROS; BBB-transwell barrier integrity for the tadalafil arm (TEER + permeability — if tadalafil does not improve barrier measures, its vascular-leg assignment fails even if neurons survive). BIM-induction tracking: the pre-specified readout is whether BIM induction is the priming signal the combination counteracts (deep dive #1 PD extension).

**Falsification (PD-specific).** Same Bliss + minimum-effect rule. Additional directional falsifier: if the trio's vascular benefit is mediated by *increased* angiogenic signalling (VEGF up, permeability up) rather than normalisation, the result is recorded as contradicting the Stage 1 redefinition, and the PD leg-2 direction must be re-opened — a combination that works by the mechanism Stage 1 excluded is not a confirmation of Stage 1. If trio-minus-tadalafil equals the trio, leg 2 contributes zero for PD.

### 8.4 Stage 2 — multiple sclerosis

**Model and stressor.** Two-model design (mandatory, because no single MS model captures the disease): (a) MOG-induced chronic progressive EAE in C57BL/6J mice — the model in which baclofen + acamprosate is patent-reported to reduce symptoms >30% — run as a **replication** of that patent result first (same doses: baclofen 30 mg/kg/day + acamprosate 2 mg/kg/day, clinical score primary), with pre-registered success criteria, before any extension; (b) human iPSC-derived oligodendrocyte/OPC + cortical-neuron co-culture under inflammatory/oxidative stress (TNF-α + ROS) for the in-vitro combination test, so the stress leg's decisive cell (the oligodendrocyte) is measured. EAE is an animal model; the co-culture is the human-cell complement. Neither alone is sufficient for an MS claim.

**Drug trio (working assignments):** synaptic/E-I leg — **baclofen** (glutamate-toxicity protection in the patent family; GABA-B); vascular/normalisation leg — **cilostazol** (barrier strengthening; the MS vascular leg requires barrier protection, and cilostazol is the candidate with direct TEER data — with the Shahriar 2024 caveat that barrier restoration and neoangiogenesis blockade are dissociable, so both are measured separately); stress leg — **acamprosate** (UPR/oxidative-stress context; also the EAE replication drug). **Replication arm:** baclofen + acamprosate exactly as patent-reported (EAE). **Comparator single-network drug:** baclofen alone. Note the trio re-uses the replication pair plus cilostazol — this is deliberate: the MS design extends a patent-reported in-vivo result rather than starting from the dish-only combinations.

**Endpoints (in addition to §8.1).** EAE: clinical score (replication primary), spinal-cord BBB permeability (IgG/Evans-blue equivalent), lymphocyte infiltration, demyelination score. Co-culture: oligodendrocyte/OPC survival and myelination (MBP-positive wrapping) as **co-primary** with neuronal survival — a design that measures only neurons fails the MS stress leg by construction. UPR markers in oligodendrocytes (pPERK/peIF2α/XBP1); ROS; barrier TEER/permeability for the cilostazol arm; VEGF-A and neoangiogenesis markers (to test the Shahriar dissociation: does cilostazol change barrier function, neoangiogenesis, both, or neither?).

**Falsification (MS-specific).** If the EAE replication fails (no significant clinical-score improvement under pre-registered conditions), the patent-reported MS result does not reproduce, the replication is published as a negative, and the MS trio design is re-opened rather than defended. If the trio beats baclofen alone in co-culture but trio-minus-cilostazol equals the trio, leg 2 contributes zero for MS. If the trio protects neurons but not oligodendrocytes/OPCs, the MS claim fails on its decisive cell type even if the neuronal result is positive — recorded as such. **Immune-driver boundary:** a positive Stage 2 MS result is a neuroprotection/repair-adjunct result. It does not support any claim that the combination treats MS as an immune disease, and no Stage 2 MS document may imply otherwise.

### 8.5 What Stage 2 costs and needs (stated plainly)

Stage 2 needs: a wet lab or CRO with human iPSC neuronal differentiation (motor, dopaminergic, cortical, oligodendrocyte/OPC), HBMEC culture, BH3-profiling capability (flow-cytometry/cytochrome-c or JC-1 readouts in neurons — the flagship pilot validates this assay before the confirmatory phase), an EAE facility for the MS arm, a statistician for the pre-registered Bliss analysis, and compound supply (research-grade baclofen, acamprosate, cilostazol, tadalafil, zonisamide, terbinafine, levosimendan). None of these exists in the Foundation today. No cost figure is given: no budget was costed in this pass, and no figure will be invented. Sequencing: pre-registration and the BBB desk items (GAP-ST1-3) first; HBMEC replication of the six patent combinations second (cheapest, replicates the patent's own assay); neuronal factorial third; EAE replication fourth (most expensive, most regulated).

### 9.3 Falsification summary (the whole transfer, in one place)

- **Stage 1 (this document):** the narrowing trigger (no credible vascular evidence) was not met for any disease — so no disease was narrowed to two networks. The original "promote angiogenesis" reading of leg 2 was falsified for PD and MS and left unsupported as a driver claim for ALS; leg 2 was redefined, not dropped.
- **Stage 2, per disease:** no Bliss excess (95% CI excluding 0) **and** no minimum meaningful effect (§8.1) over the best single-network drug → transfer fails for that disease. Trio-minus-vascular-drug equals the trio → leg 2 contributes zero → three-network claim fails for that disease even if the combination works. BCL-XL inhibitor control fails to move priming in the opposite direction → the BH3 readout is invalid for that model and the mechanistic conclusions are suspended.
- **MS-specific:** EAE replication of baclofen + acamprosate fails → the patent-reported MS result does not reproduce; publish the negative and re-open the MS design. Trio protects neurons but not oligodendrocytes/OPCs → MS claim fails on its decisive cell type.
- **Combination-level:** a combination that works only through a mechanism Stage 1 excluded (e.g. increased angiogenic signalling in PD) is recorded as contradicting Stage 1, not confirming it.
- **Development-level:** a freedom-to-operate failure (GAP-ALZ-1) kills the development path, not the science. Keep the two separate.
